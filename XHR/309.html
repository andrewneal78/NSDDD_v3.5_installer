[210] FEATURES OF TRANSNATIONAL TERRORISM

[211] But the regional security environment of today is different and complex

[421] WHAT ARE SOLAS AND THE ISPS CODE

[422] The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) is one of the most important international conventions dealing with maritime safety

[423] It is also one of the oldest, with the first version having been adopted at a 1914 convention

[424] Since then, four other SOLAS conventions have been held

[425] SOLAS specifies standards on safety for the construction, equipping and operations of ships

[426] The ISPS (International Ship and Port Facility Security) Code contains mandatory detailed security-related requirements for governments, port authorities, port facilities and shipping companies

[427] A non-mandatory section contains a series of guidelines on how to meet these requirements

[428] The Code provides a standardized framework to evaluate the risk of terrorist acts, enabling governments and ship operators to calibrate preparedness levels and potential responses to corresponding threat levels

[429] ISPS-compliant ports put in place a wide series of security plans, with regular assessments and drills

[544] WHAT IS A DIRTY BOMB

[545] Some fears have been expressed that terrorist groups might resort to the use of “dirty bombs”

[587] CHAPTER SIX: NATIONAL SECURITY: A NATIONAL EFFORT** Defending Singapore is the Government’s most fundamental commitment to its citizens

[588] Whatever the threat, whatever the time, this task remains critical to the country’s future security and prosperity

[589] Transnational terrorism has the potential to hinder Singapore’s journey forward

[590] It can destroy lives, disrupt the economy, and damage our collective identity as a nation

[591] Our security is threatened because we have adopted an uncompromising posture on terrorism

[592] Yet, on a more fundamental level, Singapore’s very existence presents problems for some

[593] Singapore is a target for JI activity because of its place as part of the Dawlah Islamiyah Nusantara - a conception rooted in history and myth of a pan-Islamic superstate comprising much of South-east Asia

[594] As long as we remain a secular state that represents modernity and progress, we will find ourselves standing against the extremist visions of terrorist groups

[595] Singapore is particularly vulnerable for a variety of reasons

[596] We have open borders and an open society

[597] Many American and other multinational corporations are based here

[598] Millions of tourists visit Singapore each year

[599] The economy is heavily dependent on international trade carried out through extensive land, air and sea links which must not be disrupted

[600] The society is cosmopolitan, making it easy for infiltration

[601] Ultimately, the new transnational terrorism does not threaten Singapore’s very existence as an independent, sovereign nation

[602] Nonetheless, it has the capacity to inflict serious shocks on our economy and society, causing not only material and human damage, but also psychological injury

[603] It further has the potential to pit different communities against each other, weakening the multi-racial, multi-religious character of Singapore that is vital to our success

[604] The national security strategy outlined in this document maps out how Singapore can be defended

[605] It is a comprehensive solution designed to prevent terrorist incidents, protect our vulnerabilities, and help us respond effectively should attacks occur

[606] The Government has shared this with the public to provide a common understanding on the threats we face, and the steps we are taking to confront the challenges of our times

[607] It serves as a useful framework of reference for all of us

[608] WHAT IS TOTAL DEFENCE

[609] Many people think of Total Defence as a defence strategy or plan that is rolled out only during times of war

[610] But it is more than that

[611] Total Defence is about the different things we can do every day in every sector of our society to strengthen our resilience as a nation

[612] When we take National Service seriously, participate in civil emergency exercises, upgrade ourselves and learn new skills, build strong bonds with different races and religions, and feel the pride of being Singaporean, we contribute to Total Defence

[613] Introduced in 1984, the concept of Total Defence was adapted from the experiences of Switzerland and Sweden

[614] There are two reasons why we adopted it

[615] First, as a young nation with a small population, we cannot afford to maintain a regular armed force

[616] Thus, we need to involve every Singaporean to multiply our defence capability

[617] Second, the nature of modern warfare has changed

[618] Wars are no longer limited to the battlefield

[619] Instead, potential aggressors can strike in less obvious, non-military ways

[620] The five elements of Total Defence work together as a comprehensive defence strategy for Singapore

[621] Source: http://www.totaldefence.org.sg

[622] But the battle against terrorism is not that of the Government alone

[623] Now, more than ever, Singaporeans can see how the principles of Total Defence are relevant and must be applied to this fight

[624] The five components of Total Defence - Military, Civil, Economic, Social and Psychological Defence - provide the framework for a comprehensive and coherent response

[625] We need to muster our collective resilience and individual strengths to live with terrorism

[626] This has to be a united effort against a national threat
