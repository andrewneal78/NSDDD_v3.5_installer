[412] The Distant Future

[413] Although Estonian security is assured, maintaining stability requires constant attention and action

[414] The vigilance have to be sustained into the distant future since, although the security environment is more and more unpredictable and ever-changing, there are security factors whose influence will be revealed only decades from now but whose seeds are already present

[415] For this reason, there is in addition to current and highly likely security threats a need to monitor factors that may affect Estonian security in the more distant future

[416] We must raise awareness about this subject and use preventive measures if possible

[417] The three trends that could hinder or obstruct achieving the main objective of Estonian security policy are the ageing and gradual decreasing size of the Estonian population; failure to reach the goals of global sustainable development, or the low effectiveness thereof; and the impact of technological evolution on humankind and security

[418] To cope with all these trends, we must develop research-based methods that should be realised, depending on their impact, in cooperation with Estonian society or in the context of global measures

[419] Although the trends may turn out to be problematic in the distant future, they must be acknowledged and measures needed to solve certain issues should be discussed now

[420] The biggest issue that depends on Estonia itself and may turn out to be a security risk is its demographic condition

[421] The Estonian population is ageing and decreasing

[422] Global demographic trends are the opposite and, if their influence is combined with potential negative changes in the living environment, an economic recession, radicalisation and conflicts or crises, it may be that waves of migration of a magnitude never before seen will occur, and Estonia will become a potential target country for these

[424] Achieving these would reduce the root causes of conflicts and create global conditions for the survival of humankind

[425] Relevant measures concerning environmental, economic and social development should alleviate tensions arising from poverty, inequality, environmental degradation, bad governance, population growth and low employment, and help to create conditions that allow the global population (which will potentially number more than ten billion) to coexist and develop peacefully

[426] Should the global society fail in reaching these goals, security pressures will increase and tensions will become keener

[427] These may grow into a large-scale conflict or global chaos

[428] As a member of the global community and a recognised developed country, Estonia must participate in promoting sustainable development, and society has a distinct role in achieving the aforementioned goals

[429] First and foremost, this consists in admitting that Estonian society is a part of the rest of the world and that we are responsible for what is happening there

[430] If we consciously and deliberately participate in the process, the results will be better and more substantial

[431] Security risks will be neutralised more effectively if we do not ignore the process or pay little attention to it

[432] Technological evolution is speeding up and its influence on security and warfare, as well as on people’s daily life and behaviour, is evident

[433] Modern smart technologies and their applications will be replaced by next-generation intelligent technologies able to make decisions independently in addition to analysing information

[434] The application of artificial intelligence, genetics, bio- and nanotechnology and our increasing ability to harness solar and magnetic fusion energy create new possibilities that will change our living environment and existence to a remarkable extent

[435] Besides the major positive impacts, the use of new technologies will also be accompanied by threats and risks; old ones will be complemented by new ones that require novel countermeasures and solutions

[436] In the context of security and defence, this means that if a potential opponent’s technological solutions are very advanced, we need to work hard to ensure that the defence organised against them is smart, preventive and well thought through
