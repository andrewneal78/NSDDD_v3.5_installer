[110] V. CONCEPTUALIZATION OF DEFENSE Basically, DEFENSE implies action (and/or foresight) against threats to SECURITY; therefore, security is the product of the CAPACITY TO DEFEND; it is a condition that enables and contributes to the realization of a function or task

[111] When we apply these two broad concepts to the State, we speak of INTEGRAL DEFENSE and SECURITY, or NATIONAL DEFENSE and SECURITY

[112] NATIONAL DEFENSE must guarantee NATIONAL SECURITY, that is, it is worth reiterating, the action and its consequences

[113] In reality, the concept of National Defense (often) is confused with that of National Security, so much so that at the international level no consensus has been reached about the difference between both concepts, due to the different realities of different societies and the social and political dynamics of each State

[114] The importance of clarifying these concepts at the local level lies in the consequent assignment of responsibilities within the organization of the Paraguayan State

[115] If we seek effectiveness and efficiency in the field of defense and security, this assignment of functions must be as precise and functional as possible

[116] Therefore, it is necessary to clarify and delimit concepts and functions

[117] National Defense is inherent to the State

[118] In fact, in its modern conception, the State was built and developed on that basis, as a system consisting of a complex set of institutions that allow for the protection of the individual and collective rights of the social group that makes up the Nation, each institution in its own sphere, with its specific capabilities, means, and functions

[119] The system would be completely inadequate if it did not have the capacity to effectively use force when the threat involves organized violence

[120] The Armed Forces have this essential role for the State and, therefore, must always be prepared and present to act as a deterrent or, ultimately, effective force

[121] That's why all countries in the world are constantly striving to prepare their Armed Forces so that, together with other institutions, the State has an effective form of defense

[122] National Defense is applied in two different areas: In the external sphere, when other actors attempt to disregard our sovereignty

[123] These circumstances are fundamentally and basically the responsibility of diplomacy and, when they result in the need for armed defense of the Homeland, of the Armed Forces

[124] At the domestic level, the Armed Forces must combat so-called new threats, such as international terrorism, transnational organized crime, and organized armed groups that are effectively trained and equipped to undermine national sovereignty in all or part of the national territory, from within

[125] In both scenarios, all the capabilities of the State must be engaged

[126] Civil defense, that is, actions to mitigate non-military risks and threats, also falls under National Defense

[127] Likewise, the protection of strategic resources, whether natural or man-made

[128] The most immediate, heartfelt, and urgent aspect of security, as a condition for individual and collective development, is citizen security. While its main threat remains common crime, subject to police prosecution and judicial rulings, it is today even more disrupted by the effects of new threats; these, unlike crimes against public order, have much broader motivations, purposes, scope, and means

[129] Citizen Security is understood as a state of peace, tranquility, and social discipline, with full observance of public order as a guarantee of social coexistence. This contributes to internal order, which, in turn, translates into a situation that guarantees the political and legal stability of state institutions

[130] Both concepts fall under the broader concept of Internal Territorial Defense, which is a responsibility of the State's highest leadership

[131] The basic institutions of National Defense and Citizen Security are the Armed Forces and the Police, respectively

[132] The big difference is that the former apply force against the potential enemy, while the latter uses force against criminals who are usually fellow citizens

[133] This means that we face a double challenge with the new threats

[134] The Armed Forces must now also act against an internal enemy (national or foreign

[135] The police must act against individuals who break the law (national or foreign), but when organized into groups, with far greater resources and a reach that goes beyond public order and, in many cases, with a transnational component

[136] A situation of compromise then arises in both institutions

[137] First, the Armed Forces, which on the one hand must not deny themselves the means and capabilities necessary to combat transnational organized crime, must also moderate the use of lethal force against fellow citizens who make up these violent groups organized outside the law

[138] It must be made very clear that: The use of combat elements of the Nation's Armed Forces in internal defense must be temporary, not in terms of time, but rather based on the fulfillment of a mission with clear objectives to nullify or neutralize a specific threat

[139] This requirement for the proper use of combat elements of the Nation's Armed Forces within the domestic sphere in no way limits their direct or indirect social actions, or their logistical support for the Police's efforts

[140] Nor does it prevent active and permanent participation in civil defense

[141] The National Police, which is institutionally and legally limited from acting within the framework of public order, considering that it faces fellow citizens who have essentially broken the law with crimes that violate public safety, now faces the same threat, but with more resources and the benefit of technological advances available to everyone

[142] Likewise, the ease and effectiveness of communications facilitates the formation of criminal groups with potential connections abroad and the transformation into illegal armed groups that threaten State Security itself

[143] In short, both the Nation's Armed Forces and the National Police, in addition to traditional threats, now face new forms of threats

[144] Therefore, they must redouble their efforts at coordination and institutional loyalty to combat them effectively, efficiently, and with greater chances of success

[145] The problem of double compromise paradoxically brings with it a glimmer of institutional solutions to combat insecurity

[146] The task must focus on: Coordinating both institutions, seeking the same purpose, but each with its own capabilities and resources, without distorting the essence of each institution

[147] Ensure the scope of the functions of both institutions, which must comply with the law and republican principles
