[212] States will continue to be the main actors in international relations, despite delegating parts of their sovereignty in the function of cooperation and strengthening international law, to regional and global organizations

[213] The international system of the 21st century is experiencing a shift of the political and economic center of gravity towards Asia and the Pacific

[214] This trend naturally increases multipolarity and affects the gradual creation of new global power powers, with rising economic and demographic development, also in other geographical areas, which increases the opportunities for economic cooperation and the development of new markets

[215] In parallel, this trend may be accompanied by instability and potential conflicts as a result of the expansion of national power of states and their desire for a greater role on the world stage, or the increase in global competition for economic and vital resources

[216] In a multidimensional strategic context, the activity of non-state actors such as multinational companies, media groups and international advocacy groups influence the definition of economic, development and security policies

[217] The influence and role of non-state actors is expected to increase in relation to the degree of global interdependence and communication

[218] In addition to the benefits, the risks posed by non-state actors with criminal or terrorist intentions are also increasing

[219] For small states, such a context offers numerous opportunities for the realization of their interests, which are closely linked to international cooperation, deepening political-economic processes of regional and subregional integration, as instruments to increase their voice and power

[220] On the other hand, this context also carries significant risks, which can be multiplied as a result of internal weaknesses

[221] Internal environment** Political, economic and social indicators present an internal security environment of the country with stable features and a tendency towards improvement

[222] Albania's comprehensive progress over the last two decades demonstrates the increasing maturity of society and Albanians, continuous strengthening of institutions, the rule of law, as well as sustainable economic growth

[223] The latter has been led by foreign investments and the growth of domestic production and remittances

[224] Despite the impact of the crisis in recent years, the country has managed to maintain stability and a stable macroeconomic framework, despite the slowdown of the economy and the growth of public debt

[225] Stability and gradual economic growth are expected to characterize the country's development in the medium term

[226] The strengthening of the rule of law and good governance over the past two decades have not followed a uniform course of improvement

[227] Corruption and organized crime, as well as other factors addressed in this document, have undermined and continue to undermine the country's development

[228] Corruption remains a serious threat to the performance of state institutions

[229] The phenomenon of corruption appears most problematic in the justice system and in public administration

[230] Corruption is fueled by the failure to respect recruitment criteria and the low level of punishment of this phenomenon by the justice system and administration

[231] Organized crime in the forms of drug trafficking, firearms, ammunition and people remains a high-risk factor

[232] It has gained economic power and uses various pressures to avoid confrontation with law enforcement agencies

[233] The existence of organized crime in Albania is facilitated by unstable socio-economic conditions and institutional shortcomings of law enforcement structures

[234] Organized crime undermines the integrity of democratic public processes, the stability of the financial system and hinders the economic and social development of the country, as well as the credibility of governance

[235] The country's administration and institutions inherit problems of politicization, lack of professionalism and meritocracy, and have limited financial, logistical, operational or infrastructural capacities

[236] This causes direct negative consequences in achieving objectives and fulfilling their mission

[237] The structural organization of the State Police, its current capacities, the presence of a large number of police-type structures at national and local levels, the high number of private security structures, as well as deficiencies in coordination between law enforcement agencies do not respond to the needs of control and administration of the territory

[238] The current capacities of the national energy system do not guarantee sustainable energy security for the country

[239] The high costs that Albania is forced to bear, by turning to the international market to secure energy, hinder the necessary investments in improving the Albanian electricity network

[240] The electricity system has a considerable internal debt, further aggravating the fragility of energy security

[241] The problems of the energy system negatively affect the interest of foreign investors to invest in Albania, especially in the fields of industry and tourism

[242] Critical elements of the infrastructure, of an energy, IT, telecommunications, road or airport nature, remain vulnerable

[243] Protecting the current inventory of this infrastructure and preparing to protect new infrastructure from acts of vandalism, sabotage, terrorism and human negligence constitutes a necessary investment for the security of the country

[244] Our country is also exposed to risks of natural origin of a geological, hydrological, atmospheric and biophysical nature, as well as those of human, technological and industrial origin

[245] Globally, Albania is ranked among the countries with high economic consequences from disasters

[246] About 86% of the territory, where approximately 88.5% of GDP is generated, is exposed to two or more risks

[247] The civil protection system is insufficient to manage the increasing number of emergencies and the rapid rehabilitation of the situation caused by them

[248] Climate change is a global phenomenon, which negatively affects the natural balances of the Republic of Albania

[249] Due to the climate, the country may face more frequent extreme phenomena such as high temperatures, prolonged droughts and major floods

[250] This may expose the country to the risk of hydrological, geological and biophysical disasters, which affect inhabited areas and significantly affect the national economy

[251] Environmental degradation in the Republic of Albania is a very worrying phenomenon

[252] It manifests itself in various forms such as the chaotic expansion of urban centers, massive deforestation, damage to agricultural lands, high pollution of water basins and erosion of rivers

[253] These phenomena have produced significant consequences on the territory, the urban landscape, health and the country's economy

[254] Albania is affected and will continue to be at risk in the future from erosion

[255] The most problematic areas are the western lowlands, the valleys of large rivers and the Adriatic coast, where the sea baseline is retreating

[256] Inland, erosion is predicted to cause a reduction in arable land and water resources

[257] Pandemic diseases pose a real and growing threat to national security

[258] In the last 20 years, the Republic of Albania has faced diseases such as cholera and polio, with several types of influenza viruses such as H5N1 and H7N9, as well as the HIV virus

[259] The potential human losses could be significant, but the greatest impact would be caused by consumer backlash, labor shortages, and cascading damage to the economic sector

[260] Albania is facing the phenomenon of mass emigration

[261] The outflow of human potential has negatively affected social, economic, cultural, and scientific development, as well as the slowdown in domestic production growth rates

[262] A significant portion of the country's active labor force contributes to the economies of other countries

[263] With the increase in prosperity and the country's rapprochement with the EU, Albania could become a destination for illegal immigration, mainly originating from countries in the Middle East, North Africa, and Asia

[264] This phenomenon is favored by the country's rather convenient geographical position

[265] Agent activities in Albania could threaten the security of classified information and attempt to influence political decision-making, critical infrastructure, the media, the economy and administration, social cohesion, and coexistence

[266] The country's status as a NATO member increases the interest of foreign intelligence services in Albania

[267] The progress of the Republic of Albania over the past decade, the reconfiguration of the map of the region, the expansion of NATO and the EU and the normalization of relations between Kosovo and Serbia have brought about fundamental positive changes in the region

[268] Relations between the countries of the region have improved and cooperation has experienced qualitative and quantitative growth

[269] Integration processes are the catalysts of democratic reforms, cooperation, good neighborliness and the reconciliation process

[270] The region is now able to produce internal stability and contribute to security and peace even in geographical areas beyond it

[271] In bilateral cooperation, the trend towards removing barriers through the construction of joint infrastructures for the free movement of people, ideas, goods and capital prevails, transforming the region into an attractive and competitive market for foreign investments, as well as promoting interaction between different cultures

[272] Despite the positive trend, the security of the region may be undermined by a range of risks

[273] Extreme nationalism and the existence of divided societies still constitute a potential that could favor centrifugal internal forces, risking the reopening of the border issue; * Political use and non-fulfillment of the rights of certain ethnic groups or minorities may still cause sporadic tensions and generate negative consequences in bilateral and multilateral relations; * Unresolved interstate disputes and problems may affect the quality of regional cooperation and good neighborly relations, slowing down integration processes; * Regional organized crime, corruption and oligarchic interests endanger the stability of the region

[274] The global security environment is changing rapidly and continuously

[275] These features make it difficult to predict and diversify the range of prevailing risks

[276] Over the next decade, it is predicted that the risks will be mainly asymmetric and hybrid in nature, generated by state, non-state or failed states

[277] The geographical position places Albania on the map of the transit of narcotics originating from Asia and close to the risks stemming from instability in the Middle East and North Africa

[278] This includes the transfer of internal political or religious conflicts, various trafficking and illegal immigration

[279] Albania is ranked among the countries where the development of telecommunications, access to the Internet and the informatization of society is progressing very quickly

[280] The increase in communication constitutes an added value in the economic and social development of the country, but, at the same time, it exposes it to risks of a cyber nature with state and non-state actors

[281] Cyber attacks have the potential to severely damage the exchange of information in public institutions, telecommunications and the financial and banking system, causing disruptions to vital services

[282] International terrorism dominates the spectrum of asymmetric threats and is expected to pose a direct threat to the security of the region and the Republic of Albania

[283] Albania is exposed to the potential risk of a terrorist attack as a result of its NATO membership and contribution to global peace and security

[284] Extremism with ideological, religious or nationalist inspiration fuels terrorist activities

[285] Extreme ideologies rely on ignorance, poverty, lack of state services and multiple regional and international connections

[286] These causes create a ground for the recruitment of vulnerable individuals, who participate in international conflicts

[287] Due to its expansionist tendency, extremism can pose a threat to the cohesion and values of Albanian society and the region

[288] Weapons of mass destruction, their means of proliferation and long-range weapons pose a permanent threat to global security and, thus, to Albania

[289] The number of states and non-state actors that intend to possess such weapons continues to grow

[290] The possibility of Albanian territory being hit by weapons of mass destruction is assessed as minimal

[291] Although greatly reduced, the conventional risk to Albania cannot be ruled out

[292] A military attack on one of NATO's allies is considered the most typical case in which Albania could be involved in a conventional conflict

[293] Developments on the Alliance's borders, the misuse of national minorities for political purposes and the change of borders in violation of international law testify to the existence of this risk

[294] Due to the obligations arising from Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty, the Republic of Albania may be involved in a conventional conflict in cases where the independence, sovereignty and territorial integrity of any of its allies are threatened

[295] Annex C – RISKS CLASSIFICATION** The classification of risks is made by examining two main parameters: the likelihood of the risk occurring and the consequences that they may cause for national security

[296] Based on the combination of these two parameters, the risks are classified into three levels

[297] The first level groups risks that have a high probability of occurrence and high consequences if they materialize

[298] The second level groups risks that combine low probability and high consequences

[299] The third level groups risks that have a low probability of occurrence and more limited consequences

[300] These conclusions were also reached by analyzing the structural and institutional weaknesses that most affect our ability to cope with these risks

[301] First level risks

[302] The following categories of risks have the highest priority for the security of the Republic of Albania

[303] The likelihood of occurrence and their consequences for national security are assessed as high

[304] Corruption and organized crime; * Energy crisis, which threatens the security of supply of the Republic of Albania and undermines economic growth; * Deficiencies in the control and administration of the territory, which threaten individual security and favor various trafficking; * Cyberattacks by state or non-state actors; * Environmental degradation and natural disasters; environmental destruction, deforestation, river pollution and industrial or human-induced natural disasters

[305] The following categories of risks constitute a second-level priority for national security

[306] The likelihood of their occurrence is assessed as low but with high consequences for security

[307] Terrorist attack, including the use of weapons of mass destruction, against the territory of the Republic of Albania; * Emergence of a pandemic that threatens the public health of the population on a large scale; * Increase in religious radicalism, which endangers the values of religious harmony in Albania and which may cause social tension; * Activation of a regional hotbed of tension with consequences for the national security of the Republic of Albania; * Conventional attack on the Republic of Albania or a NATO member state

[308] The following categories of risks constitute a third-level priority for the country's security

[309] The probability of occurrence is assessed as lower than the above level, while the consequences for national security are lower than the above level

[310] Subversive espionage activities against the interests of the Republic of Albania; * Global or regional financial crisis, which endangers the stability of the economy of the Republic of Albania

[311] Consequences of climate change on the natural balances of the Republic of Albania; * Increase in foreign immigration to the territory of Albania
