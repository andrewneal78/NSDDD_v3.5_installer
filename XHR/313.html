[479] Maneuver is movement of forces or use of our capabilities to maintain our relative advantages usually in the use of firepower

[480] Effective maneuver warfare enables to keep an enemy in unbalanced position and also to defend our own forces and capabilities

[481] Application of maneuver warfare means degradation of the integrity of the enemy and his will to fight

[482] As distinct from the coercion approach, the maneuverist approach is characterized by the use of force against the weak points of the enemy by combining the maneuver with the pace of operations in the whole conflict, with the use of surprise

[483] The key is to set such a pace of operations as to produce surprise and to increase its effects by parallel attacks against several weak points of the enemy, presenting him with a dilemma

[484] Maneuver warfare means the application of maneuver-like thinking in the conduct of military operations and, at the same time, a military way of thinking

[485] Maneuver warfare requires dealing with new requirements on the way of thinking by commanders and staffs, such as initiative, originality, courage to adopt new solutions and ability to accept mistakes by subordinates

[486] The application of this approach enables to concentrate all efforts on the set objective, which is to defeat the enemy

[487] Unlike the coercive strategy, maneuver warfare tries to avoid places with strong resistance and it concentrates on recommended areas with the least resistance, using reconnaissance

[488] The commander’s mission is to take every opportunity to carry out a successful operation and therefore, maneuver warfare supports and requires decentralization of command to the lowest level possible

[489] Maneuverist approach is applied at all levels of war

[490] In comparison with other strategies, maneuver warfare produces better results, with efficient use of efforts and resources

[491] It is the best strategy in a fight against an enemy superior in number and equipment

[492] Purposes of manoeuvrist approach at the operational level Purposes of maneuverist approach at the operational level − to form theater of operation − attack on enemy's integrity − to defend own force's integrity − to exploit particular situations 33 the use of indirect methods of strike

[493] Fast decision-making cycle within the own forces and disruption of the enemy’s decision-making cycle are of particular importance for the successful application of maneuver warfare

[494] Faster pace in operations in maneuver warfare is achieved by entering the enemy’s decision-making cycle (observation-orientation-decision-action cycle) in such a manner that our own cycle is always one step ahead of the enemy’s cycle, paralyzing the enemy and his ability to respond

[495] These measures disrupt the integrity and the will of the enemy to carry on operations and war

[496] In principle, war is a process of risk control and war victory is impossible without taking a risk

[497] Maneuverist approach responds to this challenge of war

[498] Maneuver warfare requires the use of planning and enemy assessment tools, including the assessment of centers of gravity, decisive points and lines of operations, which are, among other things, described in more detail in doctrine SVD- The central point for the understanding maneuver warfare is to understand the concept of centers of gravity

[499] Centers of gravity are those capabilities or elements from which a state, coalition, interest group or military force derives its freedom of action, physical strength, or will to fight

[500] When evaluating centers of gravity, strengths, weaknesses and critical vulnerabilities of both the enemy and own forces have to be considered

[501] Centers of gravity exist at all levels of war and, in fact, there could be more centers of gravity at each level

[502] The most vulnerable center of gravity is not always the one, which has priority from the viewpoint of target acquisition

[503] Defense of own centers of gravity prevents defeat by enemy

[504] Elimination of a properly defined enemy's center of gravity significantly weakens him and, eventually, causes his defeat

[505] The art of warfare requires concentration of efforts on critical weaknesses of the main source of the enemy’s fighting power

[506] Centers of gravity are often immaterial; it could be for example the support of the public

[507] Identification of centers of gravity is one of the most important components of the situation estimate at each echelon

[508] Basic military capabilities of the Armed Forces

[510] Application of the mission command method

[511] One of the deciding aspects of maneuverist approach to engagement of the Armed Forces is a style of command called “mission command”

[512] Mission command is based on decentralized command concept that is characterized by assignment of objectiveoriented combat missions by the superior commander to a particular subordinate commander

[513] As distinct from authoritative command, the commander states "what" needs to be achieved, he does not order, however, "how" to achieve it

[514] This gives the subordinate commanders the opportunity to use their own initiative within the defined area, time and with the allocated resources, it provides freedom of action and enables to streamline the decision making process

[515] Deciding factors of successful application of mission command are as follows: • the superior commander has to ensure that the subordinate commander understands the objectives and intentions of the superior commander, his own combat tasks and the strategic, operational and tactical context of the operation; • the subordinate commander is told what effect they are to achieve and the reason why it is necessary; • the subordinate commander is allocated sufficient resources to accomplish his combat task; • the superior commander will use authoritative measures to the least extent necessary, giving the subordinate commander freedom of action; and • the decision on the best manner of how to accomplish the combat mission will be in the hands of the subordinate commander

[703] The military authority and responsibility of a commander to issue orders to subordinates

[706] Operational command

[710] The authority delegated to a commander to assign tasks to forces under his command for the accomplishment of the mission assigned by higher authority

[714] The authority delegated to a commander to direct forces assigned so that the commander may accomplish specific missions or tasks which are usually limited by function, time, or location; to deploy units concerned, and to retain or assign tactical control of those units

[715] It does not include authority to assign separate employment of components of the units concerned

[716] Neither does it, of itself, include administrative or logistic control

[717] Tactical control
