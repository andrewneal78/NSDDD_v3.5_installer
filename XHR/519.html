[3] Saeima announcement On the approval of the National Security Concept I hereby announce that the Saeima has approved the National Security Concept at its sitting on 26 November this year

[4] Speaker of the Saeima I. Mūrniece Riga, 26 November 2015 National Security Concept (informative part

[5] Introduction The National Security Concept (hereinafter - the Concept) is a document prepared on the basis of a national threat analysis, which sets out the strategic basic principles and priorities for preventing national threats, which must be taken into account when developing new policy planning documents, legal acts and action plans in the field of national security

[6] The analysis of national threats and this Concept examines threats that directly and indirectly affect Latvia's national security and are related to changes in the international security environment, military activities near Latvia's borders, trends in international terrorism, cybersecurity, as well as efforts by certain foreign countries to influence the unity of Latvian society, the country's foreign policy orientation and domestic political stability through political, humanitarian, informational and economic means

[7] The Concept sets out the main priorities for preventing these threats in accordance with each area of threat

[8] The policy developed by state administration institutions, planned and implemented measures, as well as mutual cooperation in the field of national security must be based on the priorities set out in the Concept

[9] The Cabinet of Ministers, based on the priorities set out in the Concept, develops a National Security Plan, which includes specific measures and means for neutralising and preventing threats to the state

[10] National security and national security policy National security is a set of conditions achieved as a result of targeted measures implemented by the state, which ensure that Latvia's internal and external vulnerability is reduced, as well as the state's overall capabilities and general readiness in identifying, preventing and overcoming threats to national security are improved

[11] Latvia's national security has a foreign policy and domestic policy dimension, which are interrelated

[12] The foreign policy dimension is characterized by the current international security environment, changes in it and external threats

[13] In turn, the domestic policy dimension is characterized by the state's provision of the fundamental values set out in the Constitution of the Republic of Latvia, as well as a strong civil society that has a common understanding of issues regarding its value orientation, Latvia's belonging to the Western world, which has a desire to see Latvia as an independent, democratic and legal state also in the future, and which is ready to defend the state in critical moments

[14] The security of the state border, the capacity of state security institutions, law enforcement institutions, as well as the State Border Guard and the ability to respond in the event of a threat are extremely important aspects that relate to both the foreign policy and domestic dimensions of Latvia's national security

[15] The basis of Latvia's national security policy must be a common understanding among decision-makers and 1/13 of society that Latvia's domestic political stability and security are very closely related to the international security situation

[16] The stronger Latvia is domestically, the more effectively it will be able to respond and reduce vulnerability to external threat factors

[17] Latvia's membership in the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (hereinafter - NATO) and the European Union (hereinafter - EU) is an important fundamental element of Latvia's national security

[18] The decisions taken at the 2014 NATO Wales Summit on measures to strengthen NATO's collective defense and promote the defense and response capabilities of the alliance's member states help strengthen this fundamental element

[19] At the same time, it should be noted that Latvia's membership in NATO does not address all issues related to national security and does not provide protection against several significant threats arising from Latvia's domestic political vulnerabilities

[20] The EU's efforts in the field of security and defence also play a complementary role to NATO's collective defence

[21] With the increase in the so-called hybrid threat, increasing importance must be attached to strengthening areas that are outside the competence of the Common Security and Defence Policy, such as border security, information and cyberspace security, energy security, etc

[22] Latvia also needs to promote the effective involvement of other international organisations such as the United Nations, the Organisation for Security and Co-operation in Europe and the Council of Europe in regulating the international security situation, seeking solutions to the conflict in Ukraine and promoting its stability, territorial integrity, strengthening of democracy and European values

[23] National security policy is a set of measures aimed at identifying, preventing and overcoming threats to national security

[24] The spectrum of threats to Latvia's national security exceeds the competence of the institutions of the defense and internal affairs system

[25] This means that the entire state administration must be involved in identifying, preventing and overcoming threats to national security on the state side

[26] Taking into account the large number of institutions involved, centralized management of national security policy and mutual coordination of these institutions are important

[27] Equally important is a common understanding among state administration institutions of what national security is, what the most current threats to national security of Latvia are and how to counter them more effectively

[28] Inter-institutional cooperation mechanisms must operate effectively on a daily basis, but each institution must take the initiative in resolving national security issues within its area of responsibility

[29] Public understanding and support are important for state administration institutions in implementing national security policy, which can only be provided by a society educated in these issues

[30] Consequently, educating the public in the field of national security is one of the priorities in implementing national security policy

[31] Changes in the international security environment and its impact on national security Since 2011, when the previous Concept was approved by the Saeima, the international security environment has undergone significant changes, characterized by variability and unpredictability, as well as the emergence of new conflicts, including relatively close to the borders of Latvia

[32] Russia's aggression in Ukraine has created significant challenges to European security and the global international order

[33] After the end of World War II, Russia's aggression in Ukraine has been an unprecedented attack on the fundamental principles of international law, which determine, for example, the implementation of concluded agreements, the inviolability of borders and the territorial integrity of states

[34] These principles determine the relations between states and have been the basis for peaceful coexistence in Europe in the post-war period

[35] Latvia, together with its allies in the EU and NATO, has expressed and continues to express clear condemnation that Russia's actions in Ukraine are unacceptable and threaten European security

[36] Russia's actions in Ukraine have significantly worsened security in the Euro-Atlantic area, and they also have a long-term impact on Latvia's national security

[37] Russia's policy and aggression in Ukraine are characterized by certain trends that should be taken into account when planning the identification, prevention and overcoming of threats to Latvia's national security

[38] First, the Russian operation in Crimea demonstrated the current capabilities and readiness of this country, confirming that Russia has developed high-readiness armed forces units with high mobility, which significantly reduces the early warning time

[39] Second, Russia implements its foreign policy goals by applying complex measures of influence, which in general create the so-called hybrid threat

[40] These measures have various manifestations, covering several areas at the same time

[41] Their main essence is the gradual weakening of the state domestically, contributing to the growth of dissatisfaction and protest potential in society in order to provoke an open turn against the existing state power and violent unrest

[42] In addition, economic sanctions, interruption of energy supplies, humanitarian influence, information propaganda and psychological influence, attacks in the electronic information space, activities of aggressive influence agents, intelligence and security services, massive external diplomatic and military pressure, imposition of an alternative political process and its legitimization are used

[43] Among these means, there is a possibility of deploying traditional warfare against the opponent

[44] The manifestations of influence measures and the subjects who plan and implement them are often difficult to identify, thus making it difficult to counteract them

[45] Thirdly, it is advantageous for Russia to create conflict zones on its immediate border, in which it is difficult to see the transition from a state of peace to a crisis and further to a state of war

[46] Fourthly, it is advantageous for Russia to artificially create the perception of NATO as a source of external threats due to domestic political considerations

[47] In this way, the Russian state power is trying to unite the society of this country and achieve its loyalty to the existing power elite, while diverting the attention of the population from internal instability and socio-economic problems

[48] In turn, in the context of Latvia's national security, this poses a threat, since Latvia is a NATO member state, where military personnel from other NATO member states are stationed to ensure the security of our country

[49] Russia will not only continue its negative rhetoric against the presence of military personnel from NATO countries in Latvia, trying to turn Latvian society against it, but will also try to provoke, for example, by conducting its military aviation flights in the immediate vicinity of Latvian airspace, as well as by organizing military exercises near the borders of Latvia

[50] Russia is also implementing influence measures against Latvia, therefore, certain elements of the so-called hybrid threat can be identified in Latvia, which requires complex and long-term action to mitigate and prevent this threat

[51] growing confrontation with the West, aggressive demonstrations of military force and the increase of military potential in the West's strategic direction create tension and uncertainty

[52] In turn, information propaganda disorients decision-makers and the public

[53] These risk factors require increasing attention to strengthening national security measures in Latvia and the Baltic region

[54] Significant sources of threats affecting Europe as a whole are related to instability in the Middle East region

[55] These sources of threats are related to the destabilization of individual countries in this region, the increase in the number of refugees and asylum seekers, the possible proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, the radicalization of societies in these countries, and the increase in the threat of terrorism

[56] Threats to national security caused by international terrorism and its consequences have increased

[57] In recent years, terrorism has developed into a particularly complex and multifaceted problem, which has become a challenge not only to the security and stability of countries, but also goes against generally recognized fundamental values

[58] The ISIL/Da'esh terrorist organization is considered a special problem, which with its methods of operation has also become a driving force for other terrorist organizations, marking new trends in the so-called global jihad

[59] The so-called foreign fighters phenomenon has become a dangerous challenge in this context, when individuals travel to conflict-affected areas for financial or ideological reasons to fight there

[60] The radicalization of these individuals and the threat they pose upon their return to their countries of origin, including Europe, is a long-term threat, the extent of which is still not fully understood

[61] Therefore, coordinated action is needed at both the national and international levels to prevent the further spread of this phenomenon

[62] International economic processes significantly affect the international security environment, especially taking into account increasing competition between countries, economic integration processes at the regional and international levels, as well as interdependence in the context of import, export and production process management

[63] The international financial crisis and the subsequent economic problems in the EU and elsewhere in the world clearly demonstrate the connection between economic issues and other security elements

[64] Priorities for preventing national security threats

[65] Prevention of threats to internal security One of the defining elements of internal security, in addition to a cohesive civil society, a stable economic environment and strong public media, is how safe and stable citizens feel in their country on a daily basis, how they assess and trust the capabilities of state security institutions (the Constitutional Protection Bureau, the Security Police and the Military Intelligence and Security Service) and law enforcement institutions to prevent and protect them against various types of threats

[66] State security institutions and law enforcement institutions, as well as the State Border Guard, are directly responsible for protecting certain fundamental values set out in the Constitution of the Republic of Latvia

[67] These include state independence, the democratic system of the state, territorial integrity and internal security of the state

[68] In the context of internal security, the professional understanding of all these institutions about changes in the international security environment and their impact on national security as a whole is important, which means an adequate response to the current situation and the appropriate development of these institutions in the near future

[69] Increasing the human resource capacity of law enforcement institutions, especially the State Police, as well as the State Border Guard, modernizing existing material and technical equipment and purchasing new ones, as well as strengthening response capabilities are essential to promote internal security of the country

[70] The government has set internal and external security as one of the priorities in the formation of the medium-term budget in 2015, which also means government support for increasing the remuneration of officials with special service ranks, preventing the outflow of professional personnel and ensuring an increase in capacity in ensuring internal security of the country

[71] When protecting the Eastern border of Latvia, the personnel of the State Border Guard would be the first to encounter specific manifestations of external threats

[72] In turn, the State Police must be ready to respond immediately in situations where there is an open challenge to the existing state power in the form of protests and violent riots

[73] Ensuring and maintaining such readiness requires an appropriate approach and specific training, which must be purposefully developed

[74] In the field of internal security, the involvement of Latvian residents, especially young people, in various activities that contradict the national interests of the Latvian state is a threat

[75] In order to limit such activities, it is important to put the regulatory framework in order

[76] In 2015, the Saeima has already adopted amendments to the Criminal Law, which provide for holding a person criminally liable for unlawful participation in an armed conflict, for financing an armed conflict, as well as for recruiting, training and sending to an armed conflict

[77] It is important for state administrative institutions to continue developing the regulatory framework in order to limit the opportunities for children and young people to participate in military camps abroad

[78] Taking into account the rapid increase in the number of refugees and asylum seekers in Europe in general, as well as the commitment of the Latvian government to accept a certain number of refugees in Latvia, it is expected that this issue will create new risks and challenges for internal security in the near future

[79] The complex geopolitical situation, the presence of refugees in Latvia and the increase in their number may create tension and insecurity in society in the future

[80] The refugee issue will contribute to the radicalization of a certain part of society, which will be used in their own interests by certain, including radical, organizations that will try to combine their resources and concentrate their activities, protesting against the reception of refugees and their presence in Latvia

[81] Latvia's priorities for preventing threats to internal security: Strengthening the security and protection of the state border Strengthening the security and protection of the state border must be carried out in connection with possible external threats, since the Eastern border of Latvia is simultaneously the external border of the EU and NATO, as well as in the context of the intensification of immigration threats

[82] To ensure the security of the state border, it is necessary to strengthen the human resource capacity and material and technical equipment of the State Border Guard, paying special attention to personnel training, as well as to the arrangement and equipping of the green border with the necessary technical devices

[83] When strengthening the capacity of the State Border Guard, it is necessary to address the issue of the compatibility of its material and technical equipment, training process and response procedures with the National Armed Forces (hereinafter - NAF

[84] The process of demarcation of the state border must be continued by establishing a 12-meter border strip, carrying out the necessary clearance works and land acquisition for this purpose, as well as planning the technical equipment of this strip, attracting both EU funds and the support of strategic partners

[85] At the same time, measures related to the management of the integrated state border must be continued

[86] Capacity of law enforcement institutions The capacity and material and technical support of law enforcement institutions, especially the State Police, must be continued to be strengthened by carrying out an effective training process, thus ensuring a rapid response in crisis situations and in the event of a threat to the state

[87] The cooperation of the State Police with the public must be continued to be developed, especially ensuring that State Police officials are available to residents throughout the territory of the state in the event of a threat to the state

[88] Crisis management and communication The civil protection system and civil-military cooperation must be strengthened and developed, ensuring coordinated inter-institutional activities and the use of resources in the event of a national threat

[89] The responsible state administration institutions, in cooperation with the state security institutions, must continue regular crisis management exercises for the highest state officials, preparing for various situations of preventing and overcoming national security threats

[90] In order to prevent national security threats, it is essential for the responsible state administration institutions to ensure the implementation of the measures specified in the National Economy Mobilization Plan

[91] Improving the refugee and asylum seeker policy The state must prepare in a timely manner for a possible increase in the number of refugees and asylum seekers, including from countries whose refugees have not chosen Latvia as their target country so far

[92] The responsible institutions must improve the refugee and asylum seeker policy and existing procedures in order to ensure readiness to accept refugees

[93] The responsible institutions must develop the necessary procedures for profiling refugees and asylum seekers in order to prevent the entry and residence of undesirable persons, especially from countries at risk of terrorism, in Latvia

[94] In addition, active exchange of information with other European countries and their responsible institutions on refugee issues must be ensured

[95] Mitigating the risks of radicalization The capacity of law enforcement institutions must be strengthened to proactively identify the risks of radicalization

[96] They must, in cooperation with non-governmental organizations, establish an early warning and radicalization prevention mechanism, assigning one of the main roles to the public

[97] An important role in mitigating radicalization must be assigned to public education, promoting understanding that extremism is a significant threat to civil society and its unity, as well as to national security in general

[98] Prevention of threats posed by foreign intelligence and security services 4/13 The activities of foreign intelligence and security services (hereinafter referred to as foreign special services) against the interests of Latvia, NATO and the EU are one of the most significant threats to national security, affecting the political, economic, military and public spheres

[99] Taking into account Latvia's membership in NATO and the EU, as well as its geographical location, the greatest threat to Latvia is posed by Russian special services

[100] The aim of foreign special services is to obtain publicly unavailable and anticipatory information that would give the country represented by the service advantages in making political, economic and military decisions

[101] In addition to collecting information, foreign special services implement so-called active measures aimed at influencing the decision-making of Latvia, NATO and the EU, as well as public opinion

[102] To achieve intelligence goals, foreign special services use various methods: obtaining information from contact persons; information sources; technical and signals intelligence, including cyber intelligence; obtaining information from open sources

[103] To implement influence measures, foreign special services are trying to create a manageable network of agents in the media, state and local government institutions, non-governmental organizations, political and business environments

[104] Increasing attention is being paid to virtual space and social networks, in which foreign special services can operate remotely

[105] Intelligence and influence capabilities are being developed in the long term with the aim of activating them as needed within the framework of specific projects or when a certain situation arises

[106] Latvia continues to issue temporary residence permits to foreign citizens in exchange for investments in our country

[107] The implementation of the temporary residence permit program currently does not have a direct impact on national security, but it is important to identify and prevent certain risks in a timely manner, which are created by the possibility of undesirable persons entering Latvia and the Schengen zone under the cover of this program

[108] The strategic goal of state security institutions is to ensure a set of preemptive and preventive measures to neutralize the threat posed by foreign special services to the interests of Latvia, NATO and the EU

[109] Priority tasks should be based on strengthening and improving the activities of existing state security institutions, in accordance with the existing types of threats

[110] Latvia's priorities for preventing threats posed by foreign intelligence and security services: Development of the capabilities of state security institutions State security institutions must be able to adapt to new types of threats from foreign special services, as well as find new solutions to prevent existing threats

[111] Strengthening capacity and developing capabilities is a priority long-term task of Latvia's state security institutions

[112] Attention should be paid to personnel selection and regular training, the introduction of new technologies and solutions, as well as improving methods and increasing efficiency in daily operational and analytical work

[113] Strengthening counterintelligence and preventive measures The task of state security institutions is to carry out continuous counterintelligence measures, identifying employees of foreign special services, their collaborators, circle of interests and methods of operation

[114] By increasing the effectiveness of the counterintelligence function performed by state security institutions, it is necessary to improve the ability to predict potential weak points and anticipate the goals of foreign special services in order to ensure targeted countermeasures and prevention of threats

[115] State security institutions must ensure the implementation of a comprehensive preventive training and warning program in state administration institutions that work with sensitive, classified or strategic information

[116] The basis of an effective counterintelligence system is systematic training and notification procedures for officials whose direct work duties are related to classified information

[117] The Security Police must continue cooperation and information exchange with local governments in order to inform them about current risks to national security and advise local government officials on specific actions to mitigate these risks, as well as receive information from local governments about suspicious activities on their territory that pose a potential threat

[118] Educational work must also be continued with the public, including representatives of the media and the non-governmental sector, strengthening understanding of various types of threats, especially in the information space

[119] Improvement of state secret protection measures State security institutions must ensure compliance with existing state secret protection measures and, if necessary, improvement

[120] These measures include conducting security checks on persons who apply for a special permit to work with state secrets, as well as monitoring the daily work process with state secret objects

[121] State security institutions should continue regular training of state officials on compliance with the secrecy regime

[122] In accordance with the development of threats and new intelligence capabilities, special attention should be paid to the security of classified and state-important communications infrastructure

[123] The physical objects (premises) of state institutions should also be systematically checked and meet the necessary security requirements

[124] State security institutions should intensify control over the compliance of the procedures for the development, storage, circulation and use of 5/13 state, EU and NATO secret objects with the requirements set out in regulatory enactments

[125] Representatives of critical infrastructure and strategic sectors, especially those who have been issued industrial security certificates, should be provided with appropriate training, inspection and security auditing programs in critical infrastructure objects, as well as in specific enterprises

[126] Effective control of the temporary residence permit program The Security Police and other responsible institutions must continue systematic and targeted work to prevent the possibility that the temporary residence permit program is used to implement activities that pose a threat to national security

[127] At the same time, it is necessary to control the activities of persons who have obtained a temporary residence permit in Latvia in order to determine whether these activities correspond to the declared purpose for which the temporary residence permit was requested

[128] To study the operation of the temporary residence permit program, it is also necessary to conduct a long-term analysis in order to objectively assess both the potential economic benefits for the state and to preventively warn about possible risks in the future

[129] Strengthening cooperation with partner services Mutual cooperation in Latvia, as well as with partner services of Latvia's allied countries, is an indispensable tool for identifying and preventing threats from foreign special services

[130] Regular and effective priority setting, coordination of activities and exchange of information must be ensured within the framework of the Council of State Security Institutions

[131] Within the framework of NATO, the EU and other existing, as well as new formats, cooperation with foreign partner services should be further developed, ensuring regular exchange of information and experience

[132] State security institutions should be active, providing Latvia's contribution to the preventive prevention of threats to NATO and EU allies

[133] Prevention of military threats The importance of external risks and threats to Latvia's national security has increased significantly

[134] The existing security environment is a complex, mutual interaction of several military and non-military aspects

[135] Russia's aggression in Ukraine, its military exercises and demonstration of force at the borders of Latvia, as well as efforts to influence the domestic political situation by various economic and political means, create the need for a comprehensive approach to preventing threats

[136] The strategic basic principle of ensuring Latvia's military security remains unchanged - by continuing to improve and modernize national defense capabilities, supporting the strengthening of NATO collective defense and contributing to ensuring international security, Latvia reduces the likelihood of external military aggression or other threats and, if necessary, is ready to ensure their effective deterrence

[137] Latvia's priorities for preventing military threats: Strengthening the state's self-defense capabilities State self-defense is the responsibility of society, state institutions, local governments and the National Armed Forces, engaging in ensuring the sovereignty and inviolability of the state

[138] Critical self-defense capabilities are the earliest possible identification of threats, rapid decision-making and comprehensive government action, using all possible military and non-military means in a coordinated and integrated manner

[139] The use of military and non-military means is adjusted depending on the threat and the escalation of the level of violence in the territory of Latvia, as well as observing the principles of civil and military cooperation

[140] In the event of a national threat, the NAF ensures the protection of the land territory, sea area and airspace of the Latvian state together with the armed forces of the allies

[141] An important task for the self-defense of the state is the establishment of a host state support system for the rapid reception and deployment of allied forces in the territory of Latvia

[142] In the event of a national threat, it is important for the NAF to operate in coordination with the State Fire and Rescue Service, which is responsible for the management and coordination of the civil protection system, thus ensuring the provision of the necessary support

[143] The NAF must continue to maintain and develop combat capabilities that guarantee a rapid response to any type of challenge in the early phases of the threat, developing interoperability with other state institutions involved in overcoming it in the event of a threat, as well as creating an effective and flexible reserve and mobilization system

[144] The NAF must continue to develop active cooperation and create the closest possible coordination mechanisms with the Baltic States, as well as those allied countries that are interested in and are directly involved in strengthening the security of the Baltic Sea region

[145] The state should promote civic awareness, patriotism, direct and as wide an involvement as possible in ensuring national defence

[146] Long-term presence of allies and strengthening of response capabilities 6/13 The guarantor of Latvia's security and defence is the NATO collective defence system, which ensures that potential military threats against Latvia are considered common threats to the alliance

[147] Taking into account strategic changes in the security environment, it is in Latvia's interests to promote NATO's comprehensive adaptation to the new security situation, which would be aimed at improving the efficiency of the alliance's command structure, reorganising the NATO Response Force and establishing a rapid reaction force, which in general would allow the alliance to act and respond to any threat within a few days across the entire spectrum of military forces

[148] At the same time, demonstrating and strengthening NATO solidarity and collective capability in the eastern periphery of the alliance, especially in the Baltic Sea region and on the territory of Latvia, is gaining increasing importance

[149] Presence of allied forces is the most effective solution for Latvia's military security and defense, which ensures deterrence, closer integration with NATO defense structures and armed forces, facilitates the reception of allied forces and their response if necessary, and generally strengthens the defense positions of the Baltic Sea region as the eastern flank of the alliance

[150] Fulfillment of collective defense obligations Latvia undertakes to fulfill the collective defense obligations of Article 5 of NATO and strengthen the security of the Euro-Atlantic area

[151] Latvia will continue to contribute to NATO and EU rapid reaction battle formations, participate in international operations, develop the capabilities necessary for collective defense needs, as well as purposefully promote the growth and modernization of the NAF

[152] In order for Latvia to be able to fulfill its international obligations, implement the planned modernization of the NAF, as well as adequately respond to the challenges posed by the current international situation, it is necessary to allocate at least 2% of the gross domestic product for the financing of national defense

[153] Preventing threats to the unity of civil society A cohesive civil society based on common fundamental values is an important prerequisite for reducing Latvia's domestic political vulnerability and at the same time strengthening national security

[154] These fundamental values must be present in Latvia as an independent and democratic state that belongs to the Western world

[155] Society cannot demand the defense and provision of these fundamental values only from the state

[156] Residents must show initiative and become more involved in socio-political processes in the country, forming and leading non-governmental organizations, participating in the activities of political parties, to the point that residents are ready to defend the country from external threats

[157] The threat to the unity of society and domestic political stability is posed by the compatriot and informational policies implemented by Russian state institutions against Latvia

[158] As a result of the implementation of these policies, Russia is purposefully dividing Latvian society, promoting alienation and tension between individual groups in society

[159] Russia is trying to influence Latvian public opinion, gradually hoping to change the public's perception of historical events, current political processes in the world, and Latvia's membership in the EU and NATO in a direction that Russia desires

[160] Long-term efforts can also be identified to create distrust in the Latvian population towards their state and state administration institutions

[161] This is done with the aim of making the public distrust the state and be indifferent or even negatively disposed towards it, thus ensuring that civil society is unable to function effectively and self-organize

[162] The topics that have long dominated Russia's compatriot and information policy towards Latvia have been strengthening the status of the Russian language, expanding educational opportunities in the Russian language, protecting the rights of compatriots and non-citizens, blaming the Latvian state for the revival of fascism and Nazism, emphasizing Russia's historical presence in Latvia, and emphasizing the apparent separation of Latgale from the rest of Latvia

[163] The currentization of these topics is used to aggravate the domestic political situation in Latvia, threatening the unity of society, as well as to internationally discredit Latvia

[164] Latvia's priorities for preventing threats to the unity of civil society: Civil society participation in socio-political processes It is necessary to implement a targeted civil society development policy in the country, allocating permanent state budget funding for strengthening the capacity of civil society

[165] It is necessary to implement measures and create appropriate conditions that would promote public involvement and participation in socio-political processes in the country

[166] At the same time, efforts should be made to ensure that society feels responsible for the defense of the country and the strengthening of national security, therefore it is necessary to promote public involvement in national defense, creating awareness of threats to national security and risks that may endanger the population, as well as developing knowledge and skills so that residents can react in crisis situations

[167] Development of the state's strategic communication capabilities In order to achieve greater public involvement, targeted strategic communication between the highest state officials and society as a whole, as well as individual groups of it, such as entrepreneurs and representatives of the non-governmental sector, is important

[168] The government should also pay special attention to strategic communication with the regions of Latvia, both local governments and residents living in the regions

[169] Implementing effective strategic communication would reduce the gap between society and the state, as well as the detachment of Latvian regions from the main national-level political decision-making center in Riga

[170] Implementation of the social integration policy It is necessary to improve coordination between institutions that are responsible for and involved in the implementation of the social integration policy

[171] The functions of the responsible institutions in the field of social integration should be more precisely defined in order to avoid duplication of functions

[172] When implementing a long-term social integration policy, it should encompass a set of measures in the areas of language, education, history, information, youth and other areas, with the help of which the threat posed by the compatriots, as well as the information policy implemented by Russia, to Latvia's national security should be prevented

[173] Social integration should be implemented in close connection with an effective state language policy, ensuring that all residents are able to communicate in Latvian, as well as be able to perceive information provided by the government through public and other mass media

[174] The state language policy should be oriented towards improving the use of the state language in everyday communication, as well as ensuring a systemic approach to teaching the state language to non-citizens and immigrants

[175] For the sustainable development of the unity of civil society, it is necessary to implement an education policy that would form a single value orientation in the new generation, regardless of its ethnic origin and the language used in the family, instill belonging to the Latvian state and ensure the formation of a single social memory in young people

[176] Latvian national minorities are an important component of Latvian civil society

[177] The Latvian state must purposefully promote the participation of minorities in socio-political processes through certain initiatives

[178] Involving representatives of minorities in public administration would reduce the alienation of minorities from the state and prevent the risks of developing a bi-communal society

[179] In turn, support for individual initiatives of representatives of minorities would serve as an incentive to increase civil society activities, as well as to promote economic development

[180] In the field of social integration, it is necessary to develop measures that are based on common values and have a positive unifying potential, for example, celebrating national holidays and organizing joint cultural events

[181] Preventing threats to the Latvian information space Information space is a conditional concept today, because technology determines that it has virtually no borders - residents of Latvia can freely choose a source of information, including one whose content is not created in our country and which expresses opinions contrary to the national interests of Latvia

[182] Restricting the choice of the population, as well as controlling the access and provision of content-unwanted information in the Latvian information space, is difficult and, from certain aspects, practically impossible

[183] This is a problem not only for Latvia, but for many democratic countries, because such restrictions can conflict with the principles of democracy - freedom of the press, the prohibition of censorship, as well as with the observance of the principles of free market and competition in the field of mass media

[184] The policy implemented by Russia, using various means of influence to shape Latvian public opinion in the direction it wants, is the main external threat to the Latvian information space, because to achieve this goal, a persistent presence of the Russian information space is maintained and developed in Latvia, which creates significant and in many cases also unequal competition for the Latvian information space

[185] To shape Latvian public opinion, Russia uses a wide range of means oriented towards different audiences

[186] Among them are the already mentioned Russian television and radio stations, several of which have been rebroadcasting Russian-produced content in Latvia for a long time

[187] Russia also benefits from weak Russian-language journalism in Latvia, as it will be more dependent and influenced by Russia, as evidenced by the trends of recent years
