[255] In line with Kenya Vision 2030, the Ministry of Defence continues to support National Industrialization Process through research-based technological innovations and transfer of appropriate technology in the development of Defence Industrial Capacity

[256] The Defence Industrial Capacity will include but not limited to military equipment, food processing and clothing for self-sustenance and spurring of economic growth through utilization of local resources

[257] Space Science and Defence Research and Development (R&D)** 3.12 The Ministry of Defence recognizes Space Science, Research and Development as key components of technological advancement

[285] PART IV** **RESPONSIBILITIES FOR NATIONAL DEFENCE** **Roles and Structures of the Defence Forces** 4.1 Kenya’s Defence Forces are the military instruments of national power available for the defence of the country and have a primarily defensive posture

[286] They consist of the Kenya Army, the Kenya Air Force and the Kenya Navy

[287] They are established under Article 241 of the Constitution and the Kenya Defence Forces Act (No. 44 of

[288] The need to direct and control application of force cannot be overemphasized

[289] In order to ensure that the committed Force achieves the desired objectives, specific Standing Operating Procedures (SOPs) and Rules of Engagement (ROEs) will be issued to cover all operations and activities of the Defence forces

[290] In the conduct of operations, the Kenya Defence Forces shall adhere to the International Law of Armed Conflict

[291] In discharging their mission and other assigned duties, the Defence forces shall be a balanced, modern and technologically advanced military Force capable of executing its tasks effectively and efficiently

[292] The Defence Forces will play specific roles in peacetime, crisis and war

[293] These roles are: a. Peacetime

[294] The task of the Defence Forces in war determines their structure and activities in peacetime

[295] Their primary task is training of personnel and the maintenance of their equipment to ensure the highest operational readiness for deployment in the event of war, which contributes to deterrence

[296] The Defence Forces may be required to assist in the management of crisis

[297] In such an event, they will robustly demonstrate their immediate readiness to execute the assigned tasks

[298] During war, the role of KDF will be to defend the citizens, and Kenya’s sovereignty and territorial integrity, against external threats

[299] The KDF will restore and maintain peace and security on terms most favourable to Kenya’s interests

[300] In order to comprehensively address the respective tasks in the overall defence mandate, individual Services of Land, Air and Naval Forces are assigned specific roles

[301] Each Service may discharge its specific roles independently, jointly or in support of the others as may be determined by the appropriate authority

[302] The Kenya Army** 4.4 The Kenya Army is mandated with the defence and protection of the sovereignty of the Republic against land-based threats

[303] Further it will assist and cooperate with other authorities in situations of emergency or disaster

[304] Additionally, the Army may be called upon to restore peace in any part of Kenya affected by unrest or instability only with the approval of the National Assembly

[305] The Kenya Air Force

[306] The Kenya Air Force is mandated to defend the Republic against external air aggression, provide support to sister Services in their pursuance of national objectives; and assist and cooperate with other government authorities in situations of emergency or disaster

[307] Additionally, the Air Force may be called upon to restore peace in any part of Kenya affected by unrest or instability only with the approval of the National Assembly

[308] The Kenya Navy** 4.6 The Kenya Navy is mandated to defend the Republic against all armed threats from the sea

[309] In accomplishing this mandate, the Navy’s role is to engage and destroy forces before they reach our shores, keep maritime approaches to our ports secure and open to commercial shipping

[310] The Kenya Navy also protects Kenya’s economic resources, facilities and activities at sea from illegal interference and render aid to civil authorities in the maintenance of order in civil emergencies

[311] Additionally, the Navy may be called upon to restore peace in any part of Kenya affected by unrest or instability only with the approval of the National Assembly

[312] Multi-Agency Cooperation** 4.7 To effectively meet our defence responsibilities, the Defence Forces must be able to operate in a combined, joint and in a multi-agency role

[313] Through the Ministry, KDF will cooperate and coordinate its activities with Ministries, Departments and Agencies as part of the whole of government approach concept

[314] Reserve Forces** 4.8 Reserve Forces are those Forces which are not immediately committed in any military duty but are readily available to do so at a short notice on call-up

[315] Reserve Forces may be Regular, Volunteer or Auxiliary as provided for in the KDF Act

[316] The Need for Reserve Forces** 4.9 The need for cost-effectiveness dictates that a large portion of our country’s military strength is kept in reserve

[317] Reserve Forces provide pre-trained manpower which can quickly integrate with Regular Forces without recourse to initial training

[318] They improve national deterrence capacity and provide qualified civilian professionals who may be needed for specialized military tasks at critical times

[319] In wartime, Reserves will be deployed to provide replacements for combat losses

[320] They can also be used to form new units and formations to augment the Regular Forces

[321] In addition, reservists may undertake tasks such as garrison duties, manning air defences, internal security and guarding of key points such as supply depots thus freeing up regular troops

[322] In peacetime, reservists can also be deployed in internal security duties and disaster relief thus reducing reliance on the Regular Forces

[323] The Kenya Defence Forces maintains continuous relationship with other security agencies through the following committees: a. National Security Council (NSC

[324] The NSC is the topmost security coordination committee chaired by the Commander-in-Chief as constituted in Article 240 of the Constitution

[325] b. National Security Advisory Committee (NSAC

[326] This is an inter-ministerial security committee under the auspices of the Office of the President that provides national operational guidance and general direction in the employment of available resources and efforts

[327] The Kenya Intelligence Committee

[328] This committee coordinates all national intelligence matters and Defence Forces are represented by the CDF

[329] d. County Security Intelligence Committee (CSIC

[330] The Defence Forces will have permanent membership in some County security Intelligence Committees, where our units are based/deployed

[331] CONCLUSION** 4.11 The Kenya Defence Forces will remain a crucial element of our national power with capacity to respond to threats to our national interests

[332] The tasks they will be required to undertake range from combat operations, counter terrorism, peace support operations, and HCA activities demand that our Forces must be flexible, agile and responsive enough to achieve the desired outcomes
