[76] INTRODUCTION

[77] The Government's Security and Defence Policy Report 2009 is the latest in the series of Government Reports issued on the subject

[78] The Report provides a comprehensive evaluation of Finnish security and defence policy and guidelines for the future extending into the latter half of the 2010s

[79] The Report begins with an overview of the international situation, followed by an appraisal of how its changes affect Finland

[80] These are followed by guidelines for security and defence policy

[81] The final chapter shows how these guidelines will be implemented to improve Finland’s security

[82] Traditionally, security policy provides the means through which states respond to military or political threats

[83] Security generally refers to the general preconditions for survival, whether it is a question of the sovereignty of the state and its decision-making authority, the life of the individual or the cultural distinctness of a population group

[84] In addition to state-centred security inseparable from sovereignty, security often refers to common or human security

[85] The comprehensive concept of security comprises security issues which, if exacerbated, may turn into threats that can jeopardise or seriously harm Finland, Finns or the functions vital to Finnish society

[86] Wide-ranging threats include premeditated action such as armed aggression, terrorism or network interference

[87] They can also occur spontaneously, such as widespread failures of the electric grid or extreme forces of nature

[88] The threat scenarios for the functions vital to society have been devised with the comprehensive concept of security in mind and provide the basis for our emergency planning

[89] In an interdependent world many security challenges and threats have far-reaching transnational impacts

[90] Traditional security policy instruments and military defence alone will not suffice; fresh approaches are also needed in order to respond to them

[91] Security threats are typically difficult to foresee and provide little early warning

[92] Therefore, in addition to preparedness and response capabilities, attention must be paid to prevention and security-building measures

[93] The Report also deals with global questions such as climate change, scarcity of energy and water resources and population movements

[94] By definition, these do not constitute security threats per se to Finland

[95] but, if left unchecked, could cause widespread destruction or destabilise societies and escalate into violent conflicts, thereby adversely impacting security

[96] At the same time it is important to keep in mind that the use of military force for the attainment of political goals is still a possibility

[97] This too is reflected in the assessment of Finland’s security environment

[98] This Report lays a foundation for all reports, strategies and programmes that the Government prepares on security and international relations

[99] These include, inter alia, the Strategy for Securing the Functions Vital to Society and the Internal Security Programme, Government Report to Parliament on the Human Rights Policy of Finland, EU-related reports, the Development Policy Programme and the National Strategy for Civilian Crisis Management, the Strategic Plan of the Ministry of Defence as well as the UN Strategy

[100] Finland’s most important foreign, security and defence policy goals are safeguarding the country’s independence and territorial sovereignty, guaranteeing the basic values, security and well-being of the population and maintaining a functioning society

[101] Finland’s security is closely linked with international developments

[102] Finland shoulders its share of responsibility for finding solutions to global problems and endeavours to improve the capability of the international community to respond to threats

[103] Membership of the European Union is an integral element of Finland’s security policy

[104] It is in Finland’s interest to strengthen the EU’s international role and the security of the Union

[105] The European Security Strategy defines the Union’s security policy challenges and objectives

[106] EU membership has opened up new opportunities for Finland to influence the security environment in its immediate surroundings

[107] On the other hand, membership also obligates Finland to participate in the handling of questions which, according to traditional security policy thinking, did not necessarily belong to Finland’s priorities

[108] The European Union responds to security policy challenges comprehensively, by using the instruments at its disposal as effectively as possible

[109] Related to this Report the Ministry for Foreign Affairs prepared a Report on the Effects of Finland’s possible NATO membership (12/2007) as well as a report on the mutual assistance obligation in the Treaty on European Union

[110] The Parliamentary Monitoring Group, appointed by the Prime Minister’s Office, made its Report (8/2008) available to the Government as this Report was being fi nalised

[111] INTERNATIONAL SECURITY DEVELOPMENTS The global economy The global environment is characterised by deepening financial integration and rapid movement of capital

[112] Rising living standards have often contributed to political stability

[113] The emerging economies of Asia are more explicitly than before becoming the key engines of global economic growth

[114] Global economic growth has not only boosted economic activity, it has also fostered interdependence

[116] The impact of globalisation has been uneven and the gap between industrial nations and developing countries remains significant

[136] Technological development spawns new security solutions

[137] Among other things, technology enables the use of efficient early-warning and information exchange systems on a global scale

[138] Nearly all vital functions and services of society rely on technical systems, which in turn depend on the availability of electricity and telecommunications

[139] As wireless data transfer in information and communications technology systems becomes more common, accessibility improves

[140] On the other hand, this may degrade reliability

[141] Most private and public services depend on fast and reliable electronic systems

[142] Networking may also increase the risk of a domino effect: if one system crashes it could impinge upon other technical systems

[143] Such disturbances can significantly impact society’s functions

[144] The outsourcing of maintenance and service experts intensifies the consequences of malfunctions

[146] The rising cost of technology is particularly evident in the procurement of defence materiel, with prices estimated at doubling every seven years

[147] Scientific and technological development also creates new security challenges, especially those associated with possible abuses of technology, expertise and skills in the manufacture of chemical, biological, radiological or nuclear weapons
