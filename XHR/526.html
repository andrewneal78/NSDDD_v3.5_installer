[106] within the scope of the State's international commitments

[1130] The statutory regime for military personnel of the Armed Forces derives from the provisions of the National Defense Law and aims to structure the elements that characterize the military condition and to establish a homogeneous and unique legal system for the management of the human resources of the Armed Forces, regardless of the branches, category and form of service provided by the military

[1131] This is justified by the fact that military personnel have a functional status distinct from that of other State servants due to the very nature of the military condition, despite the Armed Forces being integrated into the State Administration through the Ministry of National Defense

[1132] Additionally, and in the context of the military organization and applicable legislation, the statute allows for the definition of the rights and duties of military personnel, the establishment of the rules underlying the military hierarchy with the consequent relationships of authority and dependence, as well as the development and structuring of the military career within the specific framework of the typologies and needs of the Armed Forces

[1133] Since the legislative reform that allowed the integration of statutory norms into a single piece of legislation, with the approval of Decree-Law No. 34-A/90 of 24 January, the statute has been subject to several adaptations resulting from reforms or legislative changes, revealing the alignment of the Armed Forces with political desires, with the evolution of society and with the change in the international context

[1134] For the same reasons, the latest revision of the statute was carried out, approved by the Council of Ministers Resolution No. 26/2013, of April 11, with the aim of ensuring the adequate adjustment of the statutory norms to the reorganization of the macrostructure of the Armed Forces and the rationalization of their resources, namely military personnel, in accordance with the guidelines provided for in the structural reform of national defense and the Armed Forces - “Defense 2020” Reform

[1135] The current Statute of the Military of the Armed Forces thus emerges as the culmination of the process of constructing the new conceptual and legislative edifice in the area of Defense, providing mechanisms for optimizing military personnel and updating organizational processes based on the valorization and safeguarding of the specificities of the military career

[1644] Defense and Security in Europe THE DEFENSE ECONOMY HISTORICAL FRAMEWORK

[1645] Despite Portugal's dominant position during the period of Discoveries in the late 15th, 16th and 17th centuries, the weight of the Portuguese defense industry was always relatively small

[1646] In the 18th century, the Defense industry consisted of a naval arsenal, a gunpowder factory, a foundry and a weapons production factory

[1647] In the mid-19th century, military goods and later fuel began to be supplied by a government entity, Military Maintenance

[1648] At the beginning of the 20th century, a factory was created to supply military uniforms and equipment

[1649] During the 1960s, the Defense industry developed to meet the requirements of anti-guerrilla operations in Africa

[1650] Until the mid-1990s, Portuguese legislation did not allow private sector companies to cover R&D, testing, production or maintenance of equipment intended exclusively for military purposes

[1651] The objective was to restrict the production of military goods, namely bombs, missiles, torpedoes, mines, hand grenades and explosives, to state-owned companies

[1652] The construction of combat aircraft, helicopters, and warships was also limited to state-owned enterprises, although components could be subcontracted to private companies

[1653] Thus, the Armed Forces' manufacturing units included: Military Maintenance, responsible for supplying military goods and later fuel; General Uniform and Equipment Workshops (OGFE), for manufacturing uniforms and equipment; General Engineering Material Workshops (OGME), for the general overhaul of military vehicles; General Aeronautical Material Workshops (OGMA), for the maintenance and repair of all aircraft, avionics, engines, communications and radar equipment of the Portuguese Air Force

[1654] OGMA also had maintenance and repair contracts for other countries and the supply of parts and components to several European aircraft manufacturers

[1655] Indústrias Nacionais de Defesa EP (INDEP), a major munitions company, produced 60 mm and 81 mm mortars, artillery and ammunition, small-caliber ammunition, machine guns and, under a German license from Heckler and Koch, the 7.62 mm G-3 rifle used by the Portuguese army

[1656] The Alfeite Arsenal, at the Lisbon Naval Base, had infrastructure for the construction of patrol vessels, auxiliary vessels, and corvettes, but its most modern ships were built abroad, limiting its activities to their maintenance and repair

[1657] Other equally relevant companies that remain in the market are EID (Electronics Research and Development Company, SA) and EDISOFT (Software Development Services Company SA

[1658] In 1996, EMPORDEF – Empresa portuguesa de Defesa SGPS SA, a publicly owned holding company under the supervision of the Ministry of National Defense and the Ministry of Finance, was created, grouping together the state's holdings in all companies in the area of National Defense

[1659] In 2015, given the profound changes that had taken place in the Defence Economy sector, and with the aim of the State intervening more in support of national companies rather than competing with them, and at the culmination of a process of disposal of State shareholdings in different companies in the Defence sector, it was decided to proceed with the liquidation and extinction of Empordef SGPS, SA

[1743] More recently, and under the terms defined in the Organic Law of the MDN, approved by Decree-Law No. 183/2014, of December 29, in particular paragraph a) of article 7 and article 22, the AMN is a structure of the Ministry defined as “the structure responsible for carrying out activities, at national level, in the areas of jurisdiction and within the framework of attributions defined in the Maritime Authority System, in compliance with the guidelines defined by the Minister of National Defense”

[1910] Orphan drugs are pharmaceutical products intended for the prevention, diagnosis or treatment of very serious or life-threatening diseases that are rare

[1911] These drugs are designated as “orphan” because, under normal market conditions, the pharmaceutical industry has little interest in developing and marketing products aimed at the small number of patients affected by very rare diseases

[1912] Medicines intended for rare diseases may be granted the “orphan drug” label based on a defined number of criteria listed below

[1913] The product is intended for an indication whose prevalence does not exceed 5 in 10,000 people in the European Union (EU

[1914] The disease is potentially fatal, severely debilitating, or a serious and chronic condition

[1915] No satisfactory method of diagnosis, prevention or treatment of the condition is yet authorized in the EU

[1916] If a method exists, the drug for which orphan designation is being requested must demonstrate that it provides significant benefits to those affected. From this list, caffeine citrate is already in the production range and is used for respiratory depression in premature infants

[1917] In addition to caffeine citrate, several pediatric oral solutions are produced, such as biotin, potassium chloride, chloral hydrate, isoniazid, zinc sulfate, and trimethoprim
