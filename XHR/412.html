[269] Such processes include promoting greater interoperability with joint, interagency, and international partners while encouraging action through decentralized execution

[270] We are conducting resource-informed planning

[271] For nearly a generation, we have consumed readiness as quickly as we have generated it

[272] As a result, our long-term readiness has declined

[273] Therefore, we are taking action to better balance achieving our operational goals with sustaining ready surge forces at home

[274] We are revising operational plans to be more flexible, creative, and integrated across Combatant Commands

[275] We also are providing the Services with time to reset, modernize, and replace vital equipment

[276] Our goal is to strengthen deterrence while ensuring the long-term viability of our full-spectrum power projection capacity

[277] Additionally, we are more fully coordinating requirements, plans, and operational execution at home and abroad to maximize collective capabilities against common concerns

[278] And we are using tailored forces that deploy for limited timeframes to execute specific missions, recognizing that “campaign persistence” is necessary against determined adversaries

[279] We are improving our global agility

[280] The ability to quickly aggregate and disaggregate forces anywhere in the world is the essence of global agility

[281] We are striving to increase our agility by improving campaign planning, sustaining a resilient global posture, and implementing dynamic force management processes that adjust presence in anticipation of events, to better seize opportunities, deter adversaries, and assure allies and partners

[282] We also are more fully sharing forces among Combatant Commands to address transregional threats

[283] We are positioning forces where they are most needed, exemplified by our rebalance to the Asia-Pacific region as well as our evolving presence in Europe, the Middle East, Latin America, and Africa

[284] We also are updating international agreements to assure access and provide legal protection for our people

[285] Such agreements allow us to strengthen the relationships that are the foundation of trust

[286] We are demanding greater effectiveness and efficiencies

[287] In a resource-constrained environment, we are striving to be careful stewards of our resources

[288] Programmatic discipline by the Services has never been more important, as it is vital to generating economic efficiencies

[289] We are working to sustain our industrial base while seeking savings through the Department of Defense’s Better Buying Power 3.0 initiative

[290] We are selectively using contractor support when it best serves the mission

[291] We also are reducing staffs, streamlining functions, eliminating redundancies, and producing more integrated and effective organizations

[292] C. Programs: Sustaining Our Quality Edge Effective programs enable our Soldiers, Sailors, Airmen, Marines, and Coast Guardsmen to fight and win

[293] Delivering next-generation programs on schedule and within cost is vital, as our current systems increasingly are being challenged by adversary capabilities

[294] To win against the diverse range of state and non-state threats confronting us, we must think innovatively, challenge assumptions, and embrace change

[295] We are improving joint interoperability

[296] We are in the process of defining the next set of interoperability standards for future capabilities

[297] In view of the anti-access/area denial (A2/AD) challenges we increasingly face, our future force will have to operate in contested environments

[298] Key to assuring such access will be deploying secure, interoperable systems between Services, allies, interagency, and commercial partners

[299] Priority efforts in that regard are establishing a Joint Information Environment (JIE), advancing globally integrated logistics, and building an integrated Joint ISR Enterprise

[300] The results of these initiatives — particularly the enhanced connectivity and cybersecurity provided by the JIE — will provide the foundation for future interoperability

[301] We are investing to enhance decisive advantages

[302] Future capabilities must sustain our ability to defend the homeland and project military power globally

[303] Important investments to counter A2/AD, space, cyber, and hybrid threats include: space and terrestrial-based indications and warning systems, integrated and resilient ISR platforms, strategic lift, long-range precision strike weapons, missile defense technologies, undersea systems, remotely operated vehicles and technologies, special operations forces, and the Cyber Mission Force, among others

[304] We also are improving our global sustainment capabilities and upgrading our command and control infrastructure to better support widely dispersed operations

[305] We are modernizing our nuclear enterprise and working to protect our Nation against asymmetric threats

[306] To improve institutional agility, we are expanding relations with American businesses, including many of the most innovative companies in the world, to learn their best practices

[307] Further, we are aligning our programmatic efforts to take advantage of insights gleaned from the Defense Innovation Initiative, which is aimed at identifying potential strategic and operational advantages through wargaming, concept development, and a wide array of technology investments

[308] As we develop new capabilities to counter threats along the continuum of conflict, we also must procure sufficient capacity and readiness to sustain our global responsibilities

[309] This may include evolving traditional platforms

[310] Or it may require developing entirely new systems that are affordable and flexible

[311] In all cases, our programs must allow us to quickly adapt, to counter adversaries employing unexpected techniques or weapons

[312] V. Conclusion This 2015 National Military Strategy provides an overview of our strategic challenges and details how we will employ the Joint Force to keep our Nation, allies, and partners safe

[313] It is a strategy that recognizes the increasing complexity of the global environment, driven by rapid and profound change

[314] It also acknowledges our significant advantages, our commitment to international norms, the importance of our allies and partners, and the powerful allure of freedom and human dignity

[315] When placed in balance against the challenges before us, these strengths will serve us well and help us achieve a more secure future
