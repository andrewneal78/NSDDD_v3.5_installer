[83] National Defense Policies of Major Countries and Their Approaches to Creating Stability in the International Community U.S. Defense Policy The U.S. recognized from the 9.11 terrorist attacks that its geographical condition could not make its territory free from a direct attack, and has decided to give top priority to homeland defense

[292] Section 1 The Basic Concept of Japan’s Defense Policy Measures to Ensure National Security For a nation to achieve its peace and security, mere desire to do so does not suffice

[293] As the world becomes increasingly interdependent, only through a comprehensive approach involving, among other things, diplomatic efforts, defense efforts and cooperation with alliance partners, can a nation ensure its peace and security

[301] The Constitution and the Right of Self-Defense It is recognized beyond doubt that, as long as Japan remains an independent nation, the Constitution does not deny the inherent right of self-defense that Japan as a sovereign state is entitled to maintain

[302] It is the established interpretation of the Constitution that it allows Japan to possess a force that is minimum necessary for national self-defense

[303] The right of self-defense under Article 9 of the Constitution may be exercised only when the following three conditions are met: ―there is an imminent and illegitimate act of aggression against Japan; ―there is no appropriate means to deal with such an act of aggression other than by resorting to the right of self-defense; and ―the use of armed strength is confined to the minimum necessary level

[304] It is recognized under international law that a state has the right of collective self-defense, which is the right to use armed force to stop an armed attack on a foreign country with which it has close relations, even when the state itself is not under direct attack

[305] Since Japan is a sovereign state, it naturally is entitled to the right of collective self-defense

[306] It has been the position of the Government, however, that the exercise of the right of collective self-defense exceeds the limit of self-defense authorized under Article 9 of the Constitution and is not therefore permissible under the Constitution

[322] The 1995 NDPO can be summarized as a concept with greater emphasis on effective operations of defense forces Japan possesses and look for more proactive use of SDF capabilities in various missions not limited to (narrowly defined) national defense

[365] In recognition of these points, future defense force shall be characterized by high response capability, mobility, flexibility and multi-purpose functionality
