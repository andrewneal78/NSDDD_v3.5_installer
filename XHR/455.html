[87] THE GLOBAL STAGE We are witnessing a time of profound changes in the global geostrategic environment, which have a decisive impact on the lives of human beings, organizations and States

[88] This new situation is marked by accelerated globalization—characterized by a complex interdependence of economic, social, political, environmental, and military processes—which has altered the maps of regional balances, geopolitical concepts, ways of life, values, and concepts of security and defense

[89] The previous world order, known as the "Cold War" period, in force since the period immediately following World War II, was marked by a clear political, economic and ideological confrontation between two large blocs that disputed - outside their territorial space - world domination

[90] This strategic environment was evident: the geographical and political spheres and the actors involved could be clearly identified

[91] Therefore, the threats were clearly determined and mostly predictable

[92] Nuclear deterrence and balance of power were the dominant political-strategic concepts in global security

[93] This is how a dynamic of geo-power was established in which the superpowers were concerned with structuring alliances on all continents, especially in those geographic locations they considered to be of strategic interest; furthermore, there was a constant concern to balance and surpass the adversary's military potential and permanently improve their own military capabilities

[94] In this context of conflict between hegemonic powers, Latin America constituted a peripheral region, and its importance stemmed from its role as a space for interdiction and neutralization against the possible ideological influence of "real socialism

[95] Within this framework, the majority of American States formed part of a continental security structure supported by the concept of collective security, in which each State, as a component of the American security system, felt protected by a defense superstructure, conceived and implemented through the Inter-American Treaty of Reciprocal Assistance (TIAR

[96] However, even before the end of the Cold War, this system presented significant questions and presented problems in its interpretation and, above all, in its application

[97] The rapid and unexpected events of the late 1980s and early 1990s—the fall of the Berlin Wall, the reunification of Germany, and the dismemberment of the Soviet Union, among others—destroyed the bipolar order of the Cold War, which had been in place for four decades

[98] This historic change established a new strategic scenario that responded to new conditions and paradigms, placing the world in a process of transition, in which the rules are just being structured

[99] A paradox is then established: the security and defense systems and concepts of countries still respond to a certain extent to a scenario that disappeared and have not yet fully adapted to the needs of the new current scenarios

[100] The most notable events marking this era are the consolidation of the democratic system as a form of government; the universalization, promotion, and respect for values related to human rights; regional integration; and the predominance of capitalism, with the opening of markets as a distinctive factor that increased the development possibilities of peoples within the framework of growing globalization

[101] Another characteristic of the “new order” is the consolidation of the United States as the world’s leading power

[102] Despite the high expectations generated by this transformation, disenchantment with the “new order” quickly became evident

[103] The current economic model requires free trade and competitive capacity as basic conditions and, although global wealth has experienced significant increases, the beneficiaries have basically been the great powers and especially the transnational companies

[104] Poor countries were poorly positioned and prepared, and had to enter into an unequal competition

[105] Analysts have drawn up various interpretations of the terrorist attacks of September

[106] In any case, these events allowed us to establish some clarifications regarding the current geostrategic reality

[107] At the beginning of the millennium, states had the capacity to address conflict models that responded to the reality of the end of the Cold War

[108] • The form and scenarios of conflicts have changed

[109] In addition to conventional forms, today they can be globalized confrontations, without borders, without the participation of organized military forces and with the civilian population as a target

[110] • The acceleration of the globalization process has universalized political, economic, and scientific progress, but also certain factors of destruction such as terrorism and organized crime

[111] Although not the most significant event in this new world order, the events of September 11, 2001, demonstrated that the process is just beginning and that we are living in a time of transition

[112] After that day, a global awareness became clear that globalization is a phenomenon that also facilitates the spread of new threats, which had been neglected or had not yet acquired transcontinental dimensions

[113] Furthermore, a different notion of “asymmetric threats” is incorporated, internationalizing the fight against terrorism and drug trafficking and giving rise to new perspectives and approaches to security

[114] NEW THREATS TO SECURITY The new threats to security and defense are the following: Extreme poverty and social injustice

[115] Rising levels of poverty and extreme poverty in poor countries affect the political stability of states, particularly through negative effects on their social and economic development

[116] Terrorism has reached global dimensions and today no State or organization is safe, given the organizational capacity and projection of these networks

[117] These new scenarios allow us to anticipate the actions not only of radical groups, but also the active presence of religious fundamentalists, whose sphere of influence extends beyond state borders

[118] Drug trafficking has developed a complex system of criminal networks, increasingly powerful and sophisticated

[119] This threat to humanity generates in its environment a vast system of corruption, violence and human degradation

[120] The possibility that terrorist and fundamentalist groups—or governments that support these types of movements—may be able to access nuclear, chemical, or bacteriological weapons

[121] The division of the former Soviet Union's nuclear arsenal and technological advances in this area have meant that the possession of nuclear weapons is no longer the exclusive preserve of the major powers; there are now several countries and organizations suspected of possessing the capability to produce nuclear weapons, and many of them are sympathetic to terrorist groups

[122] The latest events on the Indo-Pakistani border have shown the world that the dangers of a military confrontation between countries with nuclear technological capabilities are not ruled out

[123] Added to the above is the obvious danger of the possession and use, in various regions of the planet, of chemical and bacteriological weapons

[124] Corruption affects States, especially their administrative structures, diverting important economic resources, compromising national development and increasing inequality and the concentration of wealth

[125] • Environmental degradation, the alteration of the ecological balance, the constant increase in the world population, the location of nuclear and industrial waste, the contamination of water, soil, air and deforestation are growing threats that will force the world community and each State to protect their environment

[126] • The scarcity of natural resources, especially fresh water and energy, determines the prospect of future conflicts

[127] Energy will become increasingly contested, as gas, oil, and coal reserves are progressively depleted. If they are not replaced by alternative sources such as thermonuclear, nuclear, solar, or wind power, this will be a clear cause of new intra- and interstate conflicts

[128] States have developed different perceptions regarding these threats and the current situation, thus shifting their security and defense agendas

[129] The United States of America, assuming that the great powers are on the same side and have coinciding interests against their common enemies who use violence and terrorism, configures the dictates of international security, explained in its new “National Security Strategy”, guided by the following guidelines: lead the desires for human dignity; strengthen alliances to defeat global terrorism and act to prevent attacks against the United States and its friends; collaborate with other States to resolve regional conflicts and, in the Western Hemisphere, do so through “flexible coalitions” with countries that share the interests of the first power, such as Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Colombia and Canada; prevent enemies from threatening with weapons of mass destruction; sustain a new era of global economic growth through free trade and markets, which in the hemisphere translates into pushing for regional initiatives such as the creation of the Free Trade Area of the Americas (FTAA); expand the development cycle to open it up to societies and create an infrastructure for democracy; develop cooperative action programs with other centers of global power; and transform their security institutions to meet the challenges and seize the opportunities of the 21st century

[130] South America is making significant progress in building regional peace, aided by a substantial reduction in the likelihood of conventional conflicts between States occurring in the immediate future

[131] This new reality has enabled successful efforts to build and consolidate peace by strengthening ties between states and fostering confidence-building and security measures among them
