[23] Assumptions of Polish security policy

[24] The contemporary concept of state security includes both political and military issues as well as economic, ecological, social and ethnic aspects

[25] In each of these areas, Polish security policy is consistent with international law, in particular with United Nations law, with the provisions and decisions of the Conference on Security and Cooperation in Europe, and with bilateral and multilateral agreements to which Poland is a party

[26] The Republic of Poland treats its borders as inviolable and has no territorial claims against its neighbors

[27] It respects the sovereignty of other states and renounces the use of force, including the threat of force, in bilateral relations with other states

[28] Poland wants to cooperate particularly closely with neighbouring countries

[29] Integration with Western Europe Poland's future membership in the European Communities is of fundamental importance for our country

[30] Economic integration with the West is also guided by political motives and goals

[31] We treat the Association as a stage in the pursuit of achieving, as soon as possible, full membership in the European Communities, or - in the future European Union, which will be established in place of the Communities

[32] This is a difficult task requiring the introduction of many adaptive changes and consistent cooperation of the entire society

[33] We can overcome our civilizational backwardness and become an equal partner of developed countries only by quickly and effectively including Poland in the process of economic and political integration with Western Europe

[34] Voluntary isolation and failure to take advantage of the opportunity for rapid development would inevitably lead over time to the marginalization of the Polish economy and the impoverishment of society, and this would threaten the loss of freedom and sovereignty

[35] Relations with the European Communities will facilitate, and membership in them will determine our participation in the North Atlantic Alliance (NATO), should it not have occurred earlier

[36] The political and military factor continues to have a significant influence on the development of new relations between nations in Europe after the fall of the Soviet Empire

[37] Poland seeks links with existing defense alliances and security systems and strives to strengthen these structures

[38] Recognizing that the North Atlantic Alliance remains a fundamental factor of political stability and peace in Europe, Poland particularly values the Euro-Atlantic nature of this alliance and supports the presence of American troops on our continent

[39] Poland's strategic goal in the 1990s is membership in NATO and the Western European Union as the European pillar of NATO and an important factor in the European collective security system

[40] We currently develop our relations with the Alliance primarily through the North Atlantic Cooperation Council

[41] Our country's future membership in NATO will have a positive impact on Polish-German and Polish-Russian relations

[47] New regional cooperation The collapse of the USSR and the emergence of new independent states in addition to Russia, especially Ukraine, created an opportunity to permanently increase security in Eastern Europe

[48] Currently, however, it is a politically unstable region

[55] The regional cooperation developed by Poland with Hungary and CSFR, and soon – in its place – with the Czech Republic and Slovakia, also serves to reduce the above-mentioned threats

[56] Regardless of the direction of political changes in our southern neighbors, we strive to maintain cooperation within the Visegrad Group

[58] Poland aims to effectively include countries in Central and Eastern Europe in the Euro-Atlantic security system

[60] Poland has its own military potential and state defence system

[61] We are determined to defend our sovereignty and territorial integrity and oppose any aggression

[62] We are currently undergoing a profound transformation to adapt our defence structures to the needs of a sovereign, medium-sized European country like Poland

[63] The essential element of the defence system are armed forces that are constantly ready to undertake combat missions and to deploy in war

[65] In principle, Poland should achieve this goal on its own, but it may be advisable to use political or material assistance from other countries

[71] The radical political changes taking place in Poland inevitably cause a periodic increase in internal threats

[76] The fundamental way to limit the negative effects of these phenomena is to strive to shorten the transition period

[78] Security policy and defence strategy of the Republic of Poland Introduction Poland's geopolitical location is particularly convenient for maintaining close contacts with many European countries

[79] At the same time, however, the location of our country on the European geostrategic axis makes the problems of defense and security a key issue of the Polish raison d'état

[80] The aim of the Republic of Poland is to defend and strengthen an independent, sovereign statehood, guaranteeing respect for human rights, freedoms and civil liberties

[81] The lasting, independent and secure existence of the state is a condition for the formation of an efficient democratic system based on the principles of civil society and the successful implementation of market reforms that improve the national economy

[82] Poland, a country with a thousand-year tradition of statehood, adopted Christianity from Rome, joining the circle of Western civilization

[83] After a half-century break, the Polish nation desires to restore the general civilizational orientation towards the West

[84] By consolidating its independence and security, Poland wishes to participate in building European unity and creating a new, just international order

[85] The above premises, as well as the nature of existing and potential threats to the country's security, are the basis of the security policy and defence strategy of the Republic of Poland

[86] Conditions and threats

[87] The fundamental changes that took place at the turn of the 1980s and 1990s in Central and Eastern Europe created new security conditions for the Republic of Poland

[88] The collapse of the Soviet empire was a result of the rejection of the totalitarian political system and communist ideology

[89] This disintegration gave all the states and nations of Central and Eastern Europe a chance to liberate themselves from foreign domination imposing a totalitarian political system and communist ideology

[90] Poland was the first to take advantage of it, then our southern neighbors took advantage of this opportunity

[91] Hungary and CSR, countries with similar economic opportunities and political aspirations

[92] Soon after, communism was overthrown in Bulgaria, Romania and Albania

[93] Poland found itself between the unifying Western Europe and the disintegrating former USSR

[94] The pursuit of integration with the European Communities offers Poland the greatest hope for consolidating its independent statehood, democracy and successful economic development

[95] This is a geostrategic direction not only of political and economic rapprochement, but also of Poland's participation in an effective Western collective security system

[96] Our largest and most developed neighbor is the Federal Republic of Germany

[97] Cooperation with Germany is one of the main paths leading Poland towards an integrated Western Europe

[98] The development of the Polish economy also inevitably requires partnerships with the German economy

[99] The fall of the Soviet empire and the emergence of independent states alongside Russia, especially Ukraine, creates a new geostrategic quality

[100] In Eastern Europe, a completely different balance of power is emerging, enabling a lasting increase in security on a continental scale

[101] Poland is taking advantage of this opportunity

[102] However, the process of change in the East is causing a number of threats that may emerge in the near future

[103] A serious danger would be the rejection by the successor states of the USSR of the disarmament obligations taken over from that empire

[104] This could lead to uncontrolled proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and unconstrained conventional forces

[105] The threat of armed conflicts breaking out on border, ethnic or economic grounds between new political entities, with neighbouring countries, including Poland, or even the entire region, being drawn into the orbit of military operations, cannot be ruled out either

[106] Our security may be threatened by: a large wave of migration from the east, from or through the territories of neighbouring countries, as well as the economic consequences of breaking off trade relations, in particular the interruption of supplies of strategic raw materials and cutting off from traditional sales markets

[107] Radical systemic changes always cause a periodic increase in internal threats

[108] These are primarily non-military political, socio-economic and ecological threats that weaken our state and increase its vulnerability to external pressure and infiltration

[109] The inevitable social costs of the crisis of the centrally planned economy and the reforms carried out, especially recession and unemployment, contribute to the spread of frustration, disorganization, mass emigration, crime and other pathological social phenomena

[110] A social atmosphere is being created that allows the spread of dangerous chauvinistic tendencies

[111] The fundamental means of countering these threats is to maximally accelerate the transformation process and shorten the transition period

[112] Extending the reconstruction period would inevitably lead to deeper tensions and social disorders

[113] The processes of democratizing the political system, introducing a market economy and liberalizing social relations require changes to old structures and habits, which often encounters decisive resistance

[114] The progress of transformation is hampered both by the inefficiency of parts of the state apparatus, as well as by conscious opposition from apologists of the old order and extremist forces

[115] There are awareness blockades in society, resulting from long-term Sovietization, which make it difficult to adapt to new conditions

[116] Overcoming the recession and enabling Poland's further economic development is crucial for the pace and effectiveness of systemic transformation

[117] This requires widespread privatization and a fundamental change in the economic management system

[118] Partnership with the West and its assistance, both materially and institutionally, also play an important role here

[119] The success of the transformation is also largely dependent on administrative reform consolidating and stabilising the state structure and increasing the efficiency of its functioning

[120] External aspects of security policy Basic principles Poland pursues a security policy consistent with international law, in particular with the provisions of the United Nations Charter, and other interstate acts, both bilateral and multilateral

[121] This policy is also consistent with the provisions of the Conference on Security and Cooperation in Europe

[122] We consider respect for the inviolability of existing borders in Europe, renunciation of territorial claims, respect for sovereignty, non-interference in internal affairs, and renunciation of the use of force or the threat of use of force to be particularly important in international relations

[123] In order to ensure security, our country wants to cooperate closely with all its neighbors and does not intend to enter into military alliances with one neighbor against another

[124] We also treat neighbourly and regional cooperation as a way to facilitate joint involvement in the future, pan-European collective security system

[125] Poland is interested in building a collective security system that would encompass Europe and North America

[126] We advocate for the development and adoption by all states of a model of non-confrontational military doctrine, aimed at increasing the security not only of a given country, but also the security of other countries, especially neighboring countries

[127] Poland supports increased transparency and building international trust in the military sphere

[128] We consider eliminating the atmosphere of uncertainty and mutual suspicion to be an important factor in reducing the threat of war

[129] We consider the idea of creating multinational armed forces and Poland's participation in them to be worth developing

[130] We consider it possible to create mixed military units together with our neighbors

[131] We will continue to send Polish Army units to participate in UN Peacekeeping Forces or to operate in other UN, CSCE, NATO, North Atlantic Cooperation Council or Western European Union missions

[132] Poland supports disarmament initiatives and will continue to actively participate in the relevant negotiations

[133] We consider the negotiations and resulting agreements on the reduction and limitation of armed forces, especially conventional forces in Europe, to be particularly important

[134] Poland will strive to create a new military order in Europe that would eliminate the possibility of a surprise attack and large-scale offensive operations

[135] Our country supports strict observance of the principle of non-proliferation of nuclear weapons and the reduction of nuclear arsenals

[136] Having our own defense potential is one of the basic attributes of our sovereignty

[137] In line with the disarmament process, Poland is currently carrying out the necessary restructuring and conversion of its arms industry, which was overdeveloped and biased during its forced membership in the Warsaw Pact

[138] International cooperation Poland intends to develop contacts and cooperation in the field of defense with its neighbors and other countries

[139] We want to expand forms of military scientific, training and cultural cooperation

[140] We are interested in joint conferences, exchange of students and military trainees, and organizing joint practices, exercises and maneuvers

[141] We consider mutual visits and get-to-know-you events desirable

[142] In eliminating sources of potential armed conflicts, Poland also attaches great importance to areas of human activity other than political and military ones

[143] Our country strives to expand economic cooperation and opposes the use of discriminatory restrictions in this area

[144] We are against using the economic advantage of some countries to impose their will on other countries

[145] We recognize that economic partnership in the international arena and a healthy market economy within the country are important factors in political stability, which reduces the risk of armed conflict

[146] We support facilitating all types of interpersonal contacts that ensure the free flow of information, the intermingling of cultures and the elimination of nationalist prejudices

[147] It is in Poland's interest to co-shape an international security system that will increasingly effectively eliminate military threats and foster a balance of interests and cooperation in solving global problems and challenges

[148] The basic principle of our country's external security policy is to treat Europe and North America as a single security area

[149] The gradually created European security system may become the main guarantor of the sovereignty and independence of the Republic of Poland in the future

[150] Poland is working to expand a cooperative security system consisting of many complementary and interlocking international institutions

[151] We intend not only to develop, but also to initiate new forms of bilateral ties, primarily with neighbouring countries, other European countries and North American countries

[153] Our country is an active participant in the Conference on Security and Cooperation in Europe

[154] North Atlantic Alliance The North Atlantic Alliance (NATO) remains the main factor of stability and security in Europe

[155] We consider the continued military presence of the United States in Europe to be necessary, as it has a positive impact on the political transformation processes in our region and stabilizes the situation across the continent

[156] Poland aims to become a member of NATO

[157] We will continue to expand our system of contacts, cooperation and political consultations with various structures of this organization

[158] We will work to create a network of bilateral agreements with individual NATO members on defense and security matters

[159] We are interested in developing and intensifying cooperation within the North Atlantic Cooperation Council, of which we are a member

[165] For Poland's interests, the CSCE process is particularly important as a mechanism for politically and militarily stabilizing the new member states established in the territories of the former USSR

[167] The long-term priority of Polish foreign policy is to achieve our country's membership in the European Communities

[172] Poland's economic and political integration with the unifying Western Europe is a difficult and long-term process

[173] We can be admitted to the European Communities after the country has emerged from the crisis and the lag in our development in relation to Western countries has been reduced

[201] From the point of view of national security, the basic task of the economy is to meet the material defense needs of the state, and in particular: - ensuring optimal conditions for maintaining and training the armed forces in peacetime, - preparing the base for the army to perform tasks in times of threat to the state's security and during war, - creating conditions for the survival of the population in extreme situations

[203] The obligation to create and maintain economic and defense infrastructure rests mainly with the heads of ministries and voivode

[204] The functions of meeting current needs should be combined with defensive functions

[218] The existing strategic directions of activities aimed at preparing the country's economic infrastructure for defense needs will be maintained

[222] Great opportunities for strengthening national security lie in international cooperation in the economic and defense fields

[223] Poland is interested in cooperation in the arms industry, trade in equipment and weapons, and conducting joint research and implementation in the field of defense with NATO member states, CISF, Hungary and other countries

[225] The strategic goal of the defense of the Republic of Poland is to maintain the sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity of the state

[227] The defense strategy of the Republic of Poland does not assume the existence of a specific opponent or a fixed scenario of military operations

[228] It is aimed at managing and resolving crisis situations of a military nature

[229] Poland will strive to resolve all disputes through negotiation, mediation, conciliation, arbitration or international courts, as well as other methods used by the UN, CSCE or, in the future, other organizations of which our country will be a member

[230] If it is impossible to prevent an armed conflict to which the Republic of Poland is a party (or participant, if this results from concluded international agreements), we will actively oppose armed aggression while continuing diplomatic efforts to end the conflict

[231] In the event of a war that goes beyond a local conflict, Poland's strategy will be to resist for as long as possible in order to: stop the invasion - by inflicting maximum losses on the aggressor, demonstrate the determination to defend itself and gain time for the reaction of other countries and international institutions

[232] In the event of a lower-intensity conflict, independent defensive and intervention operations will be carried out in order to locate, stop and destroy the enemy invasion group as quickly as possible

[233] Patrol, cover, blocking, anti-sabotage and anti-terrorist actions will also be undertaken

[234] To ensure the functioning of the state and the life of the nation in wartime, it will be necessary to implement three groups of tasks: protection of the population and state structures, - organizing and supplementing the logistical support of the armed forces, - organizing and meeting the material and spiritual needs of the population

[236] Organizing and supplementing the logistical support of the armed forces includes in particular: strengthening these forces with reserve soldiers and conscripts, supplying technical equipment and materials, medical assistance and all kinds of spiritual services and moral support

[237] Meeting the needs of the armed forces and the population in wartime will require the subordination of the national economy to war goals

[239] Poland's participation in international security systems assumes the possibility of its military involvement in resolving conflicts outside the territory of our country based on treaties binding us and on the basis of decisions of the relevant international bodies

[240] The Polish Army must therefore be constantly prepared to form operational groups in order to perform various tasks within the framework of missions and armed expeditions as part of multinational allied forces

[241] In order to counteract war threats and to carry out external and internal defence tasks, the defence system of the Republic of Poland is maintained in times of peace and developed in times of threat and war

[249] Moreover, the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland are prepared to perform tasks arising from international obligations and agreements binding Poland at the time of the outbreak of a conflict

[250] Armed forces can also be used to eliminate the effects of accidents, catastrophes and natural disasters

[251] The Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland are based on a system of compulsory and professional military service

[253] The equipment of basic weapons will be maintained at a level not exceeding the limits established in disarmament treaties

[254] The composition, equipment and deployment of troops within the country are intended to ensure: maintaining part of the armed forces in readiness for immediate action, efficient implementation of the mobilization development of combat potential and the transition of the country to a system of war management and supply

[256] The Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland consist of three types of troops: - land forces, - air forces and air defense, - navy

[257] Their structures include operational troops and territorial defense forces and means

[258] Operational forces are prepared to perform the main tasks during war operations

[259] Territorial defense forces support and secure the activities of operational troops and carry out independent protective and defensive operations in designated areas of responsibility

[260] The land forces constitute the core of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland

[261] They are designed to repel land and air attacks by an aggressor with active defensive operations in cooperation with other types of troops, in any region of the country, in any direction and against any form of military threat

[262] The air force and air defense forces constitute the type of armed forces with the highest level of combat readiness in peacetime

[263] Their main task is to ensure protection of the territory of the Republic of Poland and to counteract violations of Polish airspace

[264] The Navy is primarily intended to defend the state's maritime border, protect shipping and economic interests in Polish maritime areas, and defend the coast in cooperation with other branches of the armed forces

[265] The combat capability of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland is achieved through high morale, discipline and training of soldiers and equipping units with modern weapons and equipment

[266] Priority is given to improving the organizational structures of the military, developing means and systems for reconnaissance, command, electronic warfare, fire control, and air and anti-tank defense

[267] The efficient functioning of the armed forces will be ensured by a system of military logistical support, using the allocated potential of all sectors of the national economy

[268] In peacetime, the armed forces are maintained in combat and mobilization readiness, ensuring their efficiency in the event of the need for their operational deployment

[269] The military's potential in peacetime is adapted to the strategic and operational needs and economic capabilities of the state

[270] The degree of completion of units and the system of their replenishment vary depending on the tasks expected during the war

[271] The transition of the armed forces from peacetime structure and numerical strength to wartime structure and numerical strength takes place through their mobilization development

[272] The fundamental factor guaranteeing the achievement of the required combat potential of military units is the planned accumulation of trained personnel reserves as well as transport and material means

[273] In addition to military command and management units, this process also involves specific bodies of the government's general administration and local governments

[274] Commanding troops in times of threat and war is carried out through the developed military command system of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland

[275] It is an integral part of the state defense management system

[276] The Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland is the President of the Republic of Poland

[277] In peacetime, the Minister of National Defence directs all activities of the armed forces

[278] Non-military defence elements Protecting the population against catastrophic industrial threats, natural disasters and the effects of warfare is a constitutional obligation of all authorities, state and local government administration, as well as the obligation of social organizations and economic entities

[279] The tasks arising from this obligation consist mainly in recognizing, preventing and combating all forms of threats to the security of the nation and the state

[280] This applies to the defence of the state, its public order and security, and the lives and property of the population in times of peace and war

[281] Criminal offences pose serious threats to people's health and property, not directly related to military operations

[282] Preventing them, as well as revealing and combating them, is the basic statutory obligation of all specialized services subordinate to the Minister of Internal Affairs, i.e.: the Office of State Protection, the Police, the Border Guard and other formations

[283] The assistance of citizens in carrying out these tasks is their legal and moral obligation towards the state and their own environment

[284] The National Civil Defence is entrusted with coordinating the activities of all forces and means serving to protect the life and property of the population and cultural assets, within the framework of the state defence system

[285] Civil Defense, in accordance with the provisions of international war law

[286] undertakes to: - plan projects to protect the life and health of the population, protect workplaces, public utilities and cultural assets against the effects of warfare, - organize the detection of threats in the above scope, - warn and alarm the population and their evacuation, - prepare protective facilities, - supply the population with equipment and personal protective equipment, - protect goods essential for survival, - organize rescue operations and the removal of contamination

[287] The National Civil Defence is provided with conditions and opportunities to operate efficiently

[288] For this purpose, staff are prepared and appointed, appropriate conditions for their functioning are created and they are provided with the necessary materials and resources

[289] Training is organised within the general national education system, in specialised centres, in local government units, local governments and workplaces

[290] The mass media are also involved for this purpose

[291] Warfare constitutes the highest form of threat to mass loss of life and property

[292] The use of warfare agents may additionally cause industrial and ecological disasters

[293] Civil Defence maintains armed neutrality in time of war

[294] Its task is to prepare and implement activities to provide assistance to the population, including in areas temporarily occupied by the enemy

[295] Defense Management System

[296] The basic functions in the state defence management system in peacetime are defined by the Constitution of the Republic of Poland

[297] They are implemented by: the President of the Republic of Poland, the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers

[298] The management of state defence is exercised by all state and local government authorities and administration bodies, in accordance with the competences and defence tasks assigned to them in the Constitution and other laws

[299] Their common goal is to prepare and maintain the state's defense potential at an appropriate level in peacetime and to efficiently develop and effectively use this potential in the event of a threat or war

[300] In peacetime, appropriate infrastructure is created and maintained to ensure that state bodies can efficiently perform this task

[301] Each year, the Sejm adopts the amount of funds for state security and defense within the framework of the budget act, including for the maintenance and development of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland

[302] Parliament also adopts all other statutory regulations relating to defence and supervises their application

[303] The President of the Republic of Poland coordinates the development of the state's defense strategy and, as the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces, determines the main directions of their development

[304] The President supervises the course of defence preparations carried out under the direction of the Prime Minister by ministers, voivodes and other public administration bodies

[305] The President's executive body in this respect is the National Security Bureau

[306] The main defence tasks at the government level are assigned to the Minister of National Defence, the Minister of Internal Affairs and the Minister of Foreign Affairs

[307] In the event of a threat to security, a war-based system of state management is developed

[308] The highest decision-making centers in times of danger and war are: the President and the Government

[309] The Sejm appoints the Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of the Republic of Poland for a period of war, and when the Sejm is not in session, he is appointed by the President

[310] The Supreme Commander, reporting directly to the President, exercises strategic command of all armed forces

[311] The President determines the composition and tasks of the advisory bodies in times of peace, and the President's War Staff in times of war

[312] In times of threat and war, the Prime Minister continues to direct the work of the government, ministers, voivodes and other state administration bodies responsible for the functioning of all non-military structures of the state

[313] The specific task of the Prime Minister and the ministries subordinate to him is to organise support, supply and servicing of the armed forces by the civilian sector and to protect the population from the effects of warfare

[314] Voivodes are authorized to make decisions on defense matters that are binding on all administrative, economic and local government units operating within the voivodeship's defense system

[315] Conclusion Detailed implementation tasks arising from this document will be determined by the relevant state authorities and administration bodies as well as the management of organisations and institutions, in accordance with the scope of their competences in defence matters
