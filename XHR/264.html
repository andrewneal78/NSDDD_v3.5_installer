[693] Historical evolution Its origins date back to the 1950s, when the General Staffs of the military institutions studied the experiences of the operational use of joint forces during the Second World War, highlighting the need to have a permanent joint organization in order to establish unity of command in the permanent planning and conduction of operations with national power in any armed conflict

[694] Faced with this need, the military schools of the Armed Forces and the then Center for Advanced Military Studies (CAEM) presented a feasibility study to create a new organization where National Defense planning would be carried out in times of peace and war, involving the three military institutions in this task, designing its mission, organization, and operation, with the aim of optimizing the use of the Armed Forces' human and material resources to guarantee the independence, sovereignty, and integrity of the Republic's territory

[695] The Joint Command of the Armed Forces was created by Supreme Decree No. 002-GM/1 of 1957, which established: “The Joint Command of the Armed Forces reports directly to the President of the Republic and is the highest level of planning and coordination of the operations of the Army, Navy, and Air Force.”

[696] Its first President was Major General Manuel Cossio Cossio

[697] The changes that have occurred over the years in the international, regional, and national environments related to national security and defense have resulted in structural and organizational modifications within the Joint Command of the Armed Forces

[698] Until 1992, the organizational structure of the CCFFAA consisted of a Presidency, a Chief of Staff, and six Joint Staff divisions

[699] That year, due to the emergency situation in our country, its organic law was amended, creating the Internal Front Command (COFI), responsible for combating subversion and drug trafficking. Thus, the Joint Command now has two Sub-Commands, one for the Internal Front and one for the External Front

[700] At the same time, the work of "Civil Affairs and Human Rights" under the responsibility of the institutions was unified, creating the Seventh General Staff Division to coordinate with the International Committee of the Red Cross in Peru and related organizations

[701] In 2002, after the subversion had been strategically defeated, COFI was deactivated, returning to its previous organizational structure

[1100] The objective of the process is to achieve a State that serves its citizens, with effective channels for citizen participation, decentralized and deconcentrated, transparent in its management, fiscally balanced, and with qualified and adequately compensated public servants

[1101] It was in the context of state reform that the Comprehensive Armed Forces Restructuring Commission, in its final report approved by Supreme Resolution No. 038-DE/SG of April 20, 2002, stated that its work is part of the state's modernization process

[1102] This process began with the creation of the new Ministry of Defense, an executive branch responsible for designing, implementing, and supervising defense policy and fulfilling two fundamental roles: Constituting an instrument of democratic control of the armed forces, and Ensuring their proper functioning as a military force

[1103] In this regard, it was necessary to provide the Ministry with a modern and efficient organizational structure. This was achieved through Law No. 27860, the Ministry of Defense Law, of November 11, 2002, which created the Vice-Ministerial Offices of Administrative and Economic Affairs and Logistics and Personnel Affairs, responsible for guiding, executing, supervising, and evaluating sectoral policies in economic and budgetary matters, and in logistics and personnel matters, respectively

[1104] Likewise, it integrated the Joint Command of the Armed Forces and the General Directorate of Policy and Strategy as line bodies, replacing the Secretariat of National Defense, and the Army, the Navy and the Air Force as executive bodies

[1105] The National Agreement included the recommendations of the Commission for the Comprehensive Restructuring of the Armed Forces, incorporating them into the Twenty-Fifth State Policy: "", which indicates the guiding policies of the Defense Sector reform process

[1106] Finally, the Defense Sector reform includes the institutional reforms proposed by the Truth and National Reconciliation Commission, as well as the actions considered in the current government's 2004-2006 Roadmap

[1107] Under this framework, actions are being implemented to strengthen public confidence in the integrity of Defense Sector personnel and their ability to fulfill their constitutional mission

[1108] Vision of the Defense Sector To be a competitive, administratively, scientifically and technologically modern sector, with highly qualified human potential, solid moral values, and with Armed Forces capable of guaranteeing Comprehensive Security, which allows Peru to pursue National Development and exercise leadership at the regional level

[1109] The basic axes of the reform are: Safeguarding the institutionality of the Armed Forces and their service to democracy, Democratic control of the Defense Sector

[1215] This public good is subject to a cost-benefit assessment that takes into account society's desire to achieve development under conditions of security

[1216] The cost-benefit measure corresponds to an intangible investment, in this case, intended to obtain safety conditions

[1217] This is a social function—such as public health, education, and citizen security—that the State manages and finances to guarantee a climate of security for society as a necessary condition for development, primarily through attracting investment and increasing economic growth rates

[1218] The issue of the allocation of fiscal resources is a matter of ongoing debate, as state resources are insufficient to adequately meet the needs of these sectors

[1219] In this scenario, the State faces strong social pressure based on the need to increase public spending to prioritize unmet social demands

[1220] Another way to understand defense spending is to consider it as an "insurance policy" that protects the country from risks and threats to its security

[1221] Similar to the previous explanation, this policy allows for the generation of the security or climate of peace necessary to meet the goals and objectives of growth and development

[1222] In terms of national defense, no country, even the most developed, is in a position to acquire a comprehensive insurance policy that guarantees perfect peace. This is mainly because threats arise unexpectedly, and because human society develops amidst conflicts of varying levels and magnitude, compounded by risks and threats emanating from nature

[1223] This intangible public good benefits the entire population of the country, without any restrictions or distinctions. Therefore, its main characteristic is that the satisfaction of the need for security is directed equally to all members of the nation

[1224] In short, two fundamental economic principles are fulfilled: Security, as an intangible public good, is received by all citizens; it is a benefit from which no one is excluded

[1225] This intangible public good is permanent and its use by some does not affect the consumption of others

[1230] The Defense Sector budget includes both fixed and variable expenses. The former, also known as current expenses, are those allocated to salaries, pensions, and payment for essential public services; therefore, this expenditure is rigid and unavoidable

[1231] Variable expenses are those allocated to the fulfillment of the sector's specific tasks. In qualitative terms, they correspond to the necessary operating expenses of the sector. They represent the budgets to cover the needs arising from instruction and training, maintenance and renewal of resources and infrastructure, and the scheduled and unscheduled operations of the Armed Institutions

[1232] The emergence of new threats that threaten the security of the State and the population requires the Armed Forces to have the necessary equipment and capabilities to confront them in order to reduce or eliminate their risks

[1233] The expenses required by these activities correspond to variable expenses
