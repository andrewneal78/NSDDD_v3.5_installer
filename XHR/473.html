[630] Impacts of climate change on food security and water management Mexico's geographic location makes it extremely vulnerable to the impacts of climate change

[631] In the coming decades, the effects of this phenomenon will exert additional pressure on our ecosystems as a result of the consolidation of an urban development model that is increasingly demanding in terms of food, water, and energy resources

[632] The effects of climate change on Mexico are real: in recent decades, human activity has generated climatic alterations in our country, many of which have been accompanied by an increase in extreme weather events

[633] Between 1971 and 2008, the average annual temperature increased by 0.6°C in the country, while between the 1950s and 2000, the average sea level rose between 1.8 and 9.3 millimeters in certain coastal cities along the Gulf of Mexico

[634] Calculations by specialized national academic institutions also predict an average temperature increase of up to 4°C during this century and a decrease in rainfall of up to 11 percent, which could undoubtedly have a considerable impact on the country's food security

[635] Because agriculture and fishing in Mexico are highly dependent on atmospheric conditions, it is reasonable to expect that climate change will have an increasing impact on both activities in the coming decades

[636] Climate change has had a significant impact on the frequency and severity of the droughts and floods our country has recently experienced

[637] Rising temperatures will allow some crops to be harvested more frequently, increasing their yield due to higher carbon dioxide levels

[638] However, this situation is not sustainable: in the medium and long term, a scenario will arise in which the migration of crops and plant and animal species will be necessary to sustain production

[639] This latest phenomenon will affect the geographic distribution of agricultural production in Mexico and around the world, incorporating new regions into food production and drastically reducing production in others

[640] From this perspective, the possibility of displacement of people seeking better living conditions and new sources of livelihood cannot be ignored

[641] On the other hand, among the expected effects of climate change is the modification in rainfall and temperature patterns that determine the availability of water in Mexico: while the northern and central regions of the country only have access to 32 percent of the water available in our country, the southeast region concentrates 68 percent of the availability of the liquid

[642] In contrast, the first two regions are home to 77 percent of the population and generate 79 percent of GDP, while the second accounts for 23 percent of the population and contributes 21 percent of GDP

[643] These figures show that the pressure on the country's water resources is linked to the disparities in its development at the regional and local levels

[644] Maintaining a sustainable food supply to meet the needs of our population will be one of the greatest challenges facing the Mexican state in the immediate future

[645] The urbanization process that began in Mexico during the second half of the 20th century will continue over the coming decades, so the demand for foods with higher added value will be one of the constants that define the link between climate change and the sustainability of our development model

[646] It is necessary to recognize that the nature of the challenges Mexico will face in the coming years warrants taking proactive measures to prevent, adapt to, and mitigate the impacts of climate change on our development model and the quality of life of our population

[647] Failure to do so could lead to the consolidation of a scenario in which current vulnerabilities could become serious threats to the Mexican state in the future

[651] The transformation of the global energy landscape and energy security The world is currently undergoing an energy transition that will have significant geopolitical repercussions and could directly impact Mexico's ability to guarantee its energy security and the stability of its public finances

[652] In recent years, the introduction of new technological capabilities has enabled access to unconventional oil and natural gas reserves

[653] Countries previously dependent on energy imports are now emerging as leaders in the production of conventional and unconventional hydrocarbons

[654] Among them are the United States and Canada, Mexico's two trading partners in North America

[655] The International Energy Agency (IEA) estimates that by 2020, the United States will become a net exporter of natural gas and the world's largest exporter of oil

[656] In Mexico, primary energy production (65 percent crude oil) exceeds demand, resulting in an overall energy surplus

[657] However, the energy the country requires for the development of its economy comes from secondary energy sources (those that have been transformed and refined

[658] Production of these latter resources is lower than domestic consumption, so in recent years we have had to resort to increasingly larger imports of natural gas, LP gas, gasoline, and diesel

[659] Similarly, it is worth noting that between 2004 and 2012, national oil production decreased by nearly 835,000 barrels per day, representing a 25 percent drop (from 3.4 million barrels per day in the first to 2.5 million). The drop in production has not affected our public finances so far, because international oil prices tripled during the same period, from $31 in 2004 to around $100 today

[660] This circumstance makes our country highly vulnerable to the volatility of international oil and other hydrocarbon prices

[661] On the other hand, it is important to note that Mexico's total oil and natural gas reserves decreased by 23 percent between 1999 and 2012, so we currently have a production horizon of just a decade, in the case of proven reserves

[662] If these trends continue, Mexico could go from being a net energy exporter to a structurally energy-deficient country

[663] This circumstance would compromise our energy security by undermining our autonomy in the supply of energy inputs that play a central role in the country's social and economic development

The lack of availability of primary energy sources for electricity generation, as well as the length and age of the transmission lines, are factors that limit the efficiency of our transmission networks

[666] To achieve this goal, it is necessary to promote a sectoral policy that allows us to diversify the sources of our energy supply under a sustainable framework guided by the aspiration to maintain energy sufficiency in the short, medium, and long term
