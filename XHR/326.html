[1] Chapter 1 South African Defence Review 1998 Introduction Aim and Focus of the Defence Review

[2] In May 1996 the Minister of Defence presented to Parliament the White Paper on National Defence for the Republic of South Africa

[3] Parliament approved the document, with strong support expressed by all political parties

[4] The overarching theme of the White Paper is the transformation of defence policy and the South African National Defence Force (SANDF) in the light of the momentous political and strategic developments which have occurred at national, regional and international levels following the demise of the Cold War and the ending of apartheid

[5] At national level these developments include the establishment of a democratic government; the introduction of the Reconstruction and Development Programme (RDP) to address poverty and socio-economic inequality; the policy of national unity and reconciliation; and the adoption of a new Constitution which outlaws discrimination, enshrines fundamental human rights and lays the basis for democratic civil-military relations

[6] After two and a half decades of isolation, South Africa has been welcomed back into the international community and has joined a host of important regional and international bodies

[7] South Africa now engages in defence co-operation with a number of countries and participates in regional security arrangements under the auspices of the Southern African Development Community (SADC

[8] In the light of these developments and the integration of the former statutory and non-statutory forces, the challenge of transformation is substantial and complex

[9] The White Paper addresses this challenge at the level of broad policy

[10] It establishes a policy framework and the main principles of defence in our new democracy

[11] The White Paper also provides for a Defence Review, the aim of which is to elaborate on this policy framework through comprehensive long-range planning on such matters as posture, doctrine, force design, force levels, logistic support, armaments, equipment, human resources and funding

[77] Key concepts underpinning the Defence Review

[78] The following key concepts, drawn mainly from the White Paper on Defence, underpin the Defence Review

[79] Approach to Security The government has adopted a broad, holistic approach to security, recognising the various non-military dimensions of security and the distinction between the security of the state and the security of people

[80] The greatest threats to the security of the South African people are socio-economic problems like poverty and unemployment, and the high level of crime and violence

[81] Approach to Defence The government has adopted a narrow, conventional approach to defence

[82] The primary function of the SANDF is defence against external aggression

[83] The other functions are secondary

[84] The Defence Review therefore sets a policy vision for the size, structure, weaponry, equipment and funding requirements of the SANDF, mainly on the basis of its primary function

[85] Where the self-defence capabilities are insufficient for certain secondary functions - such as peace support operations and internal deployment in co-operation with the SAPS - additional capabilities may be required

[86] Arms Control and Disarmament South Africa is committed to the goals of arms control and disarmament at national, regional and international levels

[87] It has introduced new policy on conventional arms transfers, banned the manufacture and export of anti-personnel mines, and endorsed the international prohibitions on the manufacture, use and export of chemical, biological and nuclear weapons and related technology

[88] International Law on Armed Conflict The Constitution provides that South Africa will conduct its defence policy, and the SANDF will execute military operations, in accordance with international law on armed conflict

[89] International law prohibits the initiation of armed hostilities by states

[90] This does not preclude the exercise of the right of self-defence if South Africa is subject to an act of aggression

[91] The Review also provides that deterrence - which seeks to dissuade aggression by promising to inflict severe harm on an attacker - will be based on a counter-force rather than a counter-value doctrine

[92] In other words, deterrence threatens to destroy enemy forces and capabilities rather than civilian institutions and populations

[93] Defensive Posture The government does not currently, and will not in the future, have aggressive intentions towards any state

[94] It regards the use or threat of military force as a measure of last resort in the face of aggression when non-violent forms of conflict resolution have failed

[95] The emphasis lies on preventing conflict through co-operation with other states, particularly in Southern Africa

[96] In the light of the above and South Africa's commitment to abide by international law, the SANDF will have a primarily defensive orientation and posture

[97] This has implications for doctrine, training and force design

[98] For example, manifestly offensive armaments and systems have been excluded from the force design

[99] Other potentially offensive weapons systems which give undue strategic reach have been limited in number

[100] Defence spending and the Peace-time Force The Department of Defence accepts the national imperative of channelling the financial resources of the state to the RDP in order to alleviate socio-economic problems like poverty and unemployment

[101] In the light of this imperative, and in the absence of any foreseeable external military threat, the Defence Review seeks to establish an affordable peace-time force

[102] The peace-time force will comprise a relatively small regular component backed up by a sufficiently large part-time component

[103] These components should constitute a balanced and sustainable core force capable of dealing with small-scale defence contingencies which arise in the short-term

[104] Since the long-term future is inherently uncertain, it is also necessary to maintain the capabilities required to expand the core force if the security situation deteriorates significantly

[105] The SANDF's annual budget makes provision for the maintenance, development and preparation of the peace-time force

[106] As a general rule, it does not provide for operations due to the unpredictable nature therefor; special budgetary allocations therefore have to be made where the President authorises the employment of the SANDF in operations, including peace support operations and co-operation with the SAPS in the maintenance of law and order

[107] Currently, the SANDF does budget for its internal deployments because of the on-going nature of this task

[108] International Peace Operations With the advent of democracy, South Africa has moved from an isolationist position to one of engagement in international fora and participation in regional, continental and international peace initiatives

[109] This will lead, in time, to South Africa's involvement in multi-national peace support operations

[110] Regional Security South Africa's relations with neighbouring states have shifted from animosity to friendship and co-operation

[113] Ideally, the military should be utilised only in extreme situations, such as a state of emergency and against threats to the constitutional order

[137] Deterrence As noted above, the White Paper requires the maintenance of a defence capability which is sufficiently credible to deter potential aggressors

[138] Deterrence plays a crucial role in preventing armed conflict

[139] The maintenance of this capability should take account of the following

[146] Collective capabilities would also deter threats to the region as a whole

[147] Capabilities to neutralize possible threats should be at a level of readiness commensurate with the lead time for such threats to develop

[150] Deterrence will not only be pursued against potential short-term aggression through immediate force readiness

[158] International law does not preclude the exercise of the right of self-defence against external aggression

[160] Further, capabilities to reverse the effects of offensive actions are required

[161] The SANDF must be able halt, contain and roll back such actions

[162] Accordingly, appropriate offensive capabilities will be required at operational level

[200] It should take account, in particular, of the political, strategic and geographic situation in Southern Africa

[201] The current reality is that Southern Africa is a region of allies

[205] Certain contingencies may occur with little warning, but in the Southern African context other contingencies are likely to be of a longer term nature where early warning will be obtained

[216] Analysis of Contingencies Invasion

[217] Invasion is defined as a major attack aimed at occupying the South Africa or part of it, replacing the government by force and conquering its people

[218] This contingency is considered to be fairly remote since South Africa has no present or foreseeable enemies

[219] Further, a potential enemy does not stand to gain a major advantage from an invasion

[220] Although South Africa is relatively rich in resources, these do not have sufficient strategic importance given the resources available in other parts of the world

[221] The isolated geographic position of the Republic diminishes the possibility that it may be used by an external force as a springboard, base area or thoroughfare for military operations elsewhere

[222] The only exception is the remote possibility of a world war where a belligerent has the capability to attack shipping on the Cape sea route

[223] If an aggressor desires to launch an attack against a neighbouring state in Southern Africa, it would be far simpler to avoid South African territory

[224] The international situation would have to change considerably before an external power seeks to impose its ideology on South Africa through the use of force

[225] The probability of an invasion is therefore extremely low

[226] However, the impact of a successful invasion would be so catastrophic that it cannot be ignored totally as a contingency

[227] From an operational perspective, an invasion can take place over the landward border, from the sea or a combination of the two

[228] An invasion over the landward border would require a major military build-up to the north

[229] For a number of decades into the future, an operation of this kind could not be undertaken solely by an African state or alliance of African states

[230] It would require the participation of a major power

[231] It would also require coercion or invasion of one or more states to the north

[232] The terrain to the north would present the attacker with logistic problems and restrict its mobility

[233] These constraints provide a substantial warning period for defensive action

[234] An invasion from seaward would require substantial specialist resources and could be undertaken only with the involvement of a superpower or coalition of major powers

[235] Opportunities for strategic and deep interdiction in such a case would be more restricted

[236] Factors favouring South African defences are natural obstacles to the attacker and ocean conditions favourable to defensive counter-strikes

[237] As in the previous scenario, these factors provide a substantial warning period for defensive action

[238] Limited neutralising attacks

[239] In this scenario a third party, such as a major power, wants to prevent the Republic from interfering militarily in that party's designs in Southern Africa

[240] South Africa s approach to common security implies that it would threaten a major act of aggression against a SADC state

[241] The aggressor might therefore seek to neutralise South Africa s ability to project military power

[242] The targets of an attack might include air and naval transport assets, air and naval attack assets, and mobile ground forces

[243] Capabilities to defend these assets should thus be provided for in the peace-time force

[244] Such a scenario would probably entail UN, OAU and SADC condemnation of the aggressor, and would obviously be preceded by diplomatic efforts to avert a crisis

[245] The development into an attack on South Africa is therefore unlikely, although this scenario has a higher probability than the invasion contingency

[246] The impact would be much smaller, however, making the total risk lower than that of an invasion

[247] Nevertheless, force design should take note of this contingency

[248] Internal military threats to the constitutional order

[249] The SANDF may be employed to combat internal military threats to the constitutional order

[250] Such threats could take the form of civil war or general insurrection on a national or provincial scale

[251] Such threats might be supported by external agents or forces

[252] While the probability of this contingency is low, the impact would be considerable if it were not met successfully

[253] This is therefore a contingency for which the SANDF should be prepared

[254] Raids of lesser intensity may occur against the RSA for the purpose of coercion or castigation

[255] Coercion would aim to force South Africa to change its behaviour which is in conflict with another state's interests or goals, and castigation would be retaliation against South African actions regarded as offensive by such a state

[256] Raids could be launched by a major or smaller power

[257] They could take many forms, such as air raids by aircraft or missiles; landward raids by mobile or unconventional forces; and maritime raids by surface vessels, amphibious craft or clandestine forces

[258] The impact of such raids, although considerable, would be lower than that of the previous scenarios

[259] In contrast, the probability of such actions by irresponsible and irrational governments is higher than for the contingencies of invasions and limited neutralising attacks

[260] Since raids may also be launched by non-governmental, radical organisations, the probability must be considered as real

[261] The SANDF should therefore plan for these contingencies in designing the core force

[262] Blockades may be invoked to coerce South Africa to change its behaviour which is in conflict with the interests and goals of another power

[263] Blockades may take the form of interference in South Africa's sea lines of communications through mining of harbours or attacks on shipping within South Africa's maritime zone; landward blockades of trade routes to neighbouring states; or the enforcement of no-fly zones

[264] Given South Africa s dependence on trade, especially maritime trade, this could have an extremely negative effect on the well-being of the country and its people

[265] The impact would thus be considerable and should be taken into account in designing the peace-time force

[266] Attacks on embassies, ships and aircraft

[267] South Africa has a responsibility to protect its embassies, ships and aircraft outside its national borders

[268] The threat against these assets is mainly one of piracy and international terrorism

[269] Protection by host nations may not always be forthcoming or effective

[270] Although the impact of such contingencies is relatively low, the probability of their occurrence is relatively high

[271] The capability to protect and release captured embassies, ships and aircraft should therefore be provided for in the core force

[272] This capability must be at immediate readiness since the contingency may arise with little or no warning

[273] Law enforcement of marine resources and maritime zone

[274] Maritime law enforcement is not a primary defence task and is discussed in chapter

[275] However, a state may use military force to back up its exploitation of South African resources

[276] The impact of such a contingency might be significant although the probability can presently be considered as low

[277] This contingency requires capabilities similar to those needed to protect sea lines of communication

[278] The level of threat against South African islands (the Prince Edward Island group) and the impact if this threat materialises are such that no special or additional defence capabilities are required

[279] However, it should be noted that the South African sea areas around these islands are rich in potential food sources

[280] Characteristics of the Peace-Time Force The peace-time force should be designed and structured to meet the strategic realities of South and Southern Africa

[281] The following discussion considers the geographic, technological, organisational and operational factors in force design

[282] The features of the SANDF likely area of operations should obviously serve as a force design determinant

[283] Assuming that this area will be mainly South Africa, Southern Africa (for regional defence co-operation) and the surrounding seas, key factors to be taken into account include

[284] The large geographical area to be defended by relatively small forces

[285] Low infrastructure development which can be exacerbated by adverse weather conditions

[286] A scarcity of natural obstacles such as rivers and mountains

[287] Frequently adverse sea conditions

[288] Underwater ocean conditions which are favourable to submarines

[289] Access to and from South Africa largely dependent on sea and air transport

[290] A hostile health environment

[291] The peace-time force should thus have the following characteristics: 39.1 Mobility, including air and sea transport; self-contained ground forces suitable to rugged terrain and all-weather conditions; and ships with good sea-keeping capability

[292] Good underwater detection, location and attack capabilities

[293] Capabilities to ensure appropriate control of air and sea areas

[294] Operational medical support

[295] Technology The technology employed by the peace-time force should be appropriate and affordable

[296] Two main factors underpin this policy

[297] Given the lack of clarity on the possible enemies that the SANDF might have to counter, it would be imprudent to assume that a potential enemy will be unsophisticated

[298] With the present proliferation of armaments and military technology, it must be anticipated that any future enemy will have sophisticated weapons and systems

[299] South Africa's regional obligations require the maintenance of a relatively high level of technological sophistication

[300] The SANDF must be a balanced force

[301] A balanced force is one with the capabilities to meet a wide range of contingencies rather than one with equal numbers of all possible weapons systems

[302] Balance implies that the force should not be over-specialised to the extent that strategic gaps, which could be exploited by an enemy, are created

[303] This is especially important in view of the uncertainty regarding future threats

[304] Balance also means that force structure elements should complement each other since a specific capability may be rendered ineffective because of technological advances, surprise attack or other factors

[305] The aim is to achieve a balanced defence force rather than a balanced Army, Air Force and Navy

[306] For this reason, jointness between the arms of service will be promoted

[307] The SANDF must also be a flexible force which allows for expansion or reduction of force levels according to operational requirements and budgetary considerations

[308] Expansion Potential Maintaining a viable expansion potential is critical to the defence of South Africa

[309] The major determinants of expansion potential include the following: A realistic determination of warning lead times

[310] The procurement times of main equipment

[311] This depends on the nature of the specific equipment but is typically in the order of ten years from conceptualisation to operational availability

[312] This period may exceed warning lead times provided by intelligence

[313] The time required to train and prepare leader groups and troops

[314] This will influence the relative size of the full-time force and the part-time force

[315] The critical mass to which force structure elements can be reduced and below which no expansion would be possible

[316] Sustainability The SANDF force structure must be sustainable

[317] In other words, the SANDF must live within its means

[318] The aim is to have both a stable force and a stable defence budget commensurate with the threat situation and the lead times available for expansion

[319] Should the threat situation change, a new force design and funding level would have to be determined

[320] An appropriate balance should be found between operating expenditure and spending on capital renewal or upgrading

[321] Fluctuations in the capital budget should be equalised by long-term budgetary scheduling rather than through fluctuations in annual budgets

[322] Fluctuations and uncertainty in planning result in substantial fruitless expenditure

[323] A long-term view is necessary and multi-year budgetary guidelines are highly desirable

[324] Capabilities Required by the Peace-Time Force In the light of the previous discussion, it is possible to list the specific capabilities required by the peace-time force

[325] Defence intelligence capability

[326] Intelligence is an overarching capability required by the DoD. Strategic intelligence is the basis for force design, as well as early warning to ensure maximum time for expansion and defence preparation

[327] Good tactical intelligence is a force multiplier during operations

[328] Landward defence

[329] Capabilities to counter attacks over land should be mobile and largely self-contained. Air defence

[330] Air defence, including fighter-based capabilities and localised anti-aircraft capabilities, is needed to counter air attacks

[331] Maritime defence

[332] Maritime defence capabilities to counter attack from seaward and on shipping should include mine clearance and the ability to act against air, surface and submarine attacks

[333] Biological and chemical defence

[334] Subject to the provisions of the chemical and biological weapons conventions, the SANDF should maintain the capability to detect the use of chemical and biological weapons in a given area, to protect its personnel against such weapons, and to decontaminate forces and equipment which have been under such attack

[335] Interdiction capabilities

[336] Once armed hostilities have begun, the SANDF should have the capability to interdict the battlefield

[337] Counter-offensive capabilities

[338] On halting aggression, the SANDF should have the capability to drive the enemy from own or friendly territory

[339] Command and control

[340] This is critical to the effective execution of the self-defence function at all levels

[341] The diversity of military tasks performed by the different combat, combat support and supporting elements of the SANDF requires both single service and joint command and control capabilities

[342] Great emphasis should be placed on electronic warfare and command information systems

[343] Force preparation

[344] The SANDF must be able to prepare its forces continuously for self-defence

[345] Force preparation should concentrate on two categories: i) formal training of personnel at all ranks for conventional operations; and ii) training and exercising force structure elements at arms of service level, as well as joint and combined operations

[346] Support capabilities

[347] The SANDF must maintain effective support capabilities

[348] These include operational medical support; operational logistics support and technology development; land, air and sea transport; personnel and chaplaincy services to maintain the morale of soldiers engaged in operations; and legal support to commanders during operations

[349] In summary, the defence capability of the SANDF should be cost-effective; sustainable; capable of appropriate expansion; technologically appropriate; balanced and flexible; and highly motivated and prepared

[350] Chapter 4 Regional Security Co-operation Policy Framework After decades of isolation, South Africa has been welcomed back into the international community and has joined or returned to a host of regional and international organisations

[351] The most important of these are the United Nations, the Organisation of African Unity (OAU) and the Southern African Development Community (SADC

[352] South Africa has also entered into a number of international, regional and bilateral or trilateral defence agreements

[353] These generally entail reciprocal obligations and an undertaking to engage in some form of co-operation

[354] The most significant strategic development over the past few years is South Africa's new status in Southern Africa, previously an arena of intense conflict

[355] With the election of a democratic government in April 1994, relations with neighbouring states changed from suspicion and animosity to friendship and co-operation

[356] For political, strategic and geographic reasons, the government regards security and defence co-operation in Southern Africa as a priority

[357] Since the end of the Cold War the region has undergone substantial change

[358] Considerable progress has been made towards the resolution of internal conflicts, the establishment of democracy, and disarmament

[359] The prospects for regional peace and stability are greater today than at any other time in recent decades

[360] Nevertheless, much of the sub-continent is stricken by underdevelopment and the attendant problems of poverty, illiteracy and unemployment

[361] There are large numbers of refugees and displaced people; an acute debt crisis; and disease and environmental degradation

[362] Certain states remain politically volatile

[363] The worst case scenario, as experienced intensely in Angola and Mozambique, is civil war

[364] These phenomena are not confined to national borders

[365] They impact negatively on neighbouring states in the form of a range of non-military threats: environmental destruction; the spread of disease; the burden of refugees; and cross-border trafficking in drugs, stolen goods and small arms

[366] Following trends in other parts of the world, South Africa is committed to the development of a common security approach in Southern Africa

[367] In essence, this will entail the SADC states shaping their political, security and defence policies in co-operation with each other

[368] Common security arrangements have many advantages

[369] They can facilitate sharing of information and resources; early warning of potential crises; joint problem-solving; implementing confidence- and security-building measures (CSBMs); negotiating security agreements; and resolving inter-state conflict through peaceful means

[370] Certain of these endeavours are the responsibility of the Department of Foreign Affairs

[371] Chief amongst them is the prevention, management and settlement of conflict through negotiation and mediation

[372] Other types of activity, some of which are already under way, will be undertaken by the Department of Defence (DOD

[373] Regional defence co-operation can be promoted in the fields of logistics; training and education; aviation; protection of marine resources; intelligence; combined exercises; secondment of personnel; goodwill visits; and the development of combined doctrines and operational procedures

[374] The government may be called on by neighbouring countries to play a number of supportive roles

[375] For example, the SANDF can provide assistance with respect to disaster relief; controlling cross-border trafficking in small arms; clearing minefields; training; field engineering; medical support; and maintaining and upgrading weaponry and equipment

[376] The DOD will pursue the implementation of CSBMs

[377] These are measures which provide greater transparency in military matters in order to alleviate mistrust, prevent misunderstandings from developing into crises, and thereby promote collective confidence and stability

[378] Appropriate CSBMs might include the following: Annual consultation and exchange of information on defence budgets, force structure, modernisation plans and troop deployment

[379] Consideration of national threat perceptions which inform force structure and modernisation plans

[380] The establishment of a regional arms register which records imports, exports, production and holdings of conventional arms and light weaponry

[381] Notification and on-site observation of military exercises and other specified activities

[382] Verification procedures in respect of the above

[383] A communications network and a crisis hotline

[384] Procedures for dealing with unusual or unscheduled military incidents

[385] Peace Support Operations Situations may arise in Southern Africa where inter- or intra-state conflict poses a threat to peace and stability in the region as a whole

[386] If political efforts to resolve the conflict are unsuccessful, it may become necessary to deploy the SANDF in multi-national peace support operations

[387] South Africa shares the view of many of its neighbours that the creation of a standing peacekeeping force in the region is not required or feasible

[388] The SADC countries are more likely to engage in peace support operations on an ad-hoc basis if the need arises

[389] Nevertheless, the SADC states are committed to regional co-operation in preparing for peace support operations

[390] It may therefore be worthwhile to establish a small peace support operations centre, under the auspices of regional defence structures, to develop and co-ordinate planning, training, logistics, communication and field liaison teams for multi-national forces

[391] Posture South Africa has a common destiny with Southern Africa

[392] Domestic peace and stability will not be achieved in a context of regional instability and poverty

[393] It is therefore in South Africa's long-term security interests to pursue mutually beneficial relations with the other SADC states and to promote reconstruction and development throughout the region

[394] Given South Africa s relative military strength on the sub-continent, the adoption of a defensive and non-threatening posture would contribute to building confidence and positive relationships

[395] Reductions in force levels and weapons holdings might stimulate a broader process of regional disarmament

[396] However, force reductions should be kept within reasonable proportions if South Africa is to play an active support role in the region

[397] South Africa will support the conclusion of multi-lateral treaties on disarmament, arms control and foreign military involvement in the region

[398] The most important agreement would be a non-aggression pact which endorses the prohibition in international law regarding the threat or use of force

[399] Regional Structures South Africa is a signatory to the SADC Treaty and participates fully in all the structures of the organisation

[400] These include the following: 18.1 The Summit, comprising Heads of State and Government

[401] The Organ on Politics, Defence and Security, comprising the ministers responsible for foreign affairs and defence, which reports to the Summit

[402] The Inter-State Defence and Security Committee (ISDSC), comprising the ministers responsible for defence, policing and intelligence, which reports to the Organ

[403] The ISDSC has defence, public security and state security sub-committees

[404] The main functions of the defence sub-committee of the ISDSC are as follows: to review and share experiences on the military-security situation in member states; to explore areas for multi-lateral defence co-operation and the practical implementation thereof; and to exchange views and propose mechanisms for the prevention, management and resolution of conflict in Africa in general and in Southern Africa in particular

[405] The defence sub-committee has sub-sub-committees on specialist areas, including operations; intelligence; personnel development; logistics; maritime and aviation cooperation; and chaplains, legal and medical associations

[406] International Peace Support Operations Policy Framework As a fully fledged member of the international community, South Africa will fulfil its responsibility to participate in international peace support operations

[407] In the short-term, however, such participation will be regarded with caution since the political and military dynamics of these operations are new to South Africa and the DoD

[408] In order to prepare for peace support operations, the DoD is investigating the development of appropriate doctrines, operational procedures and training programmes in co-operation with foreign partners

[409] Public awareness and parliamentary debate on the complexities and different types of peace support operations are equally important

[410] South Africa will only become involved in specific peace support operations if the following conditions are met: There should be parliamentary approval and public support

[411] This requires an appreciation of the associated costs and risks, including the financial costs and the risk to military personnel

[412] The operation should be authorised by the UN Security Council

[413] Operations in Southern Africa should be sanctioned by SADC and undertaken with other SADC states

[414] Similarly, operations in Africa should be sanctioned by the OAU

[415] The operation should have a clear mandate, mission and objectives

[416] There should be realistic criteria for terminating the operation

[417] Involvement in peace support operations will not be limited to the deployment of troops

[418] It could also take the form of providing equipment, logistical support, humanitarian aid, engineering services, communications systems and medical personnel and facilities

[419] The acquisition and maintenance of military equipment will take account of the particular requirements of peace support operations

[420] Preventive diplomacy involves diplomatic action taken in advance of a predicted crisis to prevent or limit violence

[421] In particularly tense situations, preventive deployment of military forces may support such action

[422] The deployment may be aimed at deterring violence; assisting local authorities to protect threatened minorities; securing and maintaining essential services; and ensuring access to an area of operation

[423] Peacemaking is a process of diplomacy, mediation, negotiation or other form of peaceful settlement of conflict that seeks to end disputes and resolve the issues which give rise to conflict

[424] Military forces may be required to support this process

[425] Peace building consists of post-conflict actions that strengthen and rebuild civil infrastructures and institutions in order to provide a lasting peace

[426] Military forces may assist in restoring the civil authority; rebuilding physical infrastructures; conducting elections; demobilising former belligerents; and collecting and controlling small arms

[427] Peacekeeping involves military or para-military operations undertaken with the consent of the disputant parties

[428] The operations aim to monitor and facilitate the implementation of an existing peace or truce agreement, and to support diplomatic efforts to reach a long-term settlement

[429] Peacekeeping activities include observation, monitoring and supervision of the provisions of peace agreements

[430] These typically relate to cease-fires; arms control; withdrawal and demobilisation of combatants; denial or freedom of movement; and elections

[431] Peace enforcement is the application or threat of military force, authorised by the UN Security Council, to compel compliance with Security Council resolutions

[432] The aims are to maintain or restore peace and to support diplomatic efforts to reach a long-term settlement

[433] Unlike other peace support operations, peace enforcement may include combat action

[434] Military missions may include the restoration and maintenance of order and stability; guarantee or denial of movement; enforcement of sanctions; establishment and supervision of protected zones; and forcible separation of belligerent parties

[435] Humanitarian or relief activities may constitute a discreet operation or be part of the operations described above

[436] Such activities, in which military support may be required, include provision of humanitarian aid, disaster relief, health care and assistance to refugees and displaced persons

[437] It is evident from the preceding paragraphs that peace support operations may require the involvement of medical, communications, engineering, logistical, administrative, transport, combat and other components of armed forces

[438] Authority and Decision Making As a rule, the UN Security Council decides on the establishment of a peace support operation and is responsible for extending or terminating the mission

[439] Peace enforcement operations which take place under the auspices of the OAU or SADC require prior endorsement by the UN Security Council

[440] A resolution of the Security Council authorises the operation and determines the mandate

[441] The resolution typically covers the terms of reference for the mission; the appointment of the Force Commander; force levels and contributing countries; and administrative, logistical and budgetary arrangements

[442] The overall direction of the operation is in the hands of the Secretary-General who acts on behalf of, and reports to, the Security Council

[443] The Secretary-General delegates the daily operational management to a Head of Mission, but will usually retain direct control over policy matters and major decision-making

[444] The Secretary General is assisted in the exercise of these functions by a secretariat of political and military advisers in the Office of the Under-Secretary-General for Special Political Affairs (OUSGSPA

[445] The Force Commander, who may or may not be the Head of Mission, is responsible to the Secretary General for the efficient operation and administration of the multi-national force

[446] He/she exercises this responsibility through OUSGSPA which actually runs the mission

[447] The Force Commander exercises operational control of the national forces of participating countries

[448] He/she gives direct orders for execution by these forces, without resort to the contributing countries

[449] The contingents execute his/her orders without clearance from their governments

[450] The Commander does not maintain formal contact with contributing countries

[451] Such contacts are maintained by the Secretary General through OUSGSPA

[452] National commanders are directly responsible and answerable to the Force Commander for the effective operational and administrative functioning of their units

[453] Personnel serving in these units remain under the command of their national commanders

[454] Where South Africa is requested to participate in a peace support operation, the following will apply

[455] The request will be routed to the Department of Foreign Affairs

[456] That Department will liaise closely with the National Intelligence Co-ordinating Committee and the Department of Defence, through the Minister of Defence, with regard to the nature of South Africa's potential involvement

[457] Additional agreements relating to the determination of the mission and objectives of the operation will also be negotiated by the Department of Foreign Affairs in consultation with the Department of Defence

[458] The Departments of Foreign Affairs and Defence will advise Cabinet on the matters referred to above

[459] Cabinet is responsible for final decisions

[460] In terms of the Constitution, the employment of the SANDF in peace support operations requires the authorisation of the President

[461] In terms of the Constitution, Parliament is empowered to review the President's decision regarding such employment

[462] Departmental and Operational Considerations The following issues should be addressed before committing SANDF forces to a peace support operation: There must be a realistic appreciation of the extent of the SANDF s involvement in the light of its capabilities and other commitments

[463] Liaison channels must exist with UN peacekeeping structures

[464] A clear command and control system must be established, and rules of engagement must be determined

[465] Channels of finance must be opened both with the UN and between the relevant South African government departments, and financial arrangements must be finalised in the contract

[466] Standardisation and compatibility must be established with other peacekeeping forces regarding drills; telecommunications; language; and doctrine and procedures for combined operations

[467] A time scale for the period of involvement must be established, and a disengagement plan for own troops must be in place

[468] South African forces must be supported logistically from own resources for at least 28 weeks, after which UN support should be available

[469] Planning and execution of operations must be compatible with UN operating procedures

[470] Forces must be familiar with the conditions prevailing in the area of operations

[471] South African Military Health Services (SAMHS) support for own troops must be in place

[472] The SANDF has particular skills and expertise in communications, field engineering (including mine-clearing), medical, and command and control functions which are relevant to peace support operations

[473] Consideration should be given to designating these areas as the preferred mode of participation in the short- to medium-term

[474] Decisions in this regard will depend on the nature of the operation and the country of deployment

[475] The following considerations must be taken into account with regard to the area of operations: the potential political risk to South Africa if it deploys forces in neighbouring countries; and the need for foreign assistance should forces be deployed beyond the operational reach of the SANDF

[476] The Defence Act currently provides that SANDF personnel may only be compelled to serve outside the borders of the RSA "in time of war... against the enemy

[477] The legal implications of deploying personnel in peace support operations will therefore have to be addressed in new defence legislation

[478] Peace support operations are fundamentally different from traditional approaches to fighting wars

[479] The differences are manifest in the mission, objectives and doctrine of these operations, and in the role and tasks of the forces involved therein

[480] Offensive action by these forces is usually prohibited, although it may receive limited expression in peace enforcement

[481] The skills, attitudes and expectations of SANDF personnel therefore need to be addressed through thorough training prior to deployment

[482] They must be briefed properly on the political, cultural, linguistic and geographic features of the country of deployment

[483] Failure to understand and respect the nuances of local culture and politics will undermine the efficacy and perceived impartiality of the peacekeepers

[484] All personnel involved in the operation must know and understand the rules of engagement

[485] If possible, they should have an opportunity to practise the rules through simulation exercises prior to deployment

[486] Personnel also require training in mediation, negotiation and consensus-building skills

[487] They should be psychologically prepared to back down and lose face in certain circumstances

[488] They should appreciate the importance of impartiality, patience, tolerance and political sensitivity

[489] Training should be based on typical peace support operation tasks

[490] These include cease-fire monitoring and maintenance; checkpoint operations; medical assistance to the civilian population; crowd control at food distribution points; apprehension of criminals; and performing certain of the responsibilities of the civil authority if it has collapsed

[491] Military forces involved in peace operations should comply strictly with international humanitarian law, and may be mandated to oversee compliance with this law by the belligerent parties

[492] It is therefore essential that SANDF personnel deployed in such operations are familiar with the relevant provisions of the law and with the application thereof in situations of conflict

[493] Combined training should be undertaken with the forces of countries with which South Africa is likely to be involved in peace support operations

[494] In terms of the mandate of the SANDF, participation in international peace support operations is a secondary function

[495] The SANDF will therefore not normally create special structures for such operations, but will rather provide for them within the primary structure

[496] More specifically, peacekeeping and peace enforcement are secondary functions of the Army is Rapid Deployment Ground Force which includes mechanised, parachute and motorised forces

[497] Two motorised infantry battalion groups will be prepared for peacekeeping operations on a relay basis

[498] The capabilities of the mechanised and parachute forces will be used in peace enforcement operations if required

[499] Similarly, the Air Force, Navy and SA Military Health Services will not provide dedicated force components for peace support operations but will ensure that the components which might be deployed in these operations are equipped and trained appropriately

[500] Chapter 6 Co-operation with the SA Police Service Policy Framework South Africa is characterised by a high level of crime and criminal violence which affects all sectors of society and is exacerbated by a proliferation of light weapons in private hands

[501] Violence in certain parts of the country remains unacceptably high and poses a serious threat to public order and the security of citizens

[502] The government is tackling this problem through efforts to strengthen the criminal justice system, community policing and a National Crime Prevention Strategy

[503] Long-term solutions include upgrading the conditions of impoverished communities through the RDP, and supporting economic growth and development in Southern Africa

[504] Public order policing is primarily the responsibility of the SAPS

[505] Given the relative shortage of police personnel, a large number of troops are currently deployed to assist them in the maintenance of law and order

[506] Despite public calls for greater military involvement in combatting crime, the government regards this tendency as undesirable

[507] The history of South Africa and many other countries indicates that it is inappropriate to utilise armed forces in a policing role on a permanent or semi-permanent basis

[508] This perspective is based on the following: Armed forces are not trained, orientated or equipped for deployment against civilians

[509] They are typically geared to employ maximum force against an external military aggressor

[510] Ongoing employment in a law and order function may lead to the defence force becoming politicised and increasingly involved in non-military activities

[511] Such employment may also undermine the image and legitimacy of the defence force amongst sections of the population

[512] Internal deployment places a substantial burden on the defence budget

[513] It also has a disruptive effect on the economy when large numbers of part-time soldiers are called-up

[514] Efforts to apply military solutions to political problems are inherently limited and may undermine resolution of these problems

[515] The Departments of Defence and Safety and Security are formulating plans for the withdrawal of the SANDF from a policing role

[516] The SANDF would then be deployed only in exceptional circumstances, such as a state of emergency or national defence or a breakdown of public order beyond the capacity of the SAPS

[517] Until this goal is attained, the employment of the SANDF in co-operation with the police will continue

[518] Such employment will be authorised by the President, regulated by legislation and subject to the Bill of Rights and parliamentary approval and safeguards

[519] Officers and other ranks deployed in internal operations will be equipped and trained appropriately

[520] To ensure effective deployment and maximum co-operation with the SAPS, the following steps should be taken: the creation of formal liaison committees; analysis and determination of the role, method of employment and duties of the SANDF; the establishment of a clear chain of command and control, a code of conduct and appropriate rules of engagement; agreement on the duration of the SANDF deployment; and a thorough review of the deployment if the objective of the mission is not achieved within the agreed time-scale

[521] The Department of Defence will establish mechanisms to facilitate communication and liaison between the SANDF and provincial and local authorities regarding the internal deployment of troops

[522] However, these authorities will have no decision-making powers over such deployment

[523] Command of the SANDF remains centralised at national level

[524] The SANDF is currently employed in border protection in co-operation with the police

[525] Those activities which relate to combatting crime and controlling the flow of illegal immigrants into South Africa are more properly the responsibility of the SAPS

[526] This matter is similarly under review by various government departments in the context of the National Crime Prevention Strategy

[527] Strategic Approach The internal employment of the SANDF will take place within the framework of the National Crime Prevention Strategy

[528] This will ensure effective co-ordination, an integrated approach and synergy of effort

[529] In implementing this strategy, a clear distinction between the roles of the military and the police will be maintained

[530] Types of activity

[531] The defence force will not perform police functions such as criminal investigation, arresting suspects, preparing dockets and involvement in the criminal justice system

[532] The SANDF does not have normal powers of arrest, detention and search and seizure; these are legally the preserve of the SAPS

[533] Certain exceptions may be made by government regulation in a state of emergency

[534] Geographic focus

[535] SANDF employment should be concentrated in rural and border areas since these coincide with the military s conventional theatre of operations

[536] As an interim measure, an expansion of the military's role in border protection will free police resources for combatting routine and priority crimes

[537] Types of crime

[538] The SANDF should not be involved in combatting routine crime but should rather focus on i) crimes which constitute a threat to the constitutional order; ii) crimes where military employment is consistent with international practice; and iii) serious crime

[539] These categories are described below

[540] Crimes which constitute a threat to the constitutional order might include extremist political groupings which are armed; the expansion of paramilitary forces; large scale political instability; mass civil disobedience; and a complete breakdown of law and order

[541] Crime Combatting in Accordance with International Practice It is acceptable internationally to deploy armed forces to counter certain types of crime which have serious socio-economic, security and/or environmental implications

[542] The SA Army is deployed, in co-operation with the SAPS and other state departments, in border protection

[543] This is intended to prevent illegal immigration and the smuggling of drugs and weapons across landward borders

[544] Smuggling, illegal entry, plunder of marine resources and other crimes occur along the coast of the Republic

[545] The Navy is deployed in co-operation with the SAPS and the departments of environmental affairs, home affairs and revenue services in patrolling the coast to prevent such activities

[546] The Air Force has air space surveillance and control capabilities which assist in the protection of the country s air space against smuggling and illegal entry

[547] The SA Military Health Services provides medical support to the SANDF and SAPS during crime prevention operations

[548] Serious Crime The SANDF can contribute to combatting serious crime such as attacks on farms; stock theft by armed groups; illegal trafficking in drugs, arms and ammunition; car hijackings; and taxi violence

[549] Such SANDF activities include monitoring and surveillance; providing air transport support to the SAPS; providing logistic and personnel support to police engaged in roadblocks, cordons and vehicle and foot patrols

[550] The SANDF also provides reaction force back-up to the police where this is required

[551] Cost-Effective Utilisation of Resources The SANDF has a range of capabilities which can be utilised to assist the police

[552] In the interests of cost-effectiveness, these assets are put at the disposal of the police and therefore need not be duplicated by the SAPS

[553] Relevant capabilities include fixed wing and helicopter transport; specialised aerial and naval reconnaissance; naval transport; air space control; operational medical support; communications; and logistic support

[554] Force Design and Force Levels Employment in co-operation with the SAPS is not a primary factor in determining force design

[555] The SANDF relies mainly on its core defence capabilities to prepare and equip for the above tasks

[556] The extent of military deployment is determined by the security situation and is reviewed periodically

[557] Where core defence capabilities are insufficient, additional personnel are required

[558] In 1997 approximately 6000 soldiers were deployed on a daily basis in co-operation with the police

[559] Chapter 7 Non-Military Tasks Policy Framework The SANDF is designed and equipped chiefly to fulfil its primary mission of defence against aggression

[560] Nevertheless, it has personnel, skills and resources which are utilised for various non-military tasks, normally in support of the civil authority

[561] They are regarded as secondary functions and are not a major factor in the determination of force design and force levels

[562] The SANDF does not budget for the execution of these functions since they derive from existing defence capabilities

[563] In some cases the Department of Defence claims from the users the cost of activities which are undertaken on a regular basis

[564] There are exceptions where provision of a service by the SANDF is the most cost-effective option for the state

[565] This is normally the case where an extension to existing SANDF capabilities is preferable to creating the same capabilities in another department

[566] Examples are VIP air transport, hydrographic services and the national codification bureau

[567] Government regards inappropriate use of armed forces in non-military activities as economically inefficient and politically unwise Such activities are appropriate in extreme situations and where they derive from capabilities which are unique to the Defence Force

[568] More specifically, government is disinclined to employ the SANDF in socio-economic development

[569] Such employment blurs the distinction between the military and civilian spheres; it contributes to the militarisation of civil society; and it undermines the preparedness of the force with respect to its primary function

[570] However, there may be exceptional circumstances which mitigate against the delivery of services by line function departments

[637] Force Design Logic Notwithstanding this extensive list of tasks, the White Paper provides that force design should revolve chiefly around the SANDF's primary function of defence against military aggression

[638] While the process of force design involves considerable military technical detail, the design is determined largely by decisions regarding three parameters: the level of defence required; the approved defence posture; and the defence budget

[639] The level of defence is reflected in the definition, scale and intensity of military operations associated with the defence contingencies and tasks discussed in Chapter 3 of this report

[640] The size and configuration of the defence capabilities are in turn strongly influenced by the choice of defence posture, as elaborated in Chapter

[641] The process is further constrained by budgetary guidelines and the costs associated with particular capabilities

[642] In summary, the force design of the SANDF is needs driven, but cost constrained

[643] While the process is intended to produce a force design representing the best defence value for money within the selected parameters, it may not be possible to ensure the capabilities for a required level of defence within a restrictive budget

[644] The resulting imbalance or inadequacy in defence capability represents a risk to the country, which must either be accepted or reduced by other means

[645] Throughout the process there is thus an interplay of political judgement and military expertise

[646] Force Design Options The Department of Defence developed various force design options, reflecting different permutations of level of defence, defence posture and cost, for public consideration during the consultative conferences of the Defence Review

[647] Four options were presented to the parliamentary defence committees and cabinet and were discussed in detail

[648] Option One, as set out in this report, was approved subject to available finances

[649] Status of a Force Design The approval of a force design by the parliamentary defence committee, Cabinet or Parliament does not constitute blanket approval for all implied capital projects or an immutable contract in terms of the exact numbers and types of equipment

[650] At best, it constitutes approval in principal for the maintenance of the specified capabilities at an approximate level

[651] There are several reasons for this

[652] The affordability of the force design, which is central to its implementation, is subject to certain assumptions and qualifications

[653] The long-term sustainability of the design requires a continuous investment in the periodic upgrading and replacement of equipment

[654] The costing of the design thus incorporates annualised upgrade and replacement costs over the life span of the equipment

[655] This is based on realistic assumptions, under present conditions, of the anticipated life span and cost of such equipment

[656] Since the life span of major equipment is measured in decades and many replacement projects will commence many years hence, it is impossible to predict with absolute accuracy the actual annualised cost of equipment

[657] The process further assumes that the current imbalance in the so-called tooth-to-tail ratio of the SANDF will be corrected by the transformation process, thus releasing a greater portion of the budget for combat capabilities

[658] The ratio on which the calculations are based is an estimate of the anticipated result of transformation

[659] The numbers and costs of equipment in future replacement projects will also be affected by technological developments which may improve future equipment capabilities

[660] On the other hand, less capable equipment may be available at a lower unit cost

[661] During each procurement project a trade-off will thus have to be made between the required overall capability, the equipment available to the SANDF, cost, and available funds

[662] These details can only be determined under the conditions prevailing at the time, and all major acquisition projects will thus be subject to the processes and levels of approval provided for in the White Paper on Defence

[663] The processes and assumptions on which the force design is based will be reviewed in successive planning cycles

[664] This is bound to produce new insights that will require a periodic revision of the force design

[665] The planning assumptions also reflect perceptions of the geopolitical environment, which will inevitably change

[666] While no major discontinuities are anticipated, even gradual, relatively orderly change will require adjustments to defence planning on a continuous basis

[667] The force design thus reflects a dynamic environment and is itself dynamic

[668] It represents the best present indication of the type and scale of defence capabilities that South Africa should establish and maintain, but its future orientation makes deviations inevitable

[669] The transformation process in particular will impact directly on the force structure required to support the force design: force structure is subject to the Department's transformation project which will be discussed in detail in the course of the Defence Review process

[1674] Constitutional and Policy Framework The Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, read with the 1993 Constitution in terms of Schedule 6 of the new Constitution, provides

[1678] The White Paper on Defence states that, for political, strategic and economic reasons, the SANDF will be an all-volunteer force

[1679] It will comprise a relatively small Full-Time Component (FTC), including a civilian component, which is backed by a sufficiently large PTC

[1680] A basic structure of this nature is extremely cost-effective and allows for flexibility in force levels according to the internal and external security environment

[1681] The 'one force concept' is intended to ensure that the FTC and the PTC constitute an integrated defence force which is capable of defending South Africa

[1682] The FTC and the PTC together provide the SANDF’s core capabilities which can be expanded if required by calling into service additional trained part-time volunteers

[1683] The 'one force concept' provides for flexible utilisation of human resources and reduces the necessity to maintain a large and un-affordable full-time component

[1684] The essence of the concept is that all SANDF personnel enjoy fair and equitable treatment regardless of whether they are full-time or part-time members, and that they are all expected to render the same high standard of service notwithstanding the differences in the dynamics, functioning and requirements of the two components

[1685] The concept will enable South Africa to make best use of available defence funding by maintaining a force in which the FTC is optimally sized and equipped, and backed up by a PTC able to fulfil those tasks which do not require a full-time capability

[1686] In order to achieve the 'one force concept’, the future SANDF should be based on the principle of fairness and equity for all its members, whether from the full-time or part-time components, with due recognition of the different dynamics, functioning and requirements of the two components, as well as the inherent differences of service between the Arms of Service

[1687] A substantial, representative, trained, funded and equipped PTC is a national asset because it is a guarantee of national sovereignty, costs very little to maintain, and benefits the country by its very existence
