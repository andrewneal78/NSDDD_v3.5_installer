[36] CHAPTER ONE 1.0 INTRODUCTION Following the end of the decade-long civil conflict in Sierra Leone in 2002, the first-ever Security Sector Reform (SSR) was conducted between 2003 and

[37] It focused on building the capacity of the security sector to enable it to provide the enabling environment for post-conflict stabilisation, ensure the wielding of state governance all over the country and to respond to emerging security threats in the face of troops withdrawal by the United Nations Mission in Sierra Leone (UNAMSIL

[38] The second SSR was conducted between 2012 and 2014 to appraise the requirements and performance of the security sector, with a view to identifying gaps and developing a transformation strategy that will enhance its capability to respond to existing and emerging security threats

[39] The development of this National Security Policy and Strategy (NSPS) is in line with Section 18 (b)(v) of the National Security and Central Intelligence Act 2002 (now repealed and replaced by the National Security and Central Intelligence Act, 2023) which provides for the National Security Coordinator and Head of the Office of National Security to be ‘responsible for the preparation and maintenance of the National Security Policy’

[40] National Security Vision The national security vision for Sierra Leone is to create a positive environment in which the following fundamental values and principles of state policies, as enshrined in the Constitution of Sierra Leone, shall be ensured: sovereignty, political independence, territorial integrity, human rights, rule of law, national cohesion, stable economic development, social justice, security, adherence to acceptable cultural values and tradition, health and environmental protection and strengthening of international, regional and sub-regional security, peace and socio-economic cooperation

[41] Strategic Objectives The following are the objectives of the NSP&S of Sierra Leone: i. to develop a security policy geared towards the creation of a coordinated, democratic and accountable approach to national security

[55] Current National Security Outlook/Landscape Sierra Leone’s National Security landscape continues to evolve with the shift to human security becoming entrenched

[56] Threats relating to military, paramilitary and subversion have considerably reduced

[247] Policy Objectives The primary objectives of the Policy are to: i. establish a holistic approach to security through a well-developed national security architecture ii

[248] strengthen the national security co-ordination mechanism, and iii. identify the key security challenges confronting Sierra Leone and mechanisms to address them

[249] Rationale for the Policy The NSP will provide a national consensus for defining Sierra Leone’s interests and core values as well as identifying the threats and opportunities likely to facilitate or thwart the realization of these interests and values

[250] The policy is also geared towards creating the platform for addressing strategic national objectives and core aspirations (human security, good governance and development

[251] At the very outset, it will define the mechanism established by the Government of Sierra Leone that must be strictly adhered to, in order to deliver effective, professional, non-partisan and accountable security to the people of Sierra Leone

[253] It is important to note that the policy provides a whole-of-government approach to address and strengthen national security

[254] Hence, whilst the policy will affect most directly the primary security institutions and agencies Sierra Leone Police (SLP), Republic of Sierra Leone Armed Forces (RSLAF), Office of National Security (ONS), Sierra Leone Correctional Service (SLCS), National Fire Force (NFF) and Central Intelligence and Security Unit (CISU)], it will be broadly interpreted to include MDAs of Government

[255] Policy Vision The policy seeks to provide Sierra Leone’s decision-makers with an effective, professional, accountable, transparent and apolitical national security and intelligence coordination mechanism that will enable government to take informed decisions on key national security priorities

[256] Scope of the Policy The policy applies to the legally established relationship between core national security institutions and security-related MDAs and non-state actors, in relation to security policy development and implementation, monitoring and evaluation

[257] It is intended to address key national security concerns and implementation of effective strategies to mitigate threats and deliver community safety and security, ensure sustainable national development, poverty reduction and conflict prevention within the ambit of the laws of Sierra Leone

[258] It will further foster effective security coordination within the security sector and across government at the national, regional, district and chiefdom levels

[259] The resources required by government to implement the policy will also be addressed

[260] Defining Sierra Leone’s National Security within the Context of the National Constitution The national constitution of Sierra Leone obligates the government, the Armed Forces, the Police, Public officers and security agents to adopt various measures within the ambit of the law to protect the state and its citizens against both internal and external threats to its sovereignty and prosperity

[261] To that effect, Section 5b of the 1991 Constitution of Sierra Leone provides that “the security and welfare of the people of Sierra Leone shall be the primary purpose and responsibility of Government, and to this end, it shall be the duty of the Armed Forces, the Police, Public Officers and all security agents to protect and safeguard the people of Sierra Leone”

[262] In effect, the protection of the state and the welfare of its citizens constitute the main elements of Sierra Leone’s national security

[263] Sierra Leone aspires to create a positive security environment in which the fundamental values and principles of state policy, as enshrined in the national constitution are ensured

[264] These include state continuity, sovereignty, independence, territorial integrity, human rights and fundamental freedoms of all citizens in line with international standards, development of parliamentary democracy, free democratic elections, rule of law, national equality and reconciliation among peoples, stable economy, freedom of circulation of goods, services and capital, social justice and security, natural resources, healthy and stable environmental conditions for people and development of international, regional and sub-regional cooperation. 19 4.6 Sierra Leone in Context

[324] Strategic Level Coordination At the strategic level, the ONS provides security and intelligence coordination for the National Security Council (NSC) and its following sub-committees: The National Security Council Coordinating Group (NSCCG), the Joint Intelligence Committee (JIC), the Joint Maritime Committee (JMC), the Transnational Organised Crime Unit (TOCU), the Provincial Security Committees (PROSECs), the District Security Committees (DISECs) and the Chiefdom Security Committees (CHISECs

[330] The National Security Coordinator (NSCoord.) chairs the NSCCG whilst the Director responsible for Research and Assessment at the ONS serves as Secretary

[331] The specific mandate of the NSCCG is drawn from the responsibilities of the NSCoord and its standard membership can be complemented further by senior civil servants of other MDAs, or any relevant party, should the Chairman believe that the presence of that office would assist in determining security priorities and direction

[332] The JIC provides the forum for the Heads of the primary intelligence institutions to deliberate on intelligence and information from regional committees which impact upon the security of the country

[334] Through the latter, the ONS coordinates assessment of intelligence and security information

[335] JIC-approved assessments are forwarded by the NSCoord to members of the NSC and NSCCG

[336] The NSCoord chairs the JIC whilst the Director responsible for research and assessment serves as Secretary

[337] The Provincial, District and Chiefdom Security Committees (PROSECs, DISECs and CHISECs) are the key regional fora that bring together primary security players, local government administrators, civil society representatives and traditional leaders to encourage and foster the participation of the local community in dealing with security as it affects their areas

[338] They are non-executive committees run by the ONS which provides the secretariat to each of these committees to ensure they focus on security-relevant issues

[339] Operational Level Coordination At the operational level, the ONS guides security policy implementation and NSC/NSCCG-backed directives to ensure that the desired end-states are achieved

[340] At the National level, the Joint Coordination Centre (JCC) is responsible for the operationalization of intelligence as a means to respond to threats posed to national security

[354] CHAPTER FIVE 5.0 NATIONAL SECURITY STRATEGY Sierra Leone’s National Security Strategy (NSS) aims at ensuring stability and security, protection and maintenance of constitutional order, development of a democratic political system, equality of people and citizens and the exercise of human rights and fundamental freedoms, maintaining permanent peace, along with other values enshrined in the Constitution

[355] This Strategy lays the foundation upon which the various sub-strategies are derived

[356] Strategic Goals: The key goals of the strategy are to: guarantee safety and security of all inhabitants of Sierra Leone and their properties through a whole-of-government approach protect the state against external aggression and violations of its territorial integrity establish an effective maritime security governance mechanism that is inclusive and well-coordinated throughout the Republic of Sierra Leone ensure effective border management and control ensure effective management and control of the airspace provide a mechanism for effective and coordinated efforts for disaster mitigation, response and recovery ensure environmental security strengthen international cooperation and security assistance, and

[357] i) minimize Sierra Leone’s vulnerability to domestic and international terrorism

[358] Component Parts of NSS The following are the components of the NSS of Sierra Leone

[359] They have been stated as a guide for the development of the comprehensive individual strategies and implementation plans by the pertinent Ministries, Departments and Agencies

[360] Internal Security Strategy Sierra Leone, in view of the prevailing security challenges, acknowledges the need for novel re-conceptualisation of security and protection

[361] The country has transformed from the traditional concept of physical security, which was the exclusive domain of the primary security forces, to protecting the social, economic and environmental make-up of the State, with a broader stakeholder approach The objectives of the internal security strategy of Sierra Leone are therefore as follows: a. to preserve internal security through the maintenance of law and order and protection of life and property; b. to coordinate security and intelligence at national, provincial and district levels and enhancing the relationship between the security sector and the civil populace; c. to ensure the safe custody, welfare, reformation and rehabilitation of inmates; d. to enhance safety and security of human and material resources against fire incidents or disasters; e. to collect and assess intelligence regarding activities that may constitute threats to Sierra Leone; f. to ensure effective management of Sierra Leone’s immigration, including admissibility and monitoring of the movement of people in and out of the country; and g. to ensure easy access to justice

[362] To ensure that justice is easily accessible locally, the internal security strategy will ensure the strengthening of community and intelligence-led policing; improvement on crime reporting and investigation mechanism; a functional chiefdom police force; and a safe custody and inmate rehabilitation scheme

[363] Sierra Leone’s success in reestablishing local government is notable

[364] Local governments are key in leading and supporting the creation of local development strategies

[365] Communities far from Freetown but close to a district capital have seen the biggest improvement in service delivery

[366] Decentralization has provided citizens with greater opportunities to monitor the performance of local authorities on service delivery and hold them accountable through elections

[367] The experience of Sierra Leone suggests that decentralization is indeed contributing to better local governance by providing greater scope for citizens participation and engagement with local authorities, especially in rural communities

[368] A notable outcome of the internal security policy is to develop and sustain the early warning and early response mechanism through conflict prevention, mitigation and resolution in line with the national security architecture

[369] The internal security strategy will seek to strengthen security sector, cross-governmental and civil society partnerships

[370] Defence Strategy The Sierra Leone defence strategy recognises openness, responsiveness and accountability as the tenets of government policy

[371] As a major part of national and foreign policy, it has been prepared in the era of our nascent democratic consolidation

[372] The defence strategy defines how Sierra Leone can sustain a long-term peace and security needed to rebuild its economy and prosperity

[373] To achieve this, the defence strategy will be established as a significant instrument of security in the current defence system

[374] The highest priority now is to plan with the future in mind

[375] Peace and stability for a long term can only be ensured with credible and professional Republic of Sierra Leone Armed Forces (RSLAF) capable of protecting the country’s interest and its people

[376] A threat to our national interest and security can come from external and internal sources, or a combination of both

[377] The role of the RSLAF is vital in defending the nation and ensuring the integrity of our land, air and sea borders from would-be-aggressors

[378] This strategy is aimed at enhancing operational effectiveness and ensuring that the RSLAF is representative of a broad section of personnel from all backgrounds in the Sierra Leone society

[379] The strategy is also aimed at promoting a culture of fairness, wherein individuals are regarded and assessed based on their performance and commitment, not their beliefs

[380] The objectives of this defence strategy are to enhance the RSLAF to: a. maintain the territorial integrity of Sierra Leone b. deliver intelligence and mapping services, c. contribute to maritime security, fisheries protection, search and rescue capabilities, and d. develop aviation surveillance and mobility capacity and e. contribute to public and presidential guard duties

[381] The first requirement of this strategy is to maintain, consolidate and develop the current achievement of the RSLAF

[382] The permanent interest, among others, is to ensure that the professional training standards provided by our partners are sustained

[383] To actualise this goal, the Ministry of Defence shall acquire equipment and establish doctrine, whilst training materials and standards will be the subject of constant review

[384] In concord with this strategy, the government shall continue to maintain a non-partisan and democratically accountable Armed Forces and enforce clear codes of conduct for all members of the Armed Forces

[385] The Ministry of Defence, in collaboration with the RSALF, is committed to recruiting and training high calibre personnel irrespective of tribe, region, gender, religion, political or ideological beliefs

[386] The Ministry of Defence is also committed to developing an equal opportunities strategy through education and provision of strong leadership at all levels

[387] The government recognises the need to make the RSLAF a model of excellence, on which to take forward this important strategy initiative

[388] Sierra Leone subscribes to international peacekeeping and ultimately peace enforcement operations

[389] Overseas disaster relief operations will also be a feature of future tasking, considering capacity building as a key requirement

[390] Military Aid to Civil Authority (MACA) is a key component of the defence strategy

[391] This will encompass Military Aid to Civil Power (MACP), Military Aid to Government Departments (MAGD); and Military Aid to Civil Community (MACC

[392] It is the stated strategy of government to work with our neighbours to foster friendship and cooperation

[393] Defence cooperation with other Mano River Union (MRU) States is of political, strategic and geographic essence, and therefore, a priority to Sierra Leone

[394] The resuscitation of the MRU offers a new hope of maintaining peace and stability within the sub-region

[395] In view of this, initiatives to consolidate the defence and security frameworks of the MRU, ECOWAS and AU will be incorporated to address threats to the country’s territorial integrity

[396] Sierra Leone will implement its defence strategy in accordance with international law and acceptable norms

[397] The implementation of the strategy will lead to the following outcomes: - implementation of the revised Terms and Conditions of Service - review of structures and adjustments to meet revised military tasks - revised defence training requirements will be developed and delivered - procurement of equipment required for the internal and external operations of the RSLAF - defence information management systems will be developed - defence estate demarcation and accommodation facilities improved, and - defence management and expenditure processes improved, and defence legal and policy frameworks developed and implemented

[398] Maritime Security Strategy Like all other countries, Sierra Leone is highly dependent on its coastal and inland waterways for its security and the welfare of its people and the economy

[399] Pursuant to the Mid-Term National Development Plan 2019-2023, a national strategy on maritime security must be developed and implemented to cope with the associated challenges regarding maritime security

[400] The Strategy aims at addressing challenges such as illicit national and transnational maritime threats which include piracy, armed robbery, drug trafficking, illegal bunkering, etc

[401] Therefore, in response to the worrisome developments regarding maritime security issues, this strategy captures the threats to maritime security and also identifies strategic objectives aimed at combating the aforementioned threats, and spells out strategic actions deemed necessary to address present and future challenges

[402] The strategic goal encompasses the need for improved maritime domain awareness and effective maritime governance which are crucial to the realisation of the wealth of the sea and prosperity of the State

[403] The strategic objectives of the maritime strategy are to: a. establish an effective maritime security governance mechanism that is inclusive and well-coordinated throughout the Republic of Sierra Leone

[404] b. promote maritime domain awareness taking into consideration understanding of the marine resources of Sierra Leone and to ensure their protection and judicious utilization for the benefit of Sierra Leoneans c. improve the country’s maritime security and safety capabilities through capacity building for maritime institutions and the provision of the necessary logistical support to enhance the country’s defence and law enforcement capabilities, and d. develop Sierra Leone’s blue economic potential in pursuit of both maximum economic yield and long-term sustainability

[405] In order to achieve maritime security in Sierra Leone, the strategy will seek to blend public and private maritime security activities into comprehensive and integrated efforts that address all maritime threats underpinned by the principles of respect for the rule of law, integrity and patriotism

[406] National and regional coordination, cooperation, intelligence and information sharing are also required to protect and secure the maritime domain of Sierra Leone

[407] It is expected that effective implementation of the Maritime Security Strategy will enhance maritime security governance for safety and security of goods and passengers, regional cooperation and maritime domain awareness and the maximization of the country’s blue economy

[408] Border Security Strategy

[409] The notion of border is central to the concept of statehood and state sovereignty

[410] It helps to demarcate the zone in which a state exercises jurisdiction

[411] It defines States in legal and geographical terms, and connects countries with each other

[412] Our national border with Guinea and Liberia is long, porous and not properly manned

[413] The total stretch of land boundary between Sierra Leone and her neighbours (Guinea and Liberia) is 958 Kilometres

[414] The land boundary between Sierra Leone and Guinea is 652 Km, while that of Sierra Leone and Liberia is 306 km

[415] The protection of our border is essential for the protection of the State, its people and economy

[416] The free movement of people, goods and services across the border connect the state to economic opportunities through trade, tourism and foreign investment

[417] Management of border crossing points is associated with risks

[418] vices such as smuggling of contraband goods, trafficking of human beings, gun running, illegal immigration, illegal drug movement and organised criminal gangs are common threats at border crossing points

[419] Thus, the creation of an appropriate procedure with a good framework that is enforced by competent and effective State institutions is considered fundamental to holistic and integrated border management

[420] The Strategy is directed towards achieving the following undermentioned objectives: a. to provide effective guidelines that will help Government of Sierra Leone to take informed decision on border management and control; b. to develop and maintain professional, transparent and accountable guidelines and practices for officers that are deployed at the borders; c. to facilitate movements and ensure protection of the rights of people across our common borders; and d. to reduce the vices that undermine effective border management

[421] Sierra Leone’s border security strategy rests within the mandate of the 15th Protocol of the Mano River Union, which emphasizes cross-border cooperation among Member States within the Union

[422] The protocol underscores peace and security within the context of cooperation, especially in such areas as information exchange, intelligence sharing, joint security patrols, joint security assessment by Member States, joint training of security operatives by Member States, fight against the proliferation of small arms and light weapons, fight against drug smuggling, human trafficking, poaching within the reserve national parks that are trans-boundary, smuggling of precious minerals, joint use of assets and the establishment of joint border posts

[423] For the effectiveness of early warning and early response in border security management, the functionality of the Committees, which include the Joint Security Committee, Technical Committee, otherwise known as Expert Group and the Joint Border Security and Confidence Building Units should be ensured

[424] The effective implementation of the border security strategy will reduce or eradicate all forms of smuggling, improve coordination of security at border crossing points, ensure local participation in border security matters, ensure free movement of goods, persons and services across the borders, ensure the protection of human rights at all border crossing points, improve trade and commerce, enhance eco-tourism, ensure optimal use of resources and minimise conflict and violence in the border areas

[425] Disaster Risk Management Strategy Sierra Leone continues to be prone to climate-related disasters such as floods, fires, mudslides, landslides, water shortages and coastal erosion

[426] Series of disasters have killed thousands of people with the loss of homes and properties costing millions of dollars

[427] Disasters affect poor and vulnerable people the most, and can be a significant impediment to poverty reduction and sustainable development

[428] It is imperative that Sierra Leone introduces a comprehensive disaster risk management strategy, outlining guiding principles and mechanisms, from a cross sectoral perspective, and a framework that is pertinent to sustainable disaster preparedness and management

[429] Therefore, the importance of a National Disaster Risk Strategy for Sierra Leone cannot be overemphasized

[430] This strategy shall include prevention, mitigation, preparedness, response and recovery as essential elements in building a safer environment

[431] The strategy’s goals are to: (i) empower disaster management structures to prevent existing/potential risks becoming real disasters (ii) empower disaster management structures to prepare for the potential of risks becoming real disasters, and (iii) empower disaster management structures to be capable of responding appropriately and timely to disasters and support recovery

[432] The National Disaster Risk Management Strategy shall take into consideration a comprehensive approach (strengthening preparations for prevention, mitigation and response towards recovery from disaster impacts), a resilient community approach (strengthening the public’s ability to better cope with disaster impacts), a cross sectoral approach (requiring all relevant MDAs understanding their roles and the need to collaborate), an all-hazard approach (focusing on all hazards that impact the citizens and landscape of Sierra Leone) and the incorporation of legislative components (ensuring alignment between policy and legislation

[433] This strategy shall provide clarity on the institutional arrangements required to reduce vulnerabilities, strengthen capacities and improve risk governance in the country

[434] Environmental Security Strategy

[435] This strategy recognizes that Sierra Leone is endowed with abundant rainfall, substantial mineral resources, fertile soils, and extensive marine resources

[436] The sustainable utilization of these resources will engender stability, prosperity, social harmony and the well-being of society

[437] The lack of appropriate natural resource management policy and/or the ineffective implementation thereof can exacerbate pollution, lead to degradation of land, air, water and forests, and loss of livelihoods of communities

[438] In addition, extreme weather events, and failure of climate-change mitigation and adaptation can erode the resilience to disasters

[439] Sierra Leone will seek to strengthen its energy and water security service delivery capacity

[440] Sierra Leone is only a minor contributor to the global greenhouse gases emission problem, but it has one of the highest vulnerability indices in the world

[441] Solving the climate change dilemma requires a reduction of the country’s vulnerability to climate change

[442] The security implications of further environmental degradation exacerbated by climate change ranges from increased sea levels and storm surges (which will threaten coastal regions, infrastructure, and property) to water scarcity, food shortages and competition for natural resources, with the potential for conflicts

[443] The financial burden to deal with these frequent disasters will be high and will divert resources from other critical sectors such as education, health and agriculture

[444] This environmental security strategy seeks to ensure that the government addresses all environmental threats in a comprehensive and timely manner

[445] The objective of this strategy is to protect and conserve the environment by preventing and mitigating natural and anthropogenic threats to environmental security and sustainability

[446] The development of this strategy is guided by the following principles: i. Ecosystem goods and services fundamentally underpin human well-being and human security

[447] Human beings depend on the earth’s ecosystems and the services they provide

[448] conflict affects the viability or sustainability of investments in environmental protection and their outcomes

[449] iii. ecosystem degradation, resource competition, or inequitable distribution of benefits increase vulnerability and conflict risk

[450] iv. effective cooperation among stakeholders can increase capacity for conflict management, prevention, and recovery

[451] It is expected that the effective implementation of the Environmental Security Strategy will lead to: - improved environmental compliance through the periodic review and update of environmental laws

[452] the institutionalization of adaptation and mitigation measures for climate change and environmental risks  well managed natural resources to ensure environmental sustainability  sustainable utilization of forest resources and biodiversity  well-developed environmental information systems and increased awareness on environmental security  strengthened international cooperation and collaboration on the environment through the implementation of Multilateral Environmental Agreements (MEAs) and Regional Transboundary Initiatives and programmes and  implementation of transformative projects to achieve land degradation neutrality targets 5.2.7 Counter Terrorism Strategy There has been a growing trend of terrorism in the West African sub-region

[453] The trend shows a southward shift of the threat towards the Mano River Union Basin with Ivory Coast having been a direct victim in

[454] With Al-Qaeda linked organisations being active in Mali, neighbouring Guinea which is an MRU Member State, remains susceptible to the threats

[455] Al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM) which had claimed responsibility for the attacks in Bamako (Mali) Ouagadougou (Burkina Faso) and Grand Bassam (Cote d'Ivoire) has been the most active agents in the sub-regional spread of extremism

[456] The attacks bear the common hallmark of mass shootings targeting public facilities frequented by Westerners

[457] These vulnerabilities remain generally uniform in all MRU states

[458] Sierra Leone has not experienced any incident of terrorism, apart from the systematic terror tactics employed by the Revolutionary United Front (RUF) and other forces in Sierra Leone during the country’s armed conflict

[459] However, due to intelligence estimates and the nature and dynamics of terrorism, efforts have been applied to counter the threat

[460] In April 2013, Sierra Leone deployed troops to the African Union Mission in Somalia (AMISOM) to combat Al-Shabab forces within the framework of collective security and peace enforcement

[461] Heretofore, in January 2012, a local media source in Somalia indicated that Al-Shabab forces had vowed to attack Sierra Leone troops in Somalia if they carried out plans of deployment which materialised in Somalia

[462] Added to this media report, Al-Shabab is known to have issued threats of attacks against all troop contributing countries to Somalia

[463] The development of the counter-terrorism strategy of Sierra Leone is therefore impelling

[464] The objectives of the counter-terrorism strategy are to: i. prevent terrorist related activities through counter-radicalization, enhancing the criminal justice system and law enforcement ii

[465] prepare for the consequences of any terrorist attack through enacting legislation and capacity building iii. pursue terrorist and their sponsors through improved intelligence collection and deployment of requisite resources, and iv

[466] protect the public and key national assets and interest through strengthened border controls and protective security measures The Counter-Terrorism Strategy of Sierra Leone shall be prepared in the spirit of UN Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy, the African Union Plan of Action on the Prevention and Combating of Terrorism and the ECOWAS Counter-Terrorism Strategy

[467] It shall stress the need for sustained and inclusive partnership with local communities and civil societies actors to help prevent violent conflicts and extremist

[468] The Strategy shall ensure that terrorism is not associated with any religion, nationality or ethnic group

[469] It shall also adopt an inter-institutional approach

[470] The Strategy shall seek to better position Sierra Leone to address the threat of terrorism in respect of prevention and response to any terrorist related incident

[471] Cyber Security Strategy

[472] The national cyber security strategy is a concrete five-year Strategic Action Plan geared towards improving the country’s overall cybersecurity landscape; including increasing the security and resilience of its critical infrastructure, cultivating good digital citizenry and developing appropriate legislations

[473] Moreover, it will enable the government and the private sector to work coherently towards implementing specific objectives, including mobilizing the required resources

[474] The objectives of the cyber security strategy are to: - create Institutional, Legal & Regulatory Frameworks for Effective governance - protect Critical digital Infrastructures through Response Readiness - promote public education, awareness, online child protection, right and privacy citizens - develop Cyber Capabilities to support national security objectives, and - strengthen national, regional and international Cooperation

[475] The Sierra Leone National Cyber Security Strategy shall be delivered on seven key guiding principles, to ensure that a risk-based approach is adopted by the Government, private sector, academia, and civil society in assessing and responding to cyber-related threats or issues; shall seek to enhance all key stakeholders’ effectiveness in improving the cyber security posture of Sierra Leone by recognizing the various roles and responsibilities of different stakeholders and promoting national cooperation and coordination for cyber security-related activities amongst stakeholders; promoting bilateral, regional, and international collaboration, recognizing the borderless nature of cyberspace; respect for the rule of law and human rights; shall enable the continuous development of Sierra Leone’s capacity to address fast-changing cyber security issues and developments; shall ensure that the cyberspace is fully leveraged by Sierra Leone to spur broader socio-economic development, facilitates sustainable socio-economic development across the entire nation; and shall promote and facilitate both individual and collective responsibility in taking steps in combating cybercrime

[476] The effective implementation of the cyber security strategy hopes to achieve the following

[477] Establish and operationalize a national cybersecurity governance structure

[478] Develop legal and regulatory frameworks to enhance Sierra Leone cyber security posture

[479] Empower the Criminal Justice System to prevent, deter, respond, to, and mitigate cybersecurity strategy 

[480] Protect all Critical National Information Infrastructure (CNII) and essential services

[481] Establish Cyber-incident response capabilities with clear goals and responsibilities

[482] Develop cyber defiance strategy for deterring activities in Sierra Leone cyberspace 

[483] Establish a proactive national contingency plan for Cybersecurity emergencies and crisis

[484] Implement national initiatives on online child protection

[485] Promote good cybersecurity culture through public education, awareness-raising, campaigns, and capacity building for specified target groups

[486] Build citizens trust and confidence on the internet by promoting secure and safer access 

[487] Develop a framework for the integration of cybersecurity in the formal educational system 

[488] Enhance workforce training and professional skills development in cybersecurity for both experts and non-experts in the public and private sectors 

[489] Foster local cybersecurity industry in Sierra Leone

[490] Promote innovation, research and development in cybersecurity

[491] Enhance national cooperation and collaboration in the private sector •

[492] Establish regional and international cooperation mechanisms to fight against cybercrime and secure the cyberspace

[493] International Cooperation Strategy

[494] The stability of the Mano River Basin and the West Africa Region is of paramount interest to Sierra Leone

[495] The country is also committed to supporting global peace and security

[496] The promotion of good relations and cooperation and the strengthening of democracy within the Mano River Basin can have a positive impact on the internal security of Sierra Leone

[497] Sierra Leone is committed to active international cooperation on political, economic, military and security levels, and shall make efforts to fulfil its strategic interest to achieve closer and institutionalised relations with important international structures

[498] Sierra Leone’s foreign policy is directed towards achieving the four objectives of the state, which are: i. the promotion and protection of the national interest; ii

[499] the promotion of sub-regional, regional and inter-African cooperation and unity; iii

[500] the promotion of international cooperation for the consolidation of international peace and security, and mutual respect for their territorial integrity and independence; and iv. respect for international law and treaty obligations, as well as seeking the resolution of international disputes by negotiation, conciliation, arbitration or adjudication

[501] The protection of the rights, property and interest of our citizens form part of our policy objectives, irrespective of their political orientation

[502] Sierra Leone aims to develop a two-track programme to address the issue of destitute Sierra Leoneans living abroad, whatever their circumstances are

[503] The programme would include disseminating information about citizens’ rights, establishing a monitoring and reporting system, examining steps to prevent criminality and repatriation of our less fortunate compatriots living abroad, especially those who register their willingness for repatriation

[504] The protection of the country’s interest abroad remains the Foreign Ministry’s key responsibility

[505] In this regard, to ascertain the benefits Sierra Leone could derive from its external interest and remittances, a study followed by a compiled inventory shall be undertaken

[506] Prior to that, among others, the system will be re-activated whereby Sierra Leoneans arriving in other countries can register with either the Sierra Leone mission/consulate or recognised union of Sierra Leoneans

[507] In addition to the friendly relations Sierra Leone enjoys with the United Kingdom, USA, China, Nigeria, among others, the country continues to cultivate amicable relationships with other countries to further strengthen its multilateral ties

[508] The effective implementation of foreign policy requires the establishment of administration for a diplomatic service and consular post, with mission and posts located in strategic centres around the world and staffed by competent and efficient professionals

[509] The national interest will be protected; sub-regional and regional cooperation developed and sustained; wider international cooperation developed and sustained; international law and treaty obligations respected and international assistance to Sierra Leone security and justice sectors coordinated

[510] Sierra Leone’s NSP&S has been designed in response to addressing the issues that contributed to the decade-long civil war and the strengthening of post-war security sector architecture in order to provide the enabling environment for peaceful coexistence and sustainable development

[511] The NSP&S underscores the paradigm shift from physical security to human security as the basis for Sierra Leone’s socio-economic development

[512] It requires a whole-of-government approach with pertinent stakeholder institutions working in a collaborative and coordinated manner
