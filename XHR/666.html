[450] Security policy crises The security policy situation of today is characterized by a broad and complex risk and threat picture

[451] Thus, unpredictability is a significant feature of the security policy challenges that Norway may be faced with

[452] Future political pressure against Norway cannot be ruled out with the threat of military force or, at its most extreme, an armed attack against Norwegian territory

[453] Nor can it be ruled out that terrorist

[454] and cyber attacks may represent a challenge to national security

[455] The boundaries between peace, security policy crisis and armed conflict are no longer as clearly defined

[456] The possibility cannot be ruled out that a foreign state could use a combination of different methods for political gains

[457] In addition to threats of military force, there is the possibility of hidden attacks on critical societal functions, and specific actions aimed at sowing discord among the population and distrust of legal authorities

[458] In this context, a security policy crisis would be closely approaching an armed conflict or risk of war situation, whereby our territorial integrity and national sovereignty are threatened

[459] This implies a threat to basic and fundamental national interests, values and objectives

[460] A security policy crisis could be provoked by both intentional events or unpremeditated escalation

[461] Important characteristics of such a situation will be that the crisis lies in the grey area between peace, security policy crisis and armed conflict, and has both a military and international dimension

[462] Thus, a security policy crisis is normally characterised by such a situation creating considerable fear in society and by the measures used, or that it is feared will be used, having the potential to threaten the authorities’ ability to protect national interests

[463] In a security policy crisis and armed conflict, the implications for national security and relations with other countries suggest that control and overall coordination of crisis management must be handled at the national level of authority

[464] It is stressed, however, that although the strategic level will always have a more active and decisive role in such situations, the responsibility for crisis management and ongoing administrative tasks by regional and local authorities is not necessarily changed

[465] Based on the UN pact, NATO is the foundation of Norwegian security and defence policy

[466] To a great extent, Norway will endeavour to deal with security policy challenges in close cooperation with its allies

[467] However, episodes and security policy crises may arise that are of such a nature that they will need to be tackled with the sole use of national capacities

[468] Basically, security policy crises will be of a political nature and will require political action

[469] Measures taken will be multifaceted, complex and adapted to the relevant situation

[470] These could range from diplomatic measures (such as protests and negotiations), via economic measures and measures pertaining to international law (such as sanctions), to military action

[471] The Norwegian Armed Forces and the Police are the Government’s machinery of power

[472] While the Police’s tasks are primarily directed internally, against persons and actors that violate national laws, the Norwegian Armed Forces’ tasks are primarily directed externally, against other States or actors that violate national sovereignty or threaten the nation However, military measures must always be viewed in the context of diplomatic measures or other measures that are relevant in dealing with a security policy crisis

[473] The composition and use of measures will depend on the situation

[474] Depending on the scope and nature of the crisis, civilian emergency preparedness resources may also be used within the total defence concept, i.e. both to protect civilian needs and to support the Norwegian Armed Forces
