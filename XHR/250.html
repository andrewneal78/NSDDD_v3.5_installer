[254] In addition to information gathering within areas of Norwegian interest, the task of exercising sovereignty involves the permanent and continuous processing and analysis of information about various state and non-state actors which operate, or are likely to operate in areas of Norwegian interest

[255] According to international law, only the armed forces may engage combatant forces from a foreign country

[256] The task of fighting combatant opponents is, therefore, the responsibility of the NAF

[257] Personnel belonging to the police are not defined as combatants

[258] This means that civilian police cannot legally participate in hostilities against a combatant enemy

[259] The exercising of self-defence according to international law against armed attacks is therefore a task for the NAF

[260] In certain geographic areas where Norway does not have territorial sovereignty, Norway still enjoys sovereign rights according to international law

[261] The concept of sovereign rights is most often used to describe the rights Norway enjoys as a coastal state

[262] The Convention on the Law of the Sea is among the most comprehensive multilateral agreements within the UN system

[263] The Convention includes rules that cover all ocean areas, the airspace above, and the ocean floor and structures underneath

[264] It also regulates states’ rights and duties in these areas and provides rules about environmental protection, oceanic research and transfer of technology

[265] According to the Convention, Norway enjoys sovereign rights within the 200 nautical miles Exclusive Economic Zone

[266] This pertains in particular to the right of exploring, exploiting, conserving and managing living and non-living natural resources within this area

[267] As a costal state, Norway also has several obligations, including sustainable management of resources, and the obligation to cooperate with states that are affected by decisions relating to these areas

[268] Exercising of sovereign rights according to international law also implies the right to regulate private legal subjects of foreign nationality outside Norwegian territory

[269] This is the case for example with regard to Norway’s regulations of fishery quotas outside own territorial waters

[270] Exercising of authority may acquire a security dimension, in particular if a foreign state has a different interpretation of the extent of Norwegian sovereign rights, or if a state decides to challenge these rights

[271] In certain defined areas, units of the NAF, in particular the Border Guard and Coast Guard, have responsibility for exercising authority and law enforcement on behalf, or in support of other civilian government agencies

[272] Such exercising of authority will often be complementary to the other tasks of the NAF

[273] The relationship between exercising sovereignty and exercising authority may entail a violation of both Norwegian sovereignty, and a violation of the Norwegian border and immigration laws

[274] Upholding the law is primarily a civilian task

[275] However, the intention behind a border violation and the origin of the perpetrator will be a key criterion

[276] There is a major difference between a situation where a foreign military unit is operating on the wrong side of the border, and one in which a few soldiers have fled across it

[277] The more a violation is deemed to have a national security dimension, the more reason it is for handling the incident as a case of national sovereignty

[559] A Clear Framework for the Use of Military Force The NAF are one of the most important instruments of security and foreign policy
