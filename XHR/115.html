[147] THE ENVIRONMENT OF FINLAND'S SECURITY AND DEFENCE POLICY 2.1 Threats, challenges and background factors to international security Major changes that have occurred in recent years are accentuated in the operat-ing environment where Finland’s security and defence policy is operating

[148] The 1990s, the first post-Cold War decade, were characterized by policies aimed at achieving stability

[149] Efforts were made to extend the zone of democracy and market economics, especially in Europe, and institutions were enlarged and integration pursued

[150] The major turning point for Europe took place as a peaceful transition, although instability continued in the Balkans

[151] Since the publication of the previous report on Finnish Security and Defence Policy in 2001, stability has increased in the areas close to Finland

[152] The enlargement of the EU and NATO has improved security throughout the region

[153] The same trend has been supported by changes in Russia, which, on the hand, has led the country to seek a stronger international role for itself again

[154] Although the threat of a conventional war has receded, particularly in Europe, Finland’s security is also linked through globalization to the wider international environment

[155] The new threats targeted in recent years at comprehensive security have started to be reflected to an increasing degree in the external and internal security of European countries

[156] For that reason the following examination starts with broader international developments

[157] International structures handed down from the time of the Cold War, which lasted many decades, and the cooperation system based on states’ sovereignty are the subject of many changes and challenges

[158] As yet the international community does not have a completely clear, unified understanding of how to respond to the challenges, as can be seen from the different attitudes of many European countries and the United States to the war in Iraq

[159] Since the September 2001 terrorist strikes, counter-terrorist activity has become dominant especially in the US policies

[160] Terrorism is gaining new strength from extremist groups of radical Islamists

[161] Uncertainty is also heightened by the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction

[162] While bringing new opportunities, the process of globalization tends to exacerbate inequalities

[163] Risks of different crises related to internal development of societies are on the increase

[164] Interdependence among states strengthens common security but there are risks attached to it

[165] Terrorism has been around for centuries, but the new kind of networked terrorism aimed at destruction on a massive scale is a new phenomenon

[166] It takes particular advantage of the vulnerability of Western societies, but it is present everywhere

[167] A wide range of means must be used to combat this phenomenon, and particular attention must be given to the root causes of terrorism as well

[168] In a modern society there are a great many factors involved in security, which is why security enhancement must include not only military means but also numerous other sectors

[169] Although each country is itself responsible for the security of its citizens, it is essential to have effective international cooperation and to strengthen the international legal order

[170] While the debate over how the new challenges should be tackled is a heated one, there is broad agreement that the situation can only be managed by pursuing both positive, collaborative solutions and opportunities for imposing sanctions of different kinds

[171] Considerable differences of opinion exist, however, regarding the use of force and its legitimacy in different situations

[172] Pressures on the international system are being felt in all areas, including the work of the United Nations and other international institutions

[173] It is important to be able to develop their operations so that they should meet the security challenges of the new century

[174] Finland’s immediate security is maintained in various ways, through foreign, security and defence policy measures

[175] The nation’s security is increasingly influenced not only by military but also economic and political developments in Europe and globally

[176] It is also affected by the relations between the European Union and other key actors, such as the United States and Russia, and their collaboration in seeking resolutions to the world’s crises

[177] Far from being isolated from the rest of the world, Finland and its neighbouring areas are an integral part of the wider entity

[178] This applies as much to the positive opportunities offered as to dangers and risks

[179] Finland’s position as a member of the European Union is of particular importance

[180] Finland is sharing in the responsibility for international security to an ever-greater extent

[181] Effects of globalization on security Efforts to build workable economic and democratic structures as part of the worldwide process known as globalization have involved both successes and failures

[182] On the one hand, we have seen the emergence of dynamic centres of economic power and partners that provide a serious challenge to industrial countries, while on the other, the failures have led to crises that have the potential to threaten both regional and global security

[183] Globalization has made the world smaller

[184] Both worldwide problems and problems transcending national boundaries can have an impact on global security, security in Europe and the security of Finnish society and the country’s citizens

[185] Risks and threats have assumed new forms and their effects are felt more quickly and over a wider area

[186] Combating these threats requires extensive international cooperation and a better readiness to tackle problems and their underlying causes

[187] Rapid changes in the external environment mean that Finland, too, has to put its security and defence policy in a wider context

[188] Global security problems are shaping the objectives and opportunities of the action of the European Union and also Finland

[189] They are thus no longer distant issues for Finland either

[190] The benefits of globalization are unequally distributed, and people often react violently when there are grievances and pressures for change

[191] The general view is that the successful governance of globalization requires properly capable states and workable cooperation arrangements between them

[192] Even though globalization and international stability are undoubtedly beneficial to economies such as Finland’s, growing interdependence among the countries of the world is making it increasingly difficult for individual states to manage crises on their own

[193] Economic links between countries help people, goods, services, capital and information to move freely around the globe and make more accessible sophisticated technological networks and systems that are easily vulnerable

[194] However, if terrorists and organized crime are also able to make use of these opportunities, security risks will grow and societies will become more vul-nerable in many ways

[195] The unpredictability of globalization and the possibility of it developing into crises pose real economic and security challenges for Finland, too

[196] In the era of globalization, different disturbances in one part of the world can have an immediate impact on other countries both directly and through crises in the functioning of the international order and problems in the global economy

[197] By affecting the conditions under which Finnish companies with international interests operate, such developments ultimately have an impact on the well-being of all Finns

[198] Background factors to security problems In most instances security problems manifest themselves in the form of violent eruptions and conflicts

[199] Even though a conflict may be triggered by an acute event, there are often deeper economic, social and cultural factors behind the problems

[200] About five billion of the world’s population of six billion live in developing countries

[201] Poverty, inequality and a lack of development are, as such, contributing to social instability

[202] Failure of development policy and social reforms and exclusion from the benefits of globalization lead to crises that in turn can create new conflicts and security threats

[203] The aim of long-term development and partnership policies is to support countries in their efforts to carry out social and economic reforms, while at the same time, crisis management capabilities should be improved in preparation for dealing with failures

[204] Failing states International order is based on individual states acting in accordance with agreed rules and meeting their international obligations

[205] If a state weakens or even collapses, in many cases not only is the country's own population under threat, but also regional and international stability and security

[206] Countries that are no longer able to function often become a security vacuum that provides a fertile ground for new global terrorism and organized crime and generates pressures for migration

[207] The risk of epidemics and environmental threats may also grow

[208] At the same time, the situation in failing states may aggravate and widen regional conflicts and lead to civil wars that may ultimately develop into international crises

[209] Problems concerning human rights, democracy and the rule of law Large-scale violations of human rights, a lack of good governance and rule of law, preventing citizens from participating in their own affairs and serious social inequalities generate social instability

[210] They may also create a breeding ground for extremist groups and increase pressures for uncontrolled migration

[211] The rights of refugees and minorities are often under the most serious threat

[212] Realization of universal human rights promotes the stability and provides a basis for sustainable development

[213] Human rights agreements have been widely ratified and the mechanisms set up to monitor human rights play an important role

[214] Confrontation between civilizations and action by extremist fundamentalist groups The building of workable economies and democracies may, in addition to economic, political and social factors, also be hampered or even prevented by the fact that the required modernization of a society meets resistance arising from cultural differences

[215] At worst, this development may turn into a ‘conflict of civilizations’ in which extremist fundamentalist groups take strong, even violent action

[216] During the last few years, Islamic radical movements in particular have gained strength, but extremism may also have other origins

[217] To prevent conflicts between civilizations and cultures, the dialogue between them must be intensified and the mutual information base and cooperation expanded

[218] Universal emphasis is now on making the political dialogue more effective and on developing other forms of cooperation, particularly between the Western world and Arab and Islamic countries

[219] Terrorism Terrorism has been present throughout history in different societies and in various forms, although it may not always have been referred to by that name

[220] Behind the terrorist groups have been separatist, nationalist, regional or ethnic agendas, ideological and revolutionary plans, religious fanaticism, etc

[221] In international cooperation, terrorism as a concept has come to be understood as political violence used by individuals or groups, the targets of which are selected randomly or for their symbolic value

[222] Traditional terrorism has tended to focus on local operations and on targets selected in accordance with their objectives

[223] The end of the Cold War reduced state support for terrorism but at the same time gave rise to new circumstances in which terrorist groups could become networked, the uncontrolled proliferation of weapons and explosives could occur and links could be established to international flows of capital and organized crime

[224] Many of today’s prominent terrorist groups operate without a clear political agenda

[225] Traditional terrorism (e.g. ETA, IRA) has not disappeared, however, even from Europe, but its impact is mainly local

[226] It is possible, too, that some new groups of extremists will take up terror as a weapon

[227] A new form of terrorism that operates globally and aims at having a large destructive impact is primarily associated with the network of extremist Islamist groups

[228] In Europe too, these extremist groups are currently the most serious terrorist threat and will remain so in the near future

[229] Despite being hit by countermeasures, al-Qaeda remains the most important of these networks, which all have a decentralized operational and leadership structure

[230] Though al-Qaeda itself has been weakened by counter-terrorist measures, it has attracted extremist Islamist groups and networks that have emerged locally and identify with its aims

[231] In addition to the Middle East, Southeast Asia, the Horn of Africa, East Africa and Afghanistan and its neighbouring areas are also bases for global terrorism, and there is a danger that extremist groups may spread to new areas

[232] Islamic immigrant groups in Europe are also being targeted by radical groups trying to recruit new followers

[233] Western countries and particularly the United States are the main target for attacks

[234] Europe is not only a target but also a recruiting and transit and basing area
