[189] In principle, a challenge involves causality: some phenomena precede others and do not occur in isolation, but are linked to each other through a process of interaction between a set of phenomena—risks, threats, and vulnerabilities—and a future situation

[190] The causality criterion identifies the antecedents that shape the presence and impact of the challenge, as well as its relationship to various problems of insecurity, crime, and violence in all their manifestations, and the opportunities for solutions or positive influence

[192] Security challenges develop and persist over time. Addressing them requires transcending the political dimension and shifting to a long-term state perspective that addresses their underlying causes to mitigate them and reduce their impact

[193] Finally, they imply a relationship of proportionality with respect to the State's response capacity in terms of the relevance and significance of the level of risk they may represent
