[1] The Concept of Defense of the Republic of Latvia Approved by the decision of the National Security Council of May

[2] Basically accepted at the meeting of the Cabinet of Ministers of June 13, 1995, instructing the Ministry of Defense to prepare the draft legal acts necessary for the implementation of the concept Introduction The need for military defense of Latvia It seems hopeless for a small country with limited military potential to resist a militarily much stronger aggressor

[3] However, the Latvian people are not able to survive the occupation of a foreign country

[4] If the Latvian people do not defend themselves against aggression by all possible means, Latvia will be occupied, and Latvians will cease to exist as a nation in a short time

[5] Defense of the state by military means may require great human sacrifices, but Latvia has no other way out if it wants to exist as an independent state

[17] Financial limitations and assumptions Taking into account the burden on the state budget, significant expenditures in the field of defence are currently not considered justifiable

[18] Therefore, a compromise must be found between the military-political situation, which determines the state's threat level, and the state's capabilities

[19] The current budget allocation is limited and hinders the development of the NAF

[20] In order to ensure the necessary development of the NAF, it must be assumed that: - the necessary budgetary funds will be found, which is possible by combining a more targeted use of existing funds with a real increase in the funds allocated for national defence

[21] The speed of implementation of the force structure development plan and their sequential creation depend on the budget funds allocated for national defence

[22] Latvia's defense budget % of the gross national product will not exceed the defense budgets of Western European countries

[23] Public restrictions Currently, there is a lack of public and political support for the significant development of the NAF, because for a significant part of the population, service in the Latvian NAF still reminds of service in the USSR Armed Forces and the military occupation of Latvia

[24] There is also no consensus on what the security situation of Latvia should be, although national defense is considered important

[25] All this is reflected in the attitude of young people towards compulsory national service and voluntary enlistment in military service

[26] The Ministry of Defense: - must expand debates on defense issues in the mass media; - must explain why armed resistance to aggression is necessary; - must prove that armed resistance is possible and necessary; - must ensure that society changes its views on the Latvian NAF and service in it

[27] The purpose of this document is to establish an effective defence system and determine the Latvian defence policy and the corresponding structures of the National Armed Forces in order to create a basis for the development, financial and operational plans of the National Armed Forces

[28] When developing the document, only potential internal and external military threats to the state apparatus, i.e. organised use of armed force for political purposes, are taken into account

[29] Security of the Baltic region The security of Latvia is inseparable from the security of the Baltics as a whole

[30] This is emphasised by the activities of the Baltic Council of Ministers in coordinating the resolution of security and defence problems of the Baltic States

[31] A threat to one Baltic State threatens all

[32] In the event of a crisis situation in one of the Baltic States, pressure is expected to be exerted on the other two States in order to prevent coordinated action by the Baltic States

[33] However, it is in the interests of all three Baltic States to act in a coordinated manner

[34] Coordinated military action should promote this cooperation

[35] From a purely military point of view, only together will the Baltic States be able to resist aggression

[36] The (National) Defence Ministries of the Baltic States: - should develop unified National Security and Defence Concepts; - close cooperation between the headquarters of the armed forces must be ensured; - weapons and communication systems capable of operating together must be created; - coordinated action must be ensured in the control of borders, territory, maritime economic zone and airspace; - similar military training must be ensured; - joint units must be created, such as BALTBAT

[37] Analysis of military tasks The security situation of Latvia is determined by a set of factors, including military power, the economic presence of the West and international obligations, which should emphasize Latvia's belonging to the family of European nations

[38] The military elements of this situation can be divided into two groups of tasks: those tasks that relate to peacetime and those that the NAF must perform in a state of war

[39] As a basic principle, it should be emphasized that Latvia's political and military actions must not create the impression of submission under any circumstances

[40] The peacetime tasks (M) of the NAF for the protection of the state and the population are: M

[41] To participate in creating a deterrent factor, demonstrating the NAF's determination and capabilities to protect the entire territory of Latvia: - to maintain a small and well-trained rapid reaction force capable of resisting an unexpected attack or armed border crossing; - to conduct relevant training; - to conduct reconnaissance tasks; - to demonstrate readiness to mobilize; - to prepare to receive reinforcements, cooperating to support those friendly countries and/or organizations that would send reinforcements; - to protect and secure military facilities

[42] M2: To protect the border, territory, maritime waters and airspace of Latvia: - to ensure the inviolability of the land territory of Latvia; - to ensure the inviolability of the maritime waters and airspace of Latvia; - to conduct surveillance in cooperation with civilian institutions and friendly countries; - to detect and respond to intentional violations of the border, territory, maritime waters and airspace of Latvia

[43] Support the civilian authorities in crisis management in accordance with the procedure established by law: - prevent the violent overthrow of the legitimate government and the change of the state system established in the Constitution; - be ready to use the NAF as a means of crisis management; - acquire skills in incident containment; - help prevent the deterioration of the situation; - respond to requests for assistance in combating terrorism

[44] M4: Support the broader security interests of Latvia by participating in international activities: - participate in peacekeeping and humanitarian aid operations within the framework of the UN or OSCE; - jointly with the other Baltic States, support international security with the assistance of BALTBAT; - participate to the maximum extent possible in the NATO program "Partnership for Peace

[45] M5: Undertake other tasks in the interests of the Latvian government and population: - participate in civil protection measures, liquidation of the consequences of natural disasters and major technological accidents; - perform search and rescue measures using naval and air force units; - perform NAF and government transport tasks; - jointly with civilian institutions, carry out ecological monitoring and protection; - provide personnel for guards of honour and other ceremonies

[46] Protection of the State and the Population in Time of War The wartime tasks (K) of the NAF are: K1: Immediately establish the fact of armed aggression: - protect the entire border; - resist any cross-border attack on the Republic of Latvia with armed force

[47] Protect crucially important objects: - those few objects that are crucially important to Latvia for political or practical reasons must be persistently protected for as long as possible

[48] K3: Immediately repay the enemy for aggression: - defend or destroy objects suitable for invasion, such as airfields and landing zones; - block, repel and delay the movement of the enemy; - fight where the greatest losses can be inflicted on the attacker with the least damage to oneself; - avoid decisive battles

[49] K4: If the attack cannot be repelled, conduct sustained, convincing resistance: - divide into small groups and continue local resistance with the actions of small units; - small units must be able to fight independently, exploiting the aggressor's weaknesses, even if the command structure is not functioning; - prevent the invader from having freedom of action in the occupied territory; - attract as many enemy forces as possible; - continuously inflict losses on the aggressor; - ensure sustained organized and armed resistance

[50] Ensuring the legitimate government: - assist in the deployment and possible evacuation of the most important components of the government; - ensure the legitimate government's communications with citizens, world leaders and the international press; - protect the legitimate government

[83] Full-time forces and compulsory service While some NAF tasks require well-trained, professional soldiers who are always at a high level of readiness, many other tasks require compulsory service units, which play an equally important role in the defense of Latvia

[90] Primarily, soldiers trained by the NAF should be prepared for mobilization in order to develop Latvian military practice and ethics and not rely on the methods of the former Soviet Army

[91] This means that there will be few reservists at first, but their number will increase along with the NAF's ability to arm and supply reservists

[92] Equipment Weapons should meet the needs of the NAF's tactical doctrine: mainly "hit and run" tactics

[93] The acquisition policy for the next five years will be: to continue using Soviet-era weapons and fill in the gaps, but to do everything possible to align with NATO standards in electronic, especially communications, means

[94] Small arms and battalion-level support weapons Soviet-era small arms should be used initially, as they are cheaper, more accessible and ammunition is cheaper

[95] In particular, the following should be procured: Short-range light anti-tank weapons

[96] Medium-range guided anti-tank weapons

[97] Low-level anti-aircraft weapons: machine guns and missiles

[98] Light and medium mortars

[99] Heavy weapons Heavy weapons units (e.g. tanks and artillery) are too complex to train, too expensive to maintain, and unjustifiably vulnerable to a militarily advanced, air-dominant adversary

[100] An exception might be the 100 mm guns donated by the Czech Republic, which could be used as anti-tank or coast guard weapons

[101] Communications Everything possible should be done to ensure that all NAF communications systems are able to cooperate with the other Baltic states and with NATO

[102] Equally important is the national communications plan for crisis management and ensuring communications of the legitimate government in the event of aggression

[103] Mobility and armor Initially, the mobility of the MSBr should be ensured by providing one full battalion with unarmored wheeled vehicles

[104] In the future, the ideal would be to provide the entire brigade with armored wheeled transport - if finances allow

[105] The following sequence should be observed

[111] Air mobility In order to be able to quickly move to any part of Latvia, SUVs and MSBr need air mobility

[112] Initially, it is enough to be able to move one platoon in full combat readiness to any point in Latvia in a short time

[113] Staffing New staffing should be prepared based on the real, not the intended, composition
