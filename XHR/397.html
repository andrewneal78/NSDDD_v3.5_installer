[980] Countering state threats: defence, disruption and deterrence State threats to the UK, and to our allies, are growing and diversifying as systemic competition intensifies

[981] States are becoming increasingly assertive in how they advance their own objectives and in their willingness to undermine ours

[1540] Integrated Review Annex B: Evidence and engagement This annex sets out our approach to building the evidence base for the Integrated Review

[1541] The ambition was to root the review in the best available evidence, analysis and expertise, drawing on a diverse range of high-quality sources to create a foreign policy baseline; taking key partners’ and stakeholders’ views into consideration; and embedding evidence and rigour throughout the strategy development process

[1542] To achieve this ambition, we undertook a systematic programme of engagement, analysis and challenge

[1543] We established new processes and products where these were needed to address gaps and better inform decision-making

[1544] Lessons learned during the process will inform our future approach to engagement and our use of evidence

[1545] Approach This programme was designed to support each stage of the strategy development and drafting process

[1546] This involved working with government departments and agencies, the UK’s overseas networks, and experts, practitioners and partners from across the globe

[1547] In particular, we sought to: Identify and understand the established and emergent trends in the domestic and international system

[1548] • Highlight what the UK could and should learn from other countries and from the private sector

[1549] • Clarify the strengths and weaknesses of the UK’s current policies, processes, systems and capabilities, and identify what needed to change

[1550] New mechanisms were established to integrate efforts across government so that the Review benefited from the full range of expertise and resources available, including extensive domestic and overseas networks and research and assessment capabilities

[1551] Three programmes of activity were developed: engagement; analysis and assessment; and challenge and assurance

[1552] Engagement We designed engagement to bring in different perspectives and policy ideas from across the UK and around the world

[1553] We adapted the programme in light of COVID-19 to make full use of online platforms and we issued a public call for evidence

[1554] the Cabinet Office and Foreign and Commonwealth Office, and later the FCDO, designed and delivered a virtual international engagement programme to which heads of mission, ministers and senior officials contributed

[1555] In particular, senior officials – including the Deputy National Security Adviser for the Integrated Review and the Prime Minister’s Special Adviser on Foreign Policy – held more than 100 engagements with over 20 countries across six continents, with a particular focus on key allies and partners

[1556] With support from Annex B: Evidence and engagement Wilton Park, we convened 22 young leaders and emerging experts from 14 different countries to better understand young people’s perspectives on, and ambitions for, the future

[1557] Departmental engagement: government departments and agencies consulted networks and stakeholder groups across the UK and the world to inform inputs to the Integrated Review

[1558] For example, the FCDO established a group of experts and hosted roundtables on key themes and GO-Science consulted established networks such as the UK Science and Innovation Network (SIN) to coordinate a range of inputs from external experts

[1559] Chief Scientific Advisers across government also played an important role in improving the standards of evidence use throughout the Integrated Review

[1560] This included support from the FCDO Research and Evidence Division (RED) to review the Integrated Review evidence base

[1561] Public engagement: gaining perspectives from the public was vital to informing the Integrated Review, although the process was necessarily restricted due to COVID

[1562] New research was conducted to understand public perceptions of foreign policy and national security at home and overseas

[1563] On 13 August 2020, we launched a public call for evidence

[1564] Contributions were invited on a range of security, defence, development and foreign policy questions

[1565] We received more than 450 submissions from individuals and organisations

[1566] Organisational respondents included representatives of a range of industries, non-governmental organisations, international organisations, academia, think tanks, local government, LRFs, the media, religious organisations and fire and police organisations

[1567] We reviewed and catalogued the submissions, which we then shared with policy-makers and senior officials across government

[1568] Parliamentary engagement: we made extensive use of reports by the relevant parliamentary committees

[1569] During the Integrated Review process, officials briefed the House of Commons Select Committees on Defence, Foreign Affairs and International Development

[1570] The Prime Minister gave evidence to the Liaison Committee on the Integrated Review

[1571] The Foreign Secretary and Defence Secretary also gave evidence to the relevant departmental select committees, the then-National Security Adviser gave evidence to the Joint Committee on the National Security Strategy, and Defence Minister (Lords) led briefing sessions for members of the House of Lords. Experts and practitioners: with support from the Government’s Open Innovation Team and Wilton Park, we engaged academics and experts from more than 30 different institutions and 20 countries
