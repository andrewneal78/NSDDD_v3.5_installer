[515] We must transform our intelligence capabilities and build new ones to keep pace with the nature of these threats

[516] Intelligence must be appropriately integrated with our defense and law enforcement systems and coordinated with our allies and friends

[517] We need to protect the capabilities we have so that we do not arm our enemies with the knowledge of how best to surprise us

[518] Those who would harm us also seek the benefit of surprise to limit our prevention and response options and to maximize injury

[519] We must strengthen intelligence warning and analysis to provide integrated threat assessments for national and homeland security

[520] Since the threats inspired by foreign governments and groups may be conducted inside the United States, we must also ensure the proper fusion of information between intelligence and law enforcement

[521] Initiatives in this area will include: - strengthening the authority of the Director of Central Intelligence to lead the development and actions of the Nation’s foreign intelligence capabilities; - establishing a new framework for intelligence warning that provides seamless and integrated warning across the spectrum of threats facing the nation and our allies; - continuing to develop new methods of collecting information to sustain our intelligence advantage; - investing in future capabilities while working to protect them through a more vigorous effort to prevent the compromise of intelligence capabilities; and - collecting intelligence against the terrorist danger across the government with all-source analysis

[522] As the United States Government relies on the armed forces to defend America’s interests, it must rely on diplomacy to interact with other nations

[523] We will ensure that the Department of State receives funding sufficient to ensure the success of American diplomacy

[524] The State Department takes the lead in managing our bilateral relationships with other governments

[525] And in this new era, its National Security Strategy people and institutions must be able to interact equally adroitly with non-governmental organiza-tions and international institutions

[526] Officials trained mainly in international politics must also extend their reach to understand complex issues of domestic governance around the world, including public health, education, law enforcement, the judiciary, and public diplomacy

[527] Our diplomats serve at the front line of complex negotiations, civil wars, and other humanitarian catastrophes

[528] As humanitarian relief requirements are better understood, we must also be able to help build police forces, court systems, and legal codes, local and provincial government institutions, and electoral systems

[529] Effective international cooperation is needed to accomplish these goals, backed by American readiness to play our part

[530] Just as our diplomatic institutions must adapt so that we can reach out to others, we also need a different and more comprehensive approach to public information efforts that can help people around the world learn about and understand America

[531] The war on terrorism is not a clash of civilizations

[532] It does, however, reveal the clash inside a civilization, a battle for the future of the Muslim world

[533] This is a struggle of ideas and this is an area where America must excel

[534] We will take the actions necessary to ensure that our efforts to meet our global security commitments and protect Americans are not impaired by the potential for investigations, inquiry, or prosecution by the International Criminal Court (ICC), whose jurisdiction does not extend to Americans and which we do not accept

[535] We will work together with other nations to avoid complications in our military operations and cooperation, through such mechanisms as multilateral and bilateral agreements that will protect U.S. nationals from the ICC

[536] We will implement fully the American Servicemembers Protection Act, whose provisions are intended to ensure and enhance the protection of U. S. personnel and officials

[537] We will make hard choices in the coming year and beyond to ensure the right level and allocation of government spending on national security

[538] The United States Government must strengthen its defenses to win this war

[539] At home, our most important priority is to protect the homeland for the American people

[540] Today, the distinction between domestic and foreign affairs is diminishing

[541] In a globalized world, events beyond America’s borders have a greater impact inside them

[542] Our society must be open to people, ideas, and goods from across the globe

[543] The characteristics we most cherish—our freedom, our cities, our systems of movement, and modern life—are vulnerable to terrorism

[544] This vulnerability will persist long after we bring to justice those responsible for the September 11 attacks

[545] As time passes, individuals may gain access to means of destruction that until now could be wielded only by armies, fleets, and squadrons

[546] This is a new condition of life

[547] We will adjust to it and thrive—in spite of it

[548] In exercising our leadership, we will respect the values, judgment, and interests of our friends and partners

[549] Still, we will be prepared to act apart when our interests and unique responsibilities require

[550] When we disagree on particulars, we will explain forthrightly the grounds for our concerns and strive to forge viable alternatives

[551] We will not allow such disagreements to obscure our determination to secure together, with our allies and our friends, our shared fundamental interests and values

[552] Ultimately, the foundation of American strength is at home

[553] It is in the skills of our people, the dynamism of our economy, and the resilience of our institutions

[554] A diverse, modern society has inherent, ambitious, entrepreneurial energy

[555] Our strength comes from what we do with that energy

[556] That is where our national security begins
