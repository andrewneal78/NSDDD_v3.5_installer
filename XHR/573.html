[22] Functions of Defense Chapter IV: Defense Policy Defense Policy as a State policy is a set of guidelines that tend towards the preservation of the State and interpret the national interests and objectives set forth in the Political Constitution of the Republic, in the context of national security, undertaking actions, as well as allocating the necessary resources to achieve such objectives and anticipating the threats that the State may face

[23] For the development of the Defense Policy, specific policies are derived from it, one of which is the Military Policy

[24] The National Defense Policy is the result of a broad participatory process, the content of which is based on multisectoral input, taking into account our reality as a multiethnic, pluricultural, and multilingual nation. It is part of the dynamics of democratization that emphasize the State-society relationship. This is based on discussions aimed at building consensus, making transparent the aspects of defense organization, management, and financing in order to achieve citizen support

[25] This result provides support for the Guatemalan National Defense Book, making it available to society as a whole, an essential requirement for its legitimacy, while also constituting an instrument for mutual trust measures within the framework of international relations

[26] Basis of Defense Policy To develop matters relating to the defense of sovereignty and territorial integrity, the political regime and the social regime

[27] Defense Policy is based on the Political Constitution of the Republic, and on the treaties and agreements signed by the Guatemalan State

[28] Thus, its formulation and implementation are based on adherence to the basic principles of peaceful dispute resolution, as well as the strengthening of peace, democracy, cooperation, integration, and international security. It also relies on the right to self-defense, as recognized in Article 51 of the United Nations Charter and in accordance with Article 24 of the Charter of the Organization of American States and Article 43 of the Framework Treaty for Democratic Security in Central America on Collective Security and Defense

[29] To implement this policy, a National Defense Law is required, which defines the structure, functions, and interinstitutional relations as a comprehensive vision, considering the levels of incorporation and interaction between the State and society

[30] This regulation must be accompanied by a review and adaptation methodology, allowing for institutional modernization and a reassessment of threats, in order to maintain constant readiness

[31] Purposes of the Defense Policy The publication and dissemination of the Defense Policy is aimed at consolidating knowledge about Defense and its interrelation with national life, raising awareness for the defense of the Nation

[32] Likewise, by methodologically undertaking a set of coordinated and planned measures based on a conception of the State, the goal is to achieve optimal national defense that contributes to national security, conceiving and executing the strategies necessary to confront forceful actions against the State, establishing conditions for the realization of the National Objectives

[33] At the same time, in line with international instruments and mechanisms, Defense Policy contributes to Foreign Policy in generating Confidence-Building Measures

[34] Characteristics of the Defense Policy As a State policy, it is of a national nature

[35] It responds to the country's needs and is based on national power, being legal, legitimate, and consistent with national development, under the political leadership of civilians and military personnel in the fields relevant to each, in accordance with actions in the field of international relations

[37] In addition, it considers mechanisms to anticipate, prevent, and address threats and new defense challenges

[38] It is formulated in a precise and understandable manner, to be assimilated and accepted by heterogeneous elements through reasoned conviction

[40] Defense Policy is the national attitude toward the current situation, defining risks and threats, considering the possibility of alliances, and assuming positions of neutrality and non-aggression

[41] At the same time, it considers, as a tool for achieving the objectives of the Defense, the corresponding strategies and mechanisms for the creation, support, and maintenance of the force

[99] The Mobilizable Reserve includes all citizens aged thirty to fifty-five who have performed military service in the Permanent Force or completed their training in Military Reserve units

[115] Institutionalization The process of institutionalization of the Guatemalan Army is linked to the constitutional documents that have governed the country throughout its history

[116] In the reforms to state institutions, embodied in these constitutions, the security and defense functions of the custodian of the nation's weapons: the Armed Forces, have been included without major changes

[117] They have also considered the functions of cooperation with other institutions in cases of natural or man-made disasters

[118] As part of this process, we can mention the 1825 Constitution, which established a Public Force, composed of a permanent force, an active militia, and a civic force, with functions of external defense for the Federal Republic and functions of safeguarding the internal order of the State

[119] Later, during the second half of the 19th century, following the Revolution of 1871, changes were made towards the professionalization of the Army, with the creation of the Polytechnic School in 1873, to train officers with knowledge of the technology of the time, to be employed by the State as civil engineers or surveyors, a profession that did not exist at that time

[120] In 1945, the armed forces were reaffirmed as their responsibility to guarantee independence, internal and external order and security, as well as to defend the territory; they were also assigned specific cooperation tasks in communications projects, reforestation, and increasing agricultural production

[121] By 1954, a new Constitution established the name "Guatemala Army," retaining responsibility for territorial defense and internal and external security

[122] Likewise, regarding the cooperation that must be provided, it is indicated that it will be in situations of emergency or public calamity or in works and activities of national utility

[123] The 1965 Constitution recognizes that the Army "is unique and indivisible, essentially apolitical and non-deliberative; it is composed of land, air, and sea forces." This was reiterated in the 1985 Constitution and constitutes the current foundation of the Army's institutionality

[124] Currently, the Political Constitution of the Republic defines the composition, organization, and purposes of the Army; in addition, there are treaties and agreements that outline the assignment of tasks to the armed forces of the region, and there are trends toward modifying these missions and functions

[125] Purposes and Functions of the Army The Political Constitution of the Republic of Guatemala, in article 244 establishes: "The Army of Guatemala is an institution destined to maintain the independence, sovereignty and honor of Guatemala, the integrity of the territory, peace and internal and external security

[126] In addition to assigning the aforementioned mission, the constitutional text reaffirms the validity of the principles that have given the Army consistency: apoliticality, obedience, discipline, and non-deliberation, emphasizing its essentially professional, unique, and indivisible nature

[127] Additionally, Article 249 of the Constitution establishes that the Army will provide its cooperation in situations of emergency or public calamity

[128] The performance of these functions is regulated by the Constitutional Law on Public Order, which is aimed at maintaining security, public order, and the stability of state institutions. This law applies in cases of invasion of national territory, serious disturbance of the peace, public calamity, or activities against state security

[129] The constitutional mandate is developed in the Constitutive Law of the Guatemalan Army, which, as a substantive element, contains the Fundamental Principles, as well as the regulatory framework under which the institution is organized and operates

[130] Organization and Deployment of the Guatemalan Army

[131] Organization It is organized under the concept of a modern, professional, permanent, defensive, mobile Army, adapted to the national reality, efficient and flexible, composed of Land, Air and Sea Forces as well as their reserves, trained, organized and equipped, capable of being mobilized in a short time to replace the permanent force; so as to efficiently guarantee the defense and security of the State of Guatemala, in theaters of operations, that respond to a war hypothesis, and other assigned tasks

[132] Subordinate to civil power

[133] The Army recognizes cooperation, integration, and dispute resolution through dialogue and negotiation as mechanisms

[137] The Army's defensive strategic orientation is based on the ability to maneuver jointly among its forces and the potential for carrying out combined operations with other armed forces

[138] For the operational leadership of the Army, the Chief of the National Defense Staff is responsible to the Minister of National Defense for the command, organization, training, education, discipline, conduct, as well as the tactical and strategic employment of the Land, Air, and Naval Forces to maintain the readiness of the Guatemalan Army. As the Army's technical and advisory center, it is comprised of the Directorates of Personnel, Intelligence, Operations, Logistics, and Civil Affairs, and makes assessments and develops recommendations to advise the Minister of National Defense

[139] Based on decisions issued by the Ministry of National Defense, the General Staff prepares plans and orders and is responsible for their transmission and supervision, taking the necessary measures to ensure compliance

[140] In addition, it coordinates the planning and execution of national mobilization and demobilization plans

[141] The General Regulations of the Defense Staff determine the doctrinal foundations, organization, and working procedures of the Staff, as the Army's technical and advisory center to advise the Minister of National Defense

[142] Supporting it are the Army Inspectorate General; the Logistics Support Command (CAL), the Education and Doctrine Command (CEDOC), and the Military Reserves Command; and the Special General Staff

[143] Additionally, he commands the Army's Strategic Units, derived from the previous forces, these being: the Special Forces Command "Kaibil", the Paratroopers Military Base "General Felipe Cruz" and the Marine Infantry Command "Commander Brígido Valderrama

[144] The Forces' Deployment In order to maximize effectiveness, resources, and the human factor, the national territory is divided into 7 military regions, 3 air regions, and 2 naval regions, adopting the names: Regional Commands, Naval Commands, Air Commands, Military Zones; with units to create a defense in all directions and in depth, to achieve mutual support, security, the exploitation of fire support, and unity of command

[145] The plan aims to have a professional Army, with organization and deployment based on mission accomplishment, considering: the development of forces, as well as the timely deployment of forces depending on threats, incorporating new technology, updating communication and combat means, in accordance with the national reality and the assigned missions

[152] CHAPTER IX: GROUND FORCES The Ground Forces of the Guatemalan Army make up its largest component and operate in the continental space of the national territory

[153] The armed forces have basically been made up of infantry, artillery, and cavalry units, which is why the history of the Guatemalan Army is closely associated with that of its ground forces, which, despite their diverse names, have always existed

[154] Creation The Land Forces are the oldest component of the Guatemalan Army and have evolved based on the needs of the State

[155] In the mid-19th century, General Rafael Carrera promoted it with his victory in the Battle of San José La Arada, fought on February 2, 1851, a date commemorated as the day of these forces

[156] Mission Its fundamental mission is, in the terrestrial sphere, the deterrence and development of war, as well as the organization of the defense of the Nation for the preservation of sovereignty, independence and territorial integrity

[157] Combat Units They are made up of infantry and cavalry units, which are the elements destined to carry out tactical maneuvers on the battlefield, and must possess the characteristics of mobility, firepower, protection and means of communication

[158] Infantry units are tasked with engaging in close combat with the enemy and defeating them with fire, maneuver, and shock effects

[159] Cavalry units are tasked with attacking, disrupting, and defeating enemy forces through fire, maneuver, and shock effects

[160] Combat Support Units These units are tasked with providing direct support to maneuver units, fulfilling both combat support and fire support functions. These include: artillery units for fire support to maneuver units; engineering units for combat support and combat services; military communications units for the installation, operation, and maintenance of strategic communications systems; and Military Police units at Headquarters

[161] Combat Service Support Units By their nature, combat service support units are essentially administrative and are intended to support combat forces

[162] Military Health Units cover the areas for health maintenance, leaving the recovery and rehabilitation areas to the Military Medical Center and Type "A Infirmaries" for the military regions

[163] It also has units responsible for war material, ammunition, military equipment and clothing

[164] Land Reserve As part of the Nation's military power, the Land Reserve is the component of the Military Reserves that are organized to act in land space in support of the permanent force with the purpose of allowing the exploitation of success, as well as going to places threatened by the adversary

[165] Capabilities The Land Forces are capable of conducting land warfare jointly with naval and air forces to defend Guatemalan territory, support civil security forces in maintaining internal security, cooperate with other institutions in the event of an emergency or public calamity, and carry out mobilization and demobilization with the support of other institutions

[172] There are two aviation training schools: the Military Aviation School, for the training and professionalization of fixed-wing and rotary-wing pilots; and the Military Technical Aviation School, for the training of airmen

This transport focuses on activities related to health, education, security, and support for the population in the event of natural disasters and forest fires

[175] At the same time, it carries out rescues and evacuations, and participates, along with other entities, in controlling the destruction of the Mayan Biosphere and in detecting and intercepting illegal flights

[191] Operational Units

[192] Squadron This unit is the operational organization par excellence and comprises the Surface Force of the Naval Forces, representing the naval power of the State

[193] Its mission is to conduct naval operations of safeguarding, presence, and diplomacy, as well as to conquer, contest, and exercise control of the sea, with the goal of exercising dominion over jurisdictional aquatic spaces

[194] In addition, through the exercise of the authority of the Naval Police, it must contribute to guaranteeing the sovereignty, respect for the laws, and maritime development of the Nation

[195] At the international level, he is assigned to participate in multinational peacekeeping and humanitarian support operations

[196] It is currently called the "Naval Tactical Squadron" and is oriented towards naval safeguarding

[197] Marine Infantry Command Under the direct command of the Chief of Staff of the National Defense, it is an integral unit of the Naval Forces, organized, equipped and trained to carry out missions of projection of naval power towards land

[198] Its use is strategic and temporary throughout the national territory

[199] Its mission is to conduct amphibious, riverine, riverine, and special operations in theaters of operations of national interest to contribute to the mission of the Naval Forces, in times of peace and war, within the Military Strategic Concept

[200] Naval Police Corps. This corps constitutes the legal arm of the Naval Forces in maritime spaces, port and naval facilities, where their operations depend on the exploration or use of aquatic environments, as well as on ships or vessels within their national waters

[201] Its mission is to ensure compliance with maritime legislation in aquatic spaces where the State exercises sovereignty and jurisdiction. It also applies military laws and regulations within the jurisdiction of the Naval Commands and provides physical security to installations, vessels, and personalities, with the purpose of contributing to the fulfillment of the mission of the Naval Forces

[202] Capabilities From the missions stated above, the tasks for the fulfillment of the general mission and the specific missions are deduced, and these can be grouped into naval tasks of defense, diplomacy and safeguarding, carrying out military and non-military operations in each of the areas described and which constitute a triangle of missions

[203] Naval Defense is tasked with conducting military activity against formal opponents of the State and involves war actions for the military defense of the Nation, in addition to the execution of non-combat operations (MOOTW

[204] The task of Naval Safeguard or Coast Guard is characterized by the use of naval resources to enforce national and international maritime laws, conventions, and treaties, to which the State is a party. This is carried out by the National Defense Navy since its creation, through the Harbor Commanders and Captaincies, the Navy, and the Naval Police

[205] Finally, in the field of naval diplomacy, the National Defense Navy is the armed wing of Guatemala's foreign policy, through the presence of its vessels beyond national borders, due to their extraterritorial nature and as ambassadors and bearers of the policies of the state they represent

[206] The framework of the modernization of the State emphasizes strengthening the institutions and the organization of the public sector, both in the aspects of promoting economic, social and political development, as well as in the state institutions in charge of security

[207] To achieve this, reengineering structures and processes and allocating resources are of vital importance

[208] Understanding modernization as the pursuit of improvement and rationalization of administrative systems, the modernization of functions, scientific and technological advancement, and the training of qualified human resources, it follows that strengthening the defense capacity of the State of Guatemala implies strengthening processes oriented toward the aforementioned areas

[209] For military professionals, this requires a necessary level of readiness to fulfill assigned missions, the renewal of weapons and equipment, as well as an updated military justice system, within the framework of their doctrine

[210] The global trend is to reduce the number of personnel in the armed forces, but at the same time, states are making great efforts to fill the gaps left by human beings by replacing them with technology. This is an inversely proportional process: the fewer the personnel, the more technology

[211] Based on the above, a key point in the quest for modernization is the training of human resources

[212] The military profession is one that considers specialized knowledge and whose mission is the use of armed forces, as well as their support

[213] For this reason, the Army command emphasizes the education of its human resources to compensate for and overcome technological shortcomings with the ingenuity and quality of its members, both military personnel and civilians who serve in the military institution

[214] The above is reflected in the Personnel Manual, TE-01-2, which states that “Personnel Administration: is the form or forms of organizing and employing individuals for the best use of their qualities or skills, in order to obtain their maximum efficiency and cooperation for the benefit of themselves and the group as a whole,” which requires an educational process at different levels

[215] The Guatemalan Army has a system of education, training, and instruction for the understanding, application, and standardization of Military Doctrine, unifying the practice of military sciences (land, naval, and air) and instilling the institutional principles and ethical and moral values necessary to fulfill the constitutional mission and thus contribute to national development

[216] Military Education The educational process includes the infrastructure and resources for vocational training, professionalization, technical training, and military training for the personnel assigned to command, administration, personnel management, and maintenance of material and operations of each force

[217] The aforementioned process is composed of three levels of education: Vocational, Training, and Professionalization

[218] Likewise, these are reinforced by two processes: Training and Military Training

[219] Vocational Training The officer's educational process can begin at the Adolfo V. Hall Institutes of the Republic, where the young adolescent, upon graduating with a Bachelor of Science and Letters or an Agricultural Expert degree, obtains the rank of Second Lieutenant in the Reserves, to subsequently continue his professional career, whether military or university

[220] Training The Polytechnic School, a 130-year-old Military Academy, is where the majority of ground force officers are trained, and a small percentage are trained in foreign military academies

[221] In addition to providing military training, it provides university education to Male Cadets and, since 1997, to Female Cadets, who upon completing four years of training graduate as second lieutenants in the Army's armed forces or services, in addition to obtaining a Bachelor's Degree in Resource and Technology Management

[222] The Polytechnic School is also responsible for providing basic training to the Cadets of the Military Aviation School (EMA), the Air Force, and the Guatemalan Naval School (ENG), and the Naval Forces

[223] Basic training is carried out as a two-year common area, followed by specializations in the academies of each of the other forces

[224] Professionalization of Officer Personnel The Center for Advanced Studies of National Defense is responsible for the professionalization of officers in the Land Forces, which is complemented by the Naval School for naval officers and the Military Aviation School for Air Force officers

[225] During their military career, officers must pass the Basic Course in their respective branch or service, as well as the Advanced Weapons Course and the Command and Staff Course, and optionally obtain a Master's degree in Resource and Technology Management

[226] In addition to the above, officers have the option of attending various specialized courses, both in the country and abroad, that complement military professionalization

[227] The School of Advanced Strategic Studies and the Higher School of National Defense are currently in their implementation phase

[228] Training of Specialist Personnel The specialist is the technical, specialized and operational personnel of the forces

[229] The skills and abilities required for this personnel are distributed across nearly 600 occupational specialties, identified in the Military Occupational Specialty Manual (MOSM

[230] Specialist staff have the option of pursuing a career within the institution and taking various training and refresher courses. They are also encouraged to attend civil education centers, complete a mid-level degree, or pursue university studies

[231] For the Air Force, Aviation Technicians (Aerotécnicos) complete their studies at the Military Technical Aviation School, obtaining a Bachelor's degree in Science and Letters or a degree in Aviation Mechanics

[232] The training, professionalization, and development of men and women, both in the Naval Forces and the merchant marine, is the responsibility of the Guatemalan Naval Academy

[233] Seamanship training is geared toward the study and improvement of seamanship and naval disciplines. This training prepares these personnel to crew operational units of the National Defense Navy, working with the systems and equipment of warships or merchant vessels. Its purpose is to ensure proper operation and optimal maintenance of these vessels

[234] Training of Troop Personnel The most numerous human resource in the forces is the troop personnel; their use varies in each force, focusing on their participation as a combat element

[235] The modernization process increasingly requires trained and technically qualified personnel; therefore, as a contribution to young people performing their military service and to Guatemalan society in general, the Army established the Comprehensive Soldier Education Program. Its main objective is to transform soldiers during their voluntary military service into better-trained citizens, providing added value to these individuals when they reintegrate into their community at the end of their military service

[236] The Doctrine The professional aptitude of armies lies in their doctrine, which is the guide for their forces to carry out war and non-war operations, drawing on lessons from the past, reflecting the nature of war and conflict in their own time, and anticipating the intellectual and technological advances that will make success possible

[237] Definition Military doctrine is the set of basic concepts, general principles, processes, values and standards of behavior that systematize and coordinate the activities of the Army to fulfill its constitutional mission

[238] The Guatemalan Army's doctrine establishes the foundation for its preparation and use of force, with a central strategy aimed at strengthening dialogue and seeking consensus

[239] Axiologically, it encompasses the set of values that underpin society and the principles that identify Democracy, Liberty, and the Rule of Law, as embodied in the Political Constitution of the Republic

[240] Operationally, it includes military strategy, operational strategy, tactics, logistics and leadership, as well as the planning and conduct of military operations in times of war or peace

[241] Basic Criteria for the Formulation of Doctrine The sources for the formulation of Military Doctrine are diverse: the National Defense Strategy defined by the Defense Policy is one of them; as are the ethical, historical, and cultural values of the Nation, combined with operational concepts, including technology and the Principles of War

[242] Other factors include the country's scientific and technological status, its military budget, the training of its armed forces, and available human resources

[243] In addition to the above, Foreign Policy, in conjunction with Defense Policy, influences doctrine by ensuring international support and determining military participation in accordance with international security mechanisms for maintaining, building, or restoring peace, as well as the Army's participation in comprehensive cooperation operations at the regional level

[244] For its part, the threat perception defined in the Defense Policy influences the Army's doctrine and training activities, which must be prepared during peacetime to deter, combat, and control threats that turn into conflicts or war with appropriate means

[245] Finally, and derived from Defense Policy, joint action at the national level or interoperability in the international framework, coordinated and combined operation with other armies, in the necessary proportion of land, naval, and air forces, influence the Doctrine

[246] The development of the isthmus' integration could lead to the formation of a Central American force with effective and complementary actions

[247] Education and Doctrine Command. The Guatemalan Army's educational system is a vital component in the development of positive and modern leadership. Its governing body is the Education and Doctrine Command (CEDOC), created in 1998 with the mission of governing, establishing, and updating policies and strategies regarding Army doctrine and education, establishing the educational and doctrinal philosophy for its formulation and development at different levels, orienting it according to the needs and projections of the armed institution

[248] Its organization is divided into three main directorates: the Directorate of Science and Technology, the Directorate of Doctrine, and the Directorate of Education. Resources Defense, as a necessary function for the survival of society in general, demands resources of all kinds, which are used to satisfy the requirements or needs related to the Defense of the Nation, with the purpose of safeguarding the sovereignty and integrity of its territory and the security of its inhabitants

[249] Defense resources as a public good refer to human, economic, financial, and material resources, which come primarily from the State's current revenues and additionally from external cooperation

[250] Human resources, among others, include those assigned to fulfill the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' role as the first line of defense, as well as those members of the permanent force and defense reserves, linked to the Ministry of National Defense

[251] For its part, the Ministry of Public Finance allocates financial resources for Defense through the General Budget of the Nation, approved by the Legislative Branch, which are obtained from the State's Current Revenue

[252] The Ministry of National Defense is responsible for managing the financial resources allocated for military defense

[253] It is also responsible for producing material resources for defense through the Military Industry, the Munitions Factory, and the War Material Service, among others

[254] On the other hand, there are activities carried out by non-governmental organizations that directly or indirectly support the Defense through programs and projects, among which is the protection of the ecological environment

[255] Furthermore, the private sector, in some cases, produces inputs used in the defense industry

[256] Defense Policy: Financing Components The design of a financing policy within a congruent Defense Policy comprises a fixed and a variable component: The fixed component of the Defense budget is based on the percentage of GDP for the fulfillment of the constitutional mission

[257] The variable component is determined by the legislature for each year or for each government period, subject to change based on emergency or other circumstances that require extraordinary expenditures or budget cuts

[258] This amount of resources is linked to transitional functions or new competencies supported by State policies and their adaptation to the changing needs of development

[259] Investment in Defense as part of National Security In general terms, investment in Defense refers to the resources necessary to strengthen the political, economic, social and military areas of the Nation's actions, and in specific terms means the investment linked to the Ministry of National Defense, which is determined based on the analysis of macroeconomic indicators, especially fiscal ones, per capita income, gross domestic product, the general level of internal prices, among others

[260] The National Defense budget projection is based on the respective policy framework, complying with the provisions of the Fiscal Pact

[261] In any case, the resources allocated to Defense must not exceed the limits that could put pressure on macroeconomic stability and must be in line with the indicators established for each fiscal year, in relation to the country's level of domestic and foreign debt

[262] Investment in defense is a component of public spending intended to contribute to the security and stability of the nation, constituting one of the conditions for achieving economic and social well-being

[263] Therefore, it must be interrelated with the actions of the various public administration bodies, primarily the education, health, and national infrastructure sectors, to achieve national development

[264] Therefore, it is consistent with the priorities of government policies in social matters and with the provisions of the Peace Accords

[265] On this account, defense spending is set at 0.66% of GDP in accordance with commitment 63.c

[266] of the Agreement on Strengthening Civil Power and the Role of the Army in a Democratic Society, has become, in order of priority, less than that of education and health, which is illustrated in the following graph

[267] To fulfill the constitutional mission, the authorities responsible for National Defense must direct spending based on criteria such as: Ensuring the State's deterrent power, strengthening tactical and operational capacity based on the various threats to National Security, through the defense of land, air, and sea areas, as well as through Military Reserves

[268] In line with the State's modernization policy, human resource training and development programs must be strengthened to enable them to perform various activities in a qualified and professional manner, through training schools, vocational education professionalization centers, and military instruction and training, both in Guatemala and abroad

[269] In addition, consider the demands for support from other institutions linked to emerging issues

[270] Defense expenditures include: labor costs, operations and maintenance, acquisition of military equipment, research and development, construction, pension funds, military attachés (land, naval, and air), public relations programs, health and education institutions, intelligence, radio communications and civic programs, and contributions to international military institutions such as the Conference of Central American Armed Forces (CFAC) and the Conference of American Armies (CEA), among others

[271] Controls for Defense Spending In relation to the definition and conceptualization of the controls described in previous chapters, for the case of the supervision of Defense spending, the following State Controls are considered

[272] External Controls Defense spending is a political decision and has its respective controls, with the Legislative Branch being responsible for approving, modifying, or disapproving the State Revenue and Expenditure Budget

[273] In this case, the Executive Branch sends the draft budget to Congress, which may be modified or adjusted by the same body to meet the objectives outlined in the Political Constitution of the Republic

[274] The Congress of the Republic, in accordance with its Organic Law, through the Technical Support Commission, may verify programmatic consistency in order to seek transparency in the programming and execution of the State Budget. Representatives, and in particular each of the committees that oversee and supervise it, may also request information on defense spending and, in any case, support or oppose initiatives to address their priorities

[275] In addition, the Finance Committee monitors budget execution in this and other areas

[276] Another state oversight is exercised by the National Comptroller General's Office, which audits the General Revenue and Expenditure Budget of the Ministry of National Defense, allocated for each fiscal year

[277] This activity is carried out by a Delegation of this institution, which is permanently located in the Army Finance Department to exercise its oversight function

[278] Internal Controls The Ministry of National Defense, as the constitutional entity responsible for defense, maintains its own controls through the Military Audit Office, which oversees the Military Commands and Dependencies

[279] Likewise, the Internal Audit Office of the Army Finance Department is responsible for exercising controls over the budget execution of this ministry

[280] In turn, the Army General Inspectorate, through the Technical Control, Movable and Immovable Property Inspectorate, is responsible for overseeing the management of funds allocated to the various military departments and reporting to the National Defense General Staff on the results of these allocations

[281] Determining the allocation of defense resources must respond to the priorities demanded by society, taking into account the mission, as well as agreed-upon State policies based on a solid system of principles and flexible enough to adapt to the imperatives of a changing world

[282] By establishing defense spending targets linked to priorities agreed upon with society, public confidence in defense policy is strengthened as a contribution to the common good established in the Constitution of the Republic

[283] Spending implemented in this way expresses the convergence of the country's development plans, the possibilities of the economy, and the imperatives of threats

[284] In practice, defense spending is geared toward strengthening capabilities to conduct both military and non-traditional operations, such as supporting the restoration of public order caused by social unrest and helping to mitigate the harmful effects of natural or man-made disasters

[285] One of the Budget's main focuses is on human resource training, which includes support programs derived from national and international treaties to which the State of Guatemala is a signatory, allowing it to participate in defense cooperation programs

[286] Currently, resources from defense cooperation are allocated to the academic field, primarily for the professionalization and training of Army officers in the various specializations involved in a military career, both in Guatemala and abroad

[287] It is important to mention that, as part of the cooperation programs, Guatemalan civil society personnel have been trained in security and defense issues through various courses and seminars

[288] The training of civilian personnel is intended to strengthen the defense culture and community, to raise awareness among each of them that the defense of the Nation is not the exclusive domain of the Armed Forces, but is the responsibility of all Guatemalans

[289] Resource Requirements for Defense The allocation of resources for the implementation of programs and projects is based on the planning of the Defense budget, which, like any other State budget, is done within the established legal framework and must respond to the Nation's objectives, as outlined in the Defense Policy

[290] The acquisition of a national asset is a political decision, based on technical criteria, executed by public administrators

[291] Currently, the procedures for acquiring goods or services for the Defense are tailored to needs and are governed by directives developed by the Ministry of National Defense, which govern the use of economic, human, material, and energy resources

[292] These Directives are constantly reviewed, updated, and monitored in accordance with technological and institutional developments, taking the State Procurement and Contracting Law as a reference

[293] The Defense budget takes into account three aspects

[294] The first of these is the political aspect, which includes National Security and Defense policies, that is, the definition of the institutions involved in the implementation of Defense Policy and the threats identified by them

[295] The second is the technical aspect, which encompasses the conceptual framework, methodology, and administrative and legal characteristics of current budget legislation

[296] Finally, the financial aspect, which takes into account priorities, the State's financial capacity, proposals and demands from society, and the cost of the programs to be developed within the context of National Defense

[297] Currently, Defense resources are divided into four major programmatic categories: Defense of National Sovereignty and Territorial Integrity, Military Education, Military Health and Social Services, and Military Engineering

[298] Military Industry The production of material resources for Defense in Guatemala begins in a period of dependence on friendly developed countries, who through cooperation programs contributed with these inputs, until periods that, due to political circumstances, caused a reduction in support to provide the country with these resources

[299] This situation led to the birth and, in some cases, the strengthening of the Guatemalan military industry, with the sole purpose of obtaining the material resources necessary for the Defense of the Nation

[300] This military industry is understood as the process of developing and producing the resources necessary to implement defense, primarily producing equipment, clothing, weapons, and ammunition for use by the Army

[301] Defense Budget and Spending Overview Defense Policy includes the idea that defense spending and budget should be continually reviewed by academic and political sectors, thus promoting research and contributions on methods for evaluating resources allocated to defense within the framework of development, the economy, and threats

[302] The analytical contributions derived from the aforementioned sectors, prepared independently of the Defense system, will generate objective proposals translated into added value and decision making quality, useful for the continuous improvement of defense management

[303] In this regard, the Defense Policy considers that it is possible to diversify defense spending in three areas: One of them, Resources for National Defense, which involves all state institutions involved in defense matters

[304] A second area is the Resources for the Ministry of National Defense

[305] Finally, the third area is made up of Resources for the Guatemalan Army or Military Spending

[306] In any case, the origin and destination of resources for National Defense are not the exclusive preserve of the military; all components of society must participate in it, which will require developing national capabilities that come together to generate an adequate National Defense Policy

[307] Consequently, to strengthen democracy, the State will promote economic and financial activity aimed at meeting the needs of the population, ensuring the common good of its inhabitants, and fulfilling the Permanent Objectives outlined in the Political Constitution of the Republic

[310] It is a basic document in the development of Security and Defense policies
