[1] APPENDIX TO THE REGULATION OF THE MINISTER OF DEFENSE OF THE REPUBLIC OF INDONESIA NUMBER 12 OF 2021 CONCERNING THE POLICY FOR IMPLEMENTING STATE DEFENSE 2020-2024 **POLICY FOR IMPLEMENTING** **STATE DEFENSE 2020 –

[2] Background** Article 30 paragraph (2) of the 1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia stipulates that state defense and security efforts are implemented through the total people's defense and security system (Sishankamrata

[3] In accordance with this basis, state defense policy cannot be viewed only from a defense perspective alone, but in its management it is a unified conceptual defense and security that is complete and whole

[4] Management of the state defense system is realized through the general state defense policy stipulated by the President every five years

[5] Law Number 3 of 2002 concerning State Defense states that the General State Defense Policy is a reference for planning, implementing, and supervising the state defense system

[6] Therefore, the General National Defense Policy needs to be translated into a national defense implementation policy

[7] This national defense implementation policy is formulated as a guideline for the Ministry of Defense and the Indonesian National Armed Forces (TNI) in realizing a strong national defense, encompassing development policies, capacity building, deployment and use of force, legislation, planning and budgeting, and oversight

[188] b) Developing integrated defense information system technology (*Network Centric Warfare*) in order to achieve unity of command (*Unity of Command*) in making decisions

[189] c) Developing cyber technology capabilities capable of conducting cyber warfare through offensive and defensive cyber, monitoring, security assurance, deterrence and retaliation, infiltration, weapons and cyber intelligence

[190] Encouraging relevant ministries/institutions in developing human resources and infrastructure development in mastering defense technology engineering

[191] e) Implementing the development of space defense technology through collaboration with the National Institute of Aeronautics and Space (LAPAN), as well as other related Ministries and Institutions, through the development of communication, navigation, and *positioning* satellites; remote sensing; as well as observation and surveillance intelligence

[192] Defense Industry Development

[193] The direction of Defense Industry development is aimed at building an advanced, strong, independent, and competitive industry that can support national defense, as well as support the development of national economic growth

[194] In addition, the development of the defense industry is directed towards achieving a professional, effective, efficient and integrated defense industry

[195] Several implementation policies in the development of the defense industry are as follows: a) Implementing and encouraging related ministries/institutions to implement the provisions of laws and regulations, especially those related to the obligation to use domestic defense industry products if the domestic industry is capable of producing them

[196] b) Encouraging the national defense industry, both State-Owned Enterprises (BUMN) and Private-Owned Enterprises (BUMS) in the development of the defense industry structure, namely the main equipment industry, the main and/or supporting component industry, the component and/or supporting (supplies) industry, and the raw material industry

[197] c) Encourage the defense industry to cooperate with foreign defense industries in order to develop defense industry technology through technology transfer and knowledge transfer of defense equipment from abroad through research and development cooperation, as well as production cooperation

[198] d) Improve the technological capabilities and capabilities of the defense industry by requiring that every procurement of defense equipment from abroad be accompanied by trade reciprocity, local content and/or *offset

[199] e) Coordinate national policies in planning, formulation, implementation, control, synchronization, and evaluation in order to develop the defense industry carried out by the Defense Industry Policy Committee (KKIP

[200] Encourage the implementation of domestic and foreign promotions, in various ways, including through the holding of defense industry exhibitions

[201] Development in the Field of International Cooperation

[202] Development in the field of international cooperation in the 2020-2024 period is directed to support Indonesia as the world's maritime axis; increase defense industry cooperation in order to realize the defense industry; increase Indonesia's active role in world peace missions; increase education and training cooperation to improve the quality of human resources; increase Indonesia's leadership in ASEAN (*Association of Southeast Asian Nations*); increasing cooperation with neighboring countries that share borders; increasing regional security cooperation

[203] The implementation of international cooperation development is carried out through: a) Increasing cooperation to address common security issues, mutually contributing to conflict resolution efforts, and addressing real threats that are of common interest, including encouraging increased national defense capabilities in handling real threats

[204] b) Increasing defense industry cooperation in the *transfer of knowledge* (ToK) and *Transfer of Technology* (ToT) processes in order to realize a strong, independent, and competitive domestic defense industry

[205] c) Increasing Indonesia's active role as a member of the United Nations in world peace missions through: (1) Education and training to improve the quality of human resources in the defense sector

[206] Increasing the contribution of peacekeeping missions in various regions which are carried out through: (a) Increasing *critical enablers* needed by the mission, such as jihadist, medical, *air field support unit* or *peacekeeping intelligence* capabilities

[207] b) Increasing the number and role of *peacekeepers*, including Indonesian women as part of the main gender influence, as *military observers* and *staff officers* to contribute to increasing the effectiveness of the implementation of the mandate, including in efforts to *win the hearts and minds* of the local community, as well as assisting efforts to prevent *Sexual Exploitation and Abuse* (SEA

[208] c) Increasing the quality of equipment and supplies as well as unit capabilities in line with the commitment to strengthening world peace missions launched by the UN

[209] d) Increasing the number of Indonesian military officials who play strategic roles in peace missions at the UN *Department of Peacekeeping Operation* (DPKO), UN Headquarters New York

[210] Increasing cooperation in education and training to improve the quality of human resources in the defense sector including the exchange of teachers and students, and sending TNI personnel to participate in education and training abroad

[211] e) Increasing Indonesia's leadership in the *Association of Southeast Asian Nations* (ASEAN) by strengthening ASEAN's centrality in the defense sector through the ADMM, ADMM Plus platforms, and other security cooperation forums to build a regional security architecture in order to maintain regional security stability in Southeast Asia and the Indo-Pacific

[212] f) Increasing defense cooperation by prioritizing neighboring countries that share direct borders, countries in the *Association of Southeast Asian Nations* (ASEAN) region and the South Pacific region as well as countries that have defense cooperation with Indonesia for national interests

[213] g) Increasing regional security cooperation to maintain regional stability and security through trilateral security cooperation

[214] Capability Development Policy

[215] The national defense capability development policy is directed at maintaining, improving, and developing national defense capabilities through national resource management, utilization of national territory, and empowerment of the defense industry

[216] National defense capability development consists of military and non-military defense capability development, national defense, international cooperation, and the defense industry

[217] The implementation of this capability development is as follows: 1) **Military Defense Capability Development

[218] Military defense capability development is aimed at developing the capabilities of the main components, supported by reserve components and supporting components within the framework of Military Operations for War (OMP) and Military Operations Other Than War (OMSP) with reference to the Integrated Trimatra

[219] a) **Main Component Capability Development (TNI)**, is carried out through

[220] Preparation of strategic policies and strategic products, as well as products in the form of provisions or other legal products to strengthen the TNI in carrying out tasks, both OMP and OMSP

[221] Increasing the development of capabilities in deterrence, action, and recovery in the form of diplomatic capabilities, intelligence, security defense, territorial development/empowerment of defense areas and support capabilities in accordance with developments in science and technology

[222] Development of the TNI posture through the fulfillment of the TNI's main strengths in accordance with the strategic plan

[223] Optimizing the arrangement of the TNI's strength distribution in a balanced, proportional, and integrated manner in response to the geographical conditions of the Indonesian region, and threat predictions

[224] Development of national resources for military defense is adjusted to the needs required by each dimension, and its strength distribution is adjusted to the TNI's strength distribution through the concept of regional defense based on the principles of the total people's defense and security system

[225] b) **Reserve Component Capacity Development**, is carried out through: (1) Education and training carried out at the Military Education and Training Center in stages, levels and continuously to improve the quality, utility value, and effectiveness of the reserve component as a multiplier force for the main component for the interests of national defense

[226] Maintenance and care of natural resources, artificial resources, and national facilities and infrastructure to support the readiness of the reserve component in accordance with applicable laws and regulations

[227] c) **Supporting Component Capacity Development**, is carried out through: (1) Data collection on the quality and quantity of the Supporting Component including: citizens, natural resources; artificial resources; and national facilities and infrastructure to obtain professional classification in order to strengthen the reserve component and the main component

[228] Increasing cooperation and coordination with related ministries/institutions and local governments in implementing development for the supporting component in the form of socialization, technical guidance and simulations

[229] Non-Military Defense Capability Development

[230] Non-military defense capability development is aimed at ensuring the implementation of a non-military defense system by the main elements and other elements of the nation's strength which is directed at: utilizing all resources to improve non-military defense capabilities; organizing development in each ministry/institution and regional government in line with the aspects of national defense; increasing the capabilities of the main elements and other elements of the nation's strength in facing non-military threats; and realizing the implementation of handling non-military threats that are professional, fast, precise, systematic and measurable

[231] The implementation of non-military defense capability development is as follows: a

[232] Encouraging ministries/institutions in efforts to utilize all resources to improve non-military defense capabilities

[233] b) Encouraging ministries/institutions in accordance with their roles and responsibilities, to formulate implementation policies related to the defense sector as an elaboration of the general policy of national defense

[234] c) Encourage relevant ministries/institutions in accordance with their roles and responsibilities, to improve non-military defense capabilities in the form of: early warning capabilities, national defense capabilities, diplomatic capabilities, scientific and technological capabilities, economic capabilities, social capabilities, moral capabilities and capabilities to support the implementation of Non-Military Defense

[235] Encourage the arrangement of non-military defense force titles designed to face non-military threats and adjusted to the location/position of ministries/institutions in each province/district/city throughout Indonesia

[236] e) Improve coordination and cooperation with ministries/institutions and local governments in an effort to synergize the implementation of military and non-military defense

[237] Capacity Development in the Field of International Cooperation

[238] Capacity development in the field of international cooperation is carried out to improve diplomatic and negotiation capabilities both individually and institutionally, through: a) Education and training related to tasks in the field of defense cooperation

[239] b) Assignment, placement and career development in accordance with the tasks in the field of defense cooperation

[240] Fulfillment of budget support, facilities and infrastructure needed to implement defense cooperation

[241] d) Improving coordination with relevant ministries and institutions in implementing defense cooperation

[242] e) Improving the quality of educational and training institutions in order to prepare personnel for world peacekeeping missions

[243] Defense Industry Capability Development

[244] Defense industry capability development for the development of national industry is carried out by: a

[245] Effectively enhancing the role and function of the Defense Industry Policy Committee (KKIP) in coordinating the improvement of capabilities to meet the needs of Alpalhankam independently in producing Alpalhankam products as well as the production of *parts*/components/spare parts in order to support maintenance

[246] b) Strengthening *triple helix* cooperation (government, research institutions, and industry) in order to encourage defense technology innovation

[247] c) Encouraging the development of the defense industry to improve capabilities and capabilities through cooperation with foreign defense industries, both production cooperation, development cooperation, and in new Alpalhankam products independently and through cooperation with domestic and foreign research and development institutions/institutions

[248] Policy of Deployment and Use of Force

[249] The deployment and use of national defense forces are carried out to face threats to national defense and certain conditions for the national interest, consisting of: Deployment and use of national defense forces to face military, non-military, and hybrid threats; Deployment and use of national defense forces for world peacekeeping tasks; to face certain conditions or emergency conditions

[250] The implementation of the deployment and use of national defense forces is as follows: 1) **Deployment and use of national defense forces to face military threats

[251] Threat of Aggression

[252] Deployment and use of TNI forces strengthened through increasing the strength of Kogabwilhan and Koopsus TNI within the OMP framework supported by reserve components and supporting components as well as mobilization of national forces to become national defense forces

[253] Encourage the deployment and use of ministries/institutions as other national forces in accordance with their duties and functions to carry out diplomatic efforts in the form of unarmed resistance

[254] Encourage the deployment and use of all components of the nation to face protracted war by using guerrilla warfare tactics and empowering defense areas and politically using diplomacy

[255] b) **Not Aggression

[256] The deployment and use of TNI forces strengthened by increasing the strength of Kogabwilhan and Koopsus TNI proportionally, both integrated and independent, within the framework of OMSP

[257] Encourage the deployment and use of relevant ministries/institutions and regional governments in accordance with their duties and functions related to military issues or threats to provide assistance to the TNI in order to achieve maximum results

[258] Deployment and use of national defense forces to face non-military threats

[259] a) Encourage relevant ministries/institutions and regional governments as the main elements in deploying their forces to face non-military threats with ideological, political, economic, socio-cultural, public safety, technological, and legislative dimensions

[260] b) Encourage the deployment and use of other relevant ministries/institutions as other elements of the nation's strength to provide assistance to the main elements in accordance with their duties and functions

[261] c) Deploy TNI forces in the form of assistance tasks based on applicable laws and regulations according to capacity and capability without disrupting the TNI's main duties

[262] Deployment and use of national defense forces in facing hybrid threats with a military defense pattern coordinated by the Ministry of Defense

[263] a) Deployment and use of TNI forces strengthened by increasing the strength of Kogabwilhan and Koopsus TNI proportionally according to the level of authority based on the escalation of threats

[264] b) Encourage the deployment and use of relevant ministries/institutions and regional governments to jointly face hybrid threats by paying attention to capabilities in a professional and proportional manner

[265] Deployment and use of national defense forces in carrying out world peacekeeping missions

[266] a) Deployment and use of TNI forces and encourage relevant ministries/institutions according to their fields of duty and function to participate in world peacekeeping missions at the request of the UN through UN Security Council Resolutions, international organizations and/or regional organizations based on Indonesia's foreign policy

[267] b) Increased deployment of professional troops supported by equipment and infrastructure to achieve the target of 1 Brigade per year, and place Indonesia among the ten largest countries sending troops to UN peacekeeping missions

[268] c) Increasing the capabilities and skills of troops to carry out world peacekeeping tasks is carried out by the TNI through the TNI Peacekeeping Mission Center (PMPP

[269] Deployment and use of national defense forces in facing certain conditions and emergency conditions for national interests**, organized by the TNI and non-military defense components according to their spectrum which are directed at: a) Deployment and use of TNI forces strengthened through increasing the strength of Kogabwilhan and Koopsus TNI professionally and proportionally both as the main component and as other elements of national strength according to their capacity and capabilities

[270] b) Encourage the deployment and use of forces of related ministries/institutions and regional governments according to their respective duties and functions both as the main element and as other elements of national strength

[271] Deployment and use of state defense forces to be projected outside the jurisdiction of the Unitary State of the Republic of Indonesia (NKRI) in order to maintain the safety of Indonesian citizens abroad and assist friendly countries that support Indonesia's national interests

[272] Deployment and use of forces are carried out through diplomatic channels

[275] Drafting Government Regulations and Draft Presidential Regulations which are the implementing orders of Law Number 23 of 2019 concerning Management of National Resources for National Defense

[276] Drafting other Regulations, and Ministerial Regulations, Ministerial Decrees, contained in the defense legislation program

[277] Providing input to ministries/institutions regarding the preparation/formulation of laws and regulations by taking into account aspects of national defense

[279] Its implementation is through: 1) Utilization of the budget in the formulation of strategic planning in the management of national resources for defense interests

[280] Development of military defense posture in order to achieve the Core Strength of the main components, as well as the gradual preparation of reserve components and supporting components

[281] Development of facilities and infrastructure, organizational development and additional personnel referring to the *right sizing* and *proportional growth* policies
