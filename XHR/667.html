[13] — The Prime Minister, José Manuel Durão Barroso. --- ### Strategic concept of national defense **1 — Introduction** 1.1 — The last decade of the 20th century introduced profound changes in the international scenario motivated by the implosion of the Soviet Union

[14] As a consequence, new countries emerged in this area, the legitimacy of Central and Eastern European countries as international actors was reinforced, and the East-West antagonism and its inherent logic of confrontation between blocs ended

[15] The development that international life has experienced in terms of the circulation and access to information has given globalization an equally fundamental role in all these changes, with significant consequences for the decision-making process of national and international political actors

[16] Naturally, these changes in the international environment have opened new opportunities for international cooperation and allowed for a more relaxed relationship between countries, institutions, and international organizations

[17] Conversely, they have also forced international actors to adapt their positions, and even their structures, to the new reality, which, obviously, has introduced factors of instability and unpredictability within them

[18] We are, therefore, in a period of transition, extending from the State to citizenship, with significant changes in the priorities it establishes and the register of values that guides it

[19] International actors have sought to adapt to this new scenario, finding ways to respond to an environment of unpredictable, multifaceted, and transnational threats and risks

[20] Although this new strategic environment has mitigated traditional military threats, it has given rise to factors of instability translated into new risks and potential threats, of which the tragic events of September 11, 2001, are the paradigm

[21] This is so much the case that these events profoundly altered the international political-strategic agenda, created new challenges in the field of security and defense, and introduced a new qualitative aspect of &quot;threat&quot; on the international stage, further blurring the line between this and the characterization of &quot;multifaceted and multidimensional risks.&quot; With that action, transnational terrorism thus seems to disregard ethical limits, or any other kind of limits, assuming the possibility of acting on a global scale, combining traditional violence, resulting from attacks and bombings, with the possible use of cyberspace and weapons of mass destruction

[22] The maximization of the principles of surprise and deception, in an asymmetrical combat by non-traditional actors, which includes transnational terrorism, along with the demonstration of capacity and motivation by poorly defined and not fully identified organizations to carry out high-impact actions, creates the possibility of high levels of human and material destruction

[23] The consequences of such actions on economies, security, and international stability transcend the individual response capacity of States and interrelate the concepts of internal and external security and the objectives they prefigure

[24] Transnational terrorism thus presents itself as an external threat and, when realized, as an external aggression, so its prevention and combating clearly fall within the mission of the Armed Forces

[25] It becomes necessary not only to maintain but also to create capabilities that allow for a response to the phenomenon of terrorism, as well as to the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and the possibility of nuclear, radiological, chemical, and biological accidents resulting from the intentional, improper, or unskilled use of said means and materials

[26] Consequently, pursuing the goal of guaranteeing the security and well-being of citizens, the State is obliged to rethink and adapt the concepts and instruments of security and defense to the new political-strategic environment, from a perspective of minimizing risks and guaranteeing the possibility of responding not only to these new types of challenges to peace and international stability but also to any others that may arise

[27] — The established boundary between security and defense, the specific actions applicable to each of these areas, and the entities primarily responsible for their handling result from the provisions of the National Defense and Armed Forces Law, Law No

[28] Its origins lay in the need to normalize relations between various public entities and reposition powers, entirely understandable in the context of the time

[29] Today, that issue has been overcome, and the evolution of this boundary must also be understood so as not to inhibit the coordination of efforts that different organizations should develop, seeking synergies, optimizing resources, and improving efficiency in preventing and combating current risks and threats, always in light of the principles and norms of Portuguese constitutional and legal order

[30] Thus, and because it is understood that &quot;the definition of the fundamental aspects of the State&#39;s overall strategy adopted for the achievement of the objectives of national security and defense policy&quot; needs to be adapted to the new political-strategic reality, it seems opportune to develop a new strategic concept of national defense, which is the result of a critical, participatory and multidisciplinary debate on security and defense issues

[31] — International Context** 2.1 — In a Euro-Atlantic sphere of influence, it is possible to define lines of instability involving North Africa, Sub-Saharan Africa, the Middle East, the Balkans, the Caucasus, Central Asia and South Asia, in which risks of separatism and ethnic, religious and border conflicts, fundamentalism or mass migrations are concentrated

[32] The vulnerabilities of the State in these regions increase the threats linked to terrorism, drug trafficking, human trafficking and organized crime

[33] Furthermore, the phenomena of state destabilization and globalization have contributed to increasing the risks of proliferation of weapons of mass destruction and misuse of new technologies, as well as diminishing guarantees for the maintenance and control of nuclear, radiological, chemical, and biological equipment and materials

[34] The possibility of international conflict arising from environmental and ecological deregulation and the management of natural resources, particularly water and energy resources, becoming a focus of international conflict is also considered

[35] In addition to this set of more immediate risks, it is now possible to identify another set of factors that will undoubtedly influence and condition the international environment, namely

[36] The persistence of inequalities in development

[37] The strengthening of financial internationalism

[38] The persistence of political, ideological, and religious radicalism; * The increase in interdependence due to the information and interactivity revolution; * The growth of migratory flows, the multicultural character of societies, and areas of non-integration of new communities; * Factors related to technological evolution, new &quot;viruses,&quot; and the criminal use of cyberspace

[39] The interventionist attitude of the international community and the exercise of the right of intervention

[40] The existence of a single superpower and the tension between &quot;unilateralism&quot; and &quot;multilateralism&quot

[41] The use of space for scientific, economic, or military purposes and the prospects for geostrategic use that are emerging

[42] — As a result of this situation, there is an increasing tendency today to define a broader concept of security that, while continuing to integrate the more traditional objectives of States — defense of territory and sovereignty — gives increased attention to a preventive philosophy and a global vision of the evolution of international insecurity hotspots and the crises that arise from them, with the aim of preventing and limiting them, avoiding their development into aggravated forms of conflict

[43] Within this context, as a way to optimize the response of international actors, an idea of cooperative security has been gaining ground, with repercussions in the development of international organizations

[44] The United Nations (UN) has been assuming a more interventionist role in the area of international security, framing a growing set of actions in the field of humanitarian and peacekeeping operations in various parts of the globe

[45] This willingness can be witnessed, more recently, by the efforts to internally restructure the support for peacekeeping operations conducted under its aegis and by its commitment to fostering greater engagement from countries that provide forces

[46] The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) continues to focus its attention on aspects related to the defense of human rights, the peaceful resolution and prevention of conflicts, the political management of crises, and participation in peacekeeping activities, having strengthened its political capacity, particularly in the field of preventive diplomacy

[47] In this last aspect, it is worth highlighting the counter-terrorism measures resulting from the Bishkek International Conference and the selection of this theme as a priority for action during the Portuguese presidency of the Organization

[48] — The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) has been undergoing a process of adaptation to the new international environment

[49] The Washington Summit in April 1999 adopted a new strategic concept, in which the alliance assumes the traditional tasks of collective defense of its members and the inherent operations of Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty, but also positions itself to fulfill functions of prevention and management of crises &quot;outside the area&quot

[50] In parallel, the Partnership for Peace Programme was developed as a way to strengthen both stability in the Euro-Atlantic space and the transition processes of the countries of Central and Eastern Europe and the new countries born from the former USSR

[51] The process of expanding the alliance, initiated in 1999, is a clear institutional reflection of the new cooperative philosophy that shapes international relations, aiming at Euro-Atlantic security and stability

[52] This process continues with the alliance&#39;s ongoing declaration of an &quot;open door&quot; policy and encouragement for candidates to pursue programs to meet the criteria for eventual accession

[53] NATO&#39;s &quot;bilateral&quot; relationship with the Russian Federation has also undergone significant developments, now framed by a founding act that seeks to link it to stabilization efforts in Europe

[54] The creation of the NATO/Russia Council has given a new qualitative dimension to the ongoing dialogue between the alliance and Russia

[55] The alliance also pays particular attention to its southern flank, the Mediterranean basin, having created a Mediterranean Dialogue Initiative involving some of the countries located there, as a way to guarantee security and stability in this area

[56] Moreover, this concern is shared by the European Union, to which an important contribution was made during the Portuguese presidency, resulting in a new impetus for relations between the two sides of the Mediterranean

[57] Improving its operational capabilities has been a concern for NATO, which aims to equip itself with the means to effectively fulfill the new missions entrusted to it

[58] This effort includes the concepts of force interoperability, combined joint task forces (CJTFs), and &quot;separable but not separate forces,&quot; as well as policies aimed at improving capabilities in strategic mobility, self-protection, force sustainment, command and control systems, and intelligence—policies which, in light of the events of September 11, were deemed necessary to accelerate

[59] At the Washington Summit, support was also expressed for the development of the European Security and Defence Initiative (ESDI), reflecting the need for the European Union to have an autonomous capacity to intervene militarily, seeking to create synergies between the two organizations

[60] — In parallel, at the European Union (EU) level, significant steps have been taken in the security and defence component, largely resulting from significant changes in the international scene

[61] Indeed, between the Maastricht Treaty in 1991 and the Capability Commitment Conference in 2000, passing through the Amsterdam Treaty and the Summits of Cologne, Helsinki, Santa Maria da Feira and Nice, the EU integrated the Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) into its Second Pillar, assumed an active role in international security and humanitarian support within the framework of the Petersberg missions, agreed to the creation of its own operational capability, to be completed by 2003, and promoted a European security and defence policy

[62] Similarly, and in accordance with the Laeken Declaration, the implementation of the Nice agreements with partners will strengthen the means available to the EU to conduct crisis management operations, and the development of the means and capabilities at its disposal will allow it to progressively undertake increasingly complex operations

[63] Throughout this process, which marks the end of the Western European Union (WEU), the EU seeks to coordinate with NATO, an organization that remains a structuring reference for collective security and defense, mechanisms that avoid unnecessary duplication, allow for gains in efficiency, and strengthen transatlantic ties

[64] Also within the EU, an enlargement process is underway, aimed at ensuring political and economic stability in its surrounding area

[65] The idea of cooperative security has also been reflected in the adoption, by international organizations, of legally binding instruments in international law aimed at security, confidence, and humanitarian measures, examples of which are the CFE (Conventional Armed Forces in Europe), Open Skies, START II, MTCR (Missile Technology Control Regime), the Chemical Weapons Convention, and the Anti-Personnel Mines Convention, among others

[66] Portugal is committed to following these multilateral initiatives

[67] — Within the Community of Portuguese-Speaking Countries (CPLP), a globally positive development should be highlighted

[68] The independence of East Timor signified the triumph of national will and resistance over the apparent &quot;inevitabilities&quot; that are so often criticized in international politics

[69] For Portugal, the independence of East Timor meant the validation of an intense diplomatic struggle and the realization of a very important national consensus

[70] The consolidation of the democratic process in Mozambique is another factor to emphasize

[71] It is this consolidation that, along with an economic opening to the outside world, will allow efforts and resources to be directed not only towards achieving peace but also towards achieving new and urgent levels of development

[72] Finally, there is a real opportunity for peace in Angola

[73] The international community, in general, and Portugal, in particular, have been following and supporting the challenge that the reconstruction of Angola represents, following the ceasefire, helping to develop solid foundations for democratic participation and political integration, without forgetting the need to contribute to overcoming the difficult humanitarian situation

[74] — The national context** 3.1 — The aforementioned external context largely explains the need to review the strategic concept of national defense

[75] In fact, the new global strategic environment directly and indirectly affects Portugal

[76] The international alliances and organizations to which Portugal belongs have evolved considerably; internally, there have also been changes impacting national defense policy; It should not be overlooked that a number of states, notably allies, have undertaken strategic revisions in the recent or near past

[77] Since 1994, the year in which the previous concept was approved, significant changes have occurred internationally, which must have consequences for public security and defense policies in Portugal

[78] Essentially, the following stand out

[79] The evidence that international terrorism constitutes a serious threat to the system of states, their authority, humanist values, and free societies; * The difficulty in predicting the materialization of so-called &quot;new threats,&quot; despite certainties about their lethal severity and awareness of our vulnerability to them

[80] The maintenance or emergence of new types of conflict, of an ethnic and religious nature

[81] The increasing proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, in their nuclear, radiological, biological, and chemical dimensions, as well as conventional medium- and long-range means, and their accessibility to non-state organizations

[82] The consolidation of the new European map emerging from the end of the Cold War, expressed in the accession of Eastern European countries to the Atlantic Alliance and in the Balkan crises

[83] The dynamics and impact of globalization and the persistence of critical factors in development relations between the North and the South

[84] The increasing deregulation of migratory flows and the intensification of the phenomenon of illegal immigration; * The aggravation of regional conflicts, particularly in the Middle East, and instability in Asia and Latin America

[85] The current trend towards a more frequent recourse to the instrument of threat or use of force

[86] On the other hand, the alliances and international organizations to which we belong constitute an evolving reality

[87] In this period, facts such as the alteration of NATO&#39;s strategic concept in 1999, its enlargement, and the strong impetus to European security and defense policy assumed particular importance

[88] The reform of the EU Treaties, the requirements of the Stability and Growth Pact, and the transfer of competences from the WEU to the European Union represent important national challenges

[94] — The changing variables should not make us forget constant values

[97] — With the approval of the new strategic concept of national defense, the course for reforms in the field of defense is defined, including an updated military strategic concept, the redefinition of missions, and the re-equipping of the Armed Forces

[99] — The permanent values of national defense** National defense aims to guarantee national independence, the integrity of the territory, and the freedom and security of the population against any external aggression or threat

[100] It is also responsible for guaranteeing the freedom of action of the organs of sovereignty and the strengthening of national values

[101] — The national defense strategy serves the preservation of the sovereign State

[102] The State is obliged to value the factors of national identity, protecting the Portuguese language and national symbols

[103] — National defense presupposes the defense of national cohesion

[104] This is expressed in the common cultural heritage and in the solidarity among all Portuguese people

[105] The State must prepare itself to defend the lives and interests of Portuguese people at any time, wherever they may be

[106] — Portugal will contribute to dialogue between nations, the defense of human rights, and the peaceful resolution of conflicts

[107] Participation in multinational interventions is a consolidated option that enhances the country&#39;s prestige

[108] — The articulation of defense policy with education policy will be a priority, considered an important element in the exercise of citizenship and patriotism

[109] — The strategic space of national interest** 5.1 — The national territory encompasses the mainland, the Azores, and Madeira

[110] It includes the space of circulation between these areas, the airspace and maritime space under national responsibility, the exclusive economic zone, and the continental shelf

[111] — The strategic space of current national interest includes priority areas such as the Euro-Atlantic area, relations with neighboring states, the Maghreb, the South Atlantic (Brazil), Portuguese-speaking Africa, East Timor, and countries with strong communities of Portuguese emigrants or of immigrant origin

[112] — Any other areas of the globe where national interests are at stake may be considered areas of relevant interest

[113] — Relevant threats** 6.1 — Portugal must be prepared to deter and confront any armed aggression against its territory or populations

[116] — Terrorism constitutes a serious threat

[117] The State must actively collaborate in international prevention, eradicate terrorist networks through political and economic measures, and enhance the crisis management system
