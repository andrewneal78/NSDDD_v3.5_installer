[213] BASES OF THE STATE

[214] A.- PRINCIPLES OF ITS FOUNDATION

[215] The evolution of the Bolivian State and its institutions is a dynamic process that began with the founding of the Republic of Bolivia in August

[216] The State was recognized as sovereign and independent in the international community, defining a division of specific governmental functions for the Executive, Legislative, and Judicial branches

[217] The evolutionary trends of the Bolivian state were initially those of the Liberal-Patrimonial State (1900-1952); this period was characterized by the consolidation of its basic characteristics: territory and population

[218] In the first thirty years of the century, the Bolivian State achieved political stability, institutional modernization, and economic growth

[219] The Bureaucratic Revolutionary period (1952-1982) was the stage where important political, social and economic measures were implemented

[220] Within the electoral process, universal suffrage was implemented for the first time, resulting in an increase in the number of voters

[221] On the social front, lands were expropriated and distributed among the peasants

[222] A new Education Code was enacted

[223] Also during that period, the mines belonging to businessmen Hoschild, Patiño, and Aramayo were nationalized

[224] The democratic State (1982 to date) is the period in which the stage of political transition to a democratic State is distinguished, which meant a fundamental change in the economic role of the State, which begins to take shape with a division of functions, powers and areas of action, proceeding to the division of four systems: a normative decision-making system, a regulatory one, a legal one and a decentralized and territorialized system

[225] Basing its institutionality on the Political Constitution of the State

[226] On February 20, 2004, a new process in democratic life was established, including precepts of social inclusion, such as the recognition of multiethnicity and multiculturalism, the effect of which is expressed in greater participation and social inclusion

[227] The design of state policies is based on national needs, aspirations, and interests, which are consolidated in the Permanent National Objectives. These objectives fundamentally aim to guarantee participatory democratic development with equity and justice, as well as the rule of law

[228] Considering that the State's purpose is the common good and achieving development, security, and overcoming poverty through national unity and cohesion

[229] Multiethnicity and multiculturalism

[230] The first article of the State's Political Constitution states that Bolivia is a unitary, multiethnic and pluricultural Republic, with 33 ethnic groups and 5 languages

[231] According to the latest census, it has a population of 8,142,000, of which 3,763,000 are under 18 years of age

[232] The national territory has a disaggregated geography and its sovereignty is distributed throughout the territory, comprising the central zone or central core of the country, made up of the main cities of Santa Cruz, Cochabamba and La Paz where

[233] There has been a concentration of efforts, which can become an integrating unit or a source of conflict

[234] The dichotomy of population and concentration of economic activities poses a challenge to the concept of defense, given that the country has extensive borders within its territory, classified as unpopulated and therefore vulnerable to being sources of conflict

[235] Representative and participatory democracy

[236] Democracy in Bolivia is part of the context of political modernization. The construction of representative democracy had three key dimensions, which led to the formation of the National State

[237] The first founding moment of political modernization took place within the framework of census-based and oligarchic democracy, which arose from ideas linked to political ideological positions after the War of the Pacific

[238] The second moment begins with the Chaco War (1923-1935), where, as a result of the lacerating experience and the collective consciousness, the ideological direction and search for new political models are reoriented with the emergence of socialist, Marxist and nationalist tendencies

[239] The third key moment of democracy has meant a true refoundation of the political system, following the collapse of oligarchic and populist democracy, giving way to a system of government unique in the Americas that is closer to a parliamentary-type government, both due to the origin of the formation and parliamentary legitimacy of democratic governments, and due to its support in post-electoral interparty coalitions

[240] Bolivia has made significant progress in building a democratic institutional order

[241] Democracy is based on the foundations of an expanded citizenry, premised on ethnic and cultural diversity, a coalition-based system of government, a moderate party system, and a policy of building the rule of law

[242] Social and democratic state of law

[243] Bolivia formulates its state policies, aimed at economic and social development, considering that these must be equitable and fair, ensuring that their purpose is to serve the population

[244] Thus, strengthening the democratic system, which among its principles includes the rule of law for all citizens, translated into the right to private property, liberty, equality, and justice

[246] The purposes of the State are generic manifestations that relate to society as a whole; therefore, it is the State's duty to ensure that these are achieved

[247] Purposes are characterized by being timeless and usually non-measurable abstract manifestations, which, due to their importance, are part of the spirit of holistic coherence that must be part of the constitutive elements of the State

[248] National policy is determined through them

[249] In Bolivia there are two types of ends: the supreme end and the essential ends
