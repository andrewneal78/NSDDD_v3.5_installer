[379] Functions in Support of the Czech National and NATO Collective Defence The Czech Republic shares its security and defence interests with other NATO member states

[380] The Ministry of Defence is responsible for national defence and coordination of its preparation

[381] The Czech Armed Forces plan, prepare and implement defence of their territory within the framework of NATO collective defence and in accordance with Article 5 of the Washington Treaty

[382] Defence operations can be either of a high or low intensity, and may be conducted inside or outside the Czech territory at various distances from its border

[383] High intensity operations are conducted with the use of maximum military forces and equipment deployed by NATO allies, whereas low intensity operations can be carried out with a smaller amount of NATO capacities

[384] Decisions on the character and intensity of retaliation against an armed attack on one or more allies are made by NATO member states in compliance with the Alliance’s principles and procedures

[385] Chapter 3 Defence can be conducted in two basic modes depending on the situation

[386] In an armed conflict where the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Czech Republic are endangered, while defending either its own territory or the territory of nearby allies, the state of war is declared

[387] In terms of its organizational structure and command & control arrangements, the Ministry of Defence is transformed to a wartime organization

[388] The Czech Republic transfers its forces and equipment earmarked in NATO defence planning under the authority of the designated bodies of the Alliance

[389] The General Staff of the Czech Armed Forces retains the command authority over those forces which have not been moved under the allied command

[390] The Czech Armed Forces support their own units deployed in operations while engaging in preparatory activities for defending the Czech territory and receiving military support from NATO allies

[391] Military bodies assume the responsibility for running air traffic control within the Czech Republic airspace. The Czech Republic does not necessarily have to declare a state of war for the defence of a remote territory of an allied state

[392] In such an event, forces and equipment earmarked in NATO defence planning are transferred under the authority of the designated NATO bodies

[393] The Czech Armed Forces support their own units deployed in operations, continue in conducting activities in their peacetime establishment and implement the planned preliminary measures to prepare for elevated alert levels

[394] Situations of low intensity threats against the Czech territory can be dealt with by the armed forces either by themselves or with NATO support

[395] The Czech Armed Forces can be deployed in all climatic zones, except for polar

[396] Their deployment is not expected in naval landing operations

[397] Functions in International Cooperation The Czech Armed Forces cooperate with foreign military forces by the authority of international agreements

[398] Furthermore, they participate in various activities to support peace and security, especially in international operations and missions

[399] Based on analyses of the strategic environment, the participation in international crisis response operations will present the most probable form of the Czech Armed Forces' combat employment in the near future, both inside and outside NATO/EU states

[400] Whether gradual or simultaneous, the missions will include the following operations: peacemaking, peace support and peacekeeping, post-conflict reconstruction, stabilisation of state authorities and assistance in introducing the rule of law

[401] Crisis response operations are usually complex, thus calling for political, civilian and military measures and instruments

[402] An operation is often characterised by interconnected missions with varying degree of intensity of combat phases

[403] Enemies usually apply asymmetric methods of fighting, and the boundaries between combat zones and civilian areas often disappear

[404] Peacemaking operations are conducted without the consent of conflict parties

[405] Their aim is to make the hostile parties terminate combat actions, cease violence against civilians, and deactivate their armed forces

[406] The Czech Armed Forces transfer their capacities, together with those capabilities that are functionally appropriate to the character, environment and intensity of the conflict, under the authority of the designated bodies of the Alliance in such a scope as declared in the Political-Military Ambitions of the Czech Republic, while supporting their operational activities

[407] Similar procedures are applied in conducting operations led by the EU or an ad hoc coalition

[408] Peace support or peacekeeping operations are usually conducted with the consent of the parties in question

[409] Their aim is to prevent a conflict from arising or escalating, monitor activities of opposing armed forces, prevent them from initiating or resuming combat actions, committing violence against civilians and to achieve a gradual stabilisation and restoration of peace in the area

[410] As declared in the Political-Military Ambitions of the Czech Republic, the Czech Armed Forces contribute their forces and equipment under the authority of the designated bodies of the Alliance and support their operational activities

[411] International operations can include assistance in evacuation of civilians, alleviating or coping with the aftermath of disasters, humanitarian operations or search and rescue operations

[412] As a part of missions in international operations, the Czech Armed Forces can also be engaged in reshaping or building a local defence sector through mentoring and training of the local armed forces

[413] In today’s evolving security environment of complex operations, the objectives cannot be achieved merely by military instruments

[1251] The MoD Organizational Structure and Management

[1252] In every organization, efficient and rational structures of management must result from the objectives of its activities

[1253] Thus the organizational format must respect and follow processes and functions that lead to achieving these objectives

[1254] The current MoD organizational architecture does not correspond to these principles

[1255] On the contrary, it often creates unproductive bodies with their own agendas and duplicated processes

[1256] At all levels in its system, the Ministry of Defence and its entities display problems stemming from the existence of organizational structures built in silos and managed in a linear way

[1257] This situation has resulted in a complicated and excessive apparatus with high personnel costs, which considerably burdens the Ministry of Defence with operative activities and associated administrative work

[1258] Instead of running conceptual activities, the Ministry is involved in meaningless bureaucratic procedures and processes and non-transparent internal regulations and is insufficiently integrated

[1259] Moreover, the information system can hardly be used for providing direct support to management

[1260] Implementing horizontal communication and coordination among individual lines of organization is almost impossible at the lower levels

[1261] As a consequence of these undesired features, individual bodies within the MoD sector are facing difficulties in mutual cooperation and coordination, and ambiguity in the system of distributing authorities, duties and responsibilities
