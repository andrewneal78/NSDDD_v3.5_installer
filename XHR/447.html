[118] Focus on operations Combat capability The Austrian Armed Forces have to be oriented towards the requirements of military defence

[120] The Austrian Armed Forces conduct combined-arms combat or – if combat is not the primary expectation – employ combined-arms forces

[121] Both types are characterised by the co-operation, in time and space, of forces and assets from various branches

[122] Organisationally, combined-arms combat continues to be a capability of brigade-sized units

[240] Types of forces of the Austrian Armed Forces The Austrian Armed Forces have to be organised in accordance with the principles of their ready reserve system so that they can accomplish their operational tasks under likely operational conditions

[241] As future threats (especially in cyber-space and the information environment, and in the form of attacks on critical infrastructure and terrorist attacks) will have to be defended against at very short notice or none at all, the organisation of the Austrian Armed Forces’ permanent elements must make it possible for opera-tional tasks to be accomplished in the course of routine duty in any domain, there being increased readiness levels for elements designated as response forces

[242] Combat forces comprise the bulk of those elements of the Austrian Armed Forces that accomplish operational tasks domestically or abroad

[243] They consist of permanently ready elements, which may also include ready reserve elements, and independently organised ready reserve forces

[244] Transitioning to the Austrian Armed Forces’ operational organisation of currently up to 55,000 troops must be possible in a timely and gradual fashion

[245] The personnel-related, material, training-related and legal conditions for mobilisation have to be assured so that ready reserve forces can be made operational in a timely manner

[246] Included are preparations for the enforcement of civic services provisions and the Military Powers Act

[249] Response forces Response forces are combat forces designated for first response, domestically or abroad

[251] Their organi-sation has to enable them to accomplish operational tasks during routine duty without major reinforcements in terms of personnel or equipment

[252] If need be, their sustainability can be enhanced by means of reinforcements from ready forces or adequate available ready-reserve or reserve personnel

[253] In the event of response forces being deployed as first responders rendering disaster relief, other forces have to be made available and replace them as soon as possible
