[469] Command and control in operations

[470] Command and control is the process of purposeful action of command bodies (commanders, chiefs and staffs) in assigning tasks and directing subordinate commanders of organic and assigned forces and means to use available resources as effectively as possible during planning, organizing, coordinating and conducting activities in order to meet set goals within set deadlines with minimal effort and minimal losses

[471] Command and control must be firm, flexible, continuous and secret

[472] The strength of command consists in the consistent implementation of a well-considered and reasoned decision

[473] It is achieved by timely adoption of decisions, precise formulation of tasks to subordinates, timely delivery of tasks to executors and consistent requirement for their fulfillment

[474] The flexibility of command consists in the continuous acquisition and processing of information, in anticipating the development of the situation and in flexibly reacting to its changes

[475] It is achieved by timely decision-making and issuing tasks to subordinates and, above all, by operational (continuous) specification of tasks based on an analysis of the likely development of the situation

[476] Continuity of command consists in the constant control of troops, in maintaining continuous cooperation and mutual information

[477] It is achieved by appropriate deployment of command posts, their timely transfer to new areas, maintaining reliable communication and establishing measures to protect the command and communication system against enemy action, as well as by rapid restoration in the event of their disruption

[478] Secrecy of command consists in the secrecy of all measures related to the preparation and management of the operation

[479] This is achieved in particular by limiting the circle of persons who become familiar with the plan of operation (battle) or the commander&#39;s decision, by covertly deploying and relocating command posts, by strictly adhering to the principles of protecting classified information, by using means of protecting information during its transmission, by observing the rules of communication security, by camouflage, disinformation and deception of the adversary

[480] Command is a process by which the commander makes decisions and transfers his will to his subordinates

[481] The commander has the authority, responsibility and duty to act or freely decide not to act in order to achieve the goals set by his superior

[482] Command is also an art, consisting, among other things, of the skill of creatively obtaining and using available information and, last but not least, of the skill of effectively motivating and effectively managing subordinates in order to achieve the set goals

[483] The commander must: know the moral and professional qualities of his direct subordinates, personally command them and create space for them to exercise their initiative and activity; know the situation, the combat capabilities of his unit (formation, unit), the expected possibilities and the probable intention of the enemy; make decisions in time and consistently enforce his decisions, issue tasks to subordinates, coordinate their activities and provide them with comprehensive support; personally direct the fight and, depending on the development of the situation, specify his decisions; be in the most important direction (area) in decisive situations and influence the course of the fight in a timely manner with the forces and means at his disposal; inform his superior about the situation of the forces and means subordinate to him, about his fundamental decisions and submit to him requests for support and security

[484] The commander must know the norms of international humanitarian law and be able to apply them in specific conditions of activity

[485] He always informs his chief of staff about his decisions, issued orders and regulations

[486] When commanding, the commander is supported by his staff

[487] The task of the staff is to prepare proposals for the commander, ensure the implementation of his decisions and provide support to subordinate commanders

[488] The staff has no authority of its own, it derives it from the commander and exercises it on his behalf

[499] Principles of command and control

[504] The principle of unity of command expresses the fact that a necessary condition for achieving success is unity of thinking, approaches and implementation of plans by commanders and staffs at all levels of command

[505] This unity is achieved by clearly defined goals and objectives of the operation, precisely defined relationships and powers of individual levels of command and personal responsibility of commanders for the fulfillment of realistically set tasks

[506] A condition for achieving unity of command is also the interconnection of information systems and communication systems

[507] The basic unifying element in the command and control system is the commander

[508] Decentralization (delegation) of authority cannot limit the indivisibility of command authority and responsibility

[509] The transfer of responsibility does not relieve the commander of responsibility, who remains responsible for his actions and the actions of his subordinates

[511] In some situations, the use of this method is inappropriate

[518] Efficiency represents primarily the ability to respond to sudden changes in the situation and find their optimal solution, and creativity is the ability to adopt non-standard and unconventional solutions, allowing to react even in a complex situation and effectively influence the development of the situation to one&#39;s own advantage. •

[519] The principle of choosing a decision from possible variants consists in finding and formulating possible variants of fulfilling a given task, in assessing them according to unambiguous and quantifiable criteria and in the subsequent decision to accept the variant that appears to be optimal under the given circumstances

[520] The principle of centralization of command results from the existence of a supreme and responsible authority of a commander, equipped with the powers necessary for planning and managing an operation

[521] Its application allows for the enforcement of unity of will, unity of action and speed of action, especially in complex situations, while at the same time allowing the commander to delegate the necessary powers to his subordinates

[522] Leading people (leadership, leadership) represents the ability of a commander to motivate, persuade and gain the people around him to be as actively involved as possible in the fulfillment of common, predetermined or expected tasks in the near future

[526] The choice of command method places demands on the leadership style

[527] It is influenced by the appreciation of the decision-making role of a person on the battlefield

[552] Maneuver and firepower

[553] Success in a military operation is achieved by an appropriate combination of maneuver and firepower

[554] Although the role of the use of firepower is lower in peacekeeping operations, readiness to use force (or the threat of force) significantly contributes to the effective performance of the mandate of peacekeeping forces

[555] Maneuver and firepower are closely related

[556] A maneuver can also be conducted by fire, firepower enables the maneuver to be carried out

[557] A maneuver serves to deploy or relocate troops (units) on the battlefield and assume such an operational (combat) formation that will allow creating optimal conditions for obtaining an advantageous position to fulfill the task using all hydro and geopolitical aspects

[558] In the case of peacekeeping operations, the term movement or movement is usually used instead of the term maneuver

[559] A maneuver is the most important means of implementing the principles of concentration of main efforts and economy of force use

[560] During combat operations, a maneuver makes it possible to use (achieve) another basic principle - surprise; during peacekeeping operations, it is a means of implementing the principle of freedom of movement

[561] The maneuver requires reliable information and its success depends, among other things, on the correct identification and respect for the influence of individual factors, which include primarily weather conditions, the nature of the terrain and communication network (roads, bridges, the permeability of open terrain), the existence of various obstacles to movement (e.g. flows of refugees or the population), etc

[562] The maneuver is also significantly influenced by the mobility of one&#39;s own units and assets, but is not limited to ground combat activities

[563] Aeromobility (air transportability) is an important prerequisite for the success of a maneuver in a modern operation

[564] Firepower represents the ability of an operational group to conduct direct and indirect fire with organic and supporting fire resources on targets in the operational (combat) formation of the enemy and thus reduce (neutralize) his combat potential

[565] The intention of using firepower in operations is not to systematically destroy the enemy by all means, but mainly to disrupt his morale, cohesion and ability to resist

[566] The use of firepower must be coordinated at all levels of command

[567] The potential of firepower can be best utilized if its use in an operation is planned and controlled from one place and its subsequent use is carried out in a decentralized manner

[585] Deception can be carried out at the strategic, operational and tactical levels

[635] They carry out the evacuation of medical losses from Role 2 facilities to themselves and participate in evacuations to Role 4 facilities

[637] The average duration of temporary hospitalization is usually 7 days

[638] After that, they return to the unit or are evacuated to Role 4 facilities, i.e. stationary medical facilities on the territory of the state

[666] The operational system represents a set of forces, means and their activities, combined into a whole and organized to achieve the objectives of the operation

[667] At the operational and tactical levels, operations are conducted with the aim of detecting the enemy, engaging his forces in order to deprive him of the initiative, thwarting his intention and defeating him

[668] The operational system must ensure the conduct of operations in contact with the enemy, in the depth of the enemy formation, in the rear of one&#39;s own troops and in the system of local, mutually disconnected combat formations

[669] Operations in contact with the enemy

[670] The purpose of operations in contact is primarily a clash with the enemy using various means with the aim of destroying or capturing him and thereby eliminating a vital part of his combat force

[671] Operations in contact will usually be conducted to a small depth and in a relatively short time span

[672] Their goal is to win battles of forces in direct contact with the enemy, when direct and successful actions against the enemy&#39;s combat force will have an immediate and obvious effect

[673] Operations in the depth of the enemy&#39;s operational formation

[674] The purpose of operations in depth is to detect and engage the enemy, prevent him from achieving his objectives and limit his freedom of action, thereby creating favorable conditions for close combat operations

[675] They are usually conducted over a long distance and over a longer period of time

[676] Operations in depth are essentially offensive in nature and can themselves lead to close combat

[677] They are a means of limiting the enemy&#39;s ability to maneuver by focusing on his key vulnerabilities, so that he is subsequently (as a result) unable to effectively use his combat power

[678] Operations in the rear of one&#39;s own troops

[679] The goal of operations in the rear is to ensure the freedom of action of one&#39;s own troops by protecting them, supporting combat operations and maintaining the freedom of maneuver of reserves and other units not directly involved in combat

[680] Operations in the rear have a much broader scope than logistical operations

[681] They include the following types of activities: • concentration, movement and protection of reserves or second-line units; • redeployment of forces out of contact; • support of allied forces on the territory of host states; • establishment and protection of bases and areas for the deployment of logistics facilities; • construction and protection of communication and information systems; • security and protection of civilians and facilities; • CIMIC including cooperation with local government authorities; • supply of reinforcements and troop replacements, replenishment, renewal and regeneration of forces

[682] Principles of combat operations

[683] The general principles of military operations set out in Chapter 4 of this doctrine apply in full to combat operations, for which the following principles are specific in particular: Mobility represents the ability of parts of the armed forces to move from one area to another within the required time limits while maintaining readiness (ability) to perform specified combat tasks

[684] Success in an operation (combat) will depend on the amount of information and its correct use at the right time

[685] Fulfillment of this requirement will depend on the level of communication and information systems and their interconnection with weapon systems

[686] The commanders&#39; effort must be to anticipate the adversary&#39;s intentions, based on the effective use of information, while eliminating his access to the information he needs for his decisions

[687] Controlling the airspace is one of the most important tasks of the air forces during the conduct of joint operations

[688] It creates the conditions in which operations on land, in the air and at sea can take place

[689] In all combat operations, even those in which the adversary initially has activity and freedom of action, commanders at all levels must use every opportunity to maintain or gain the initiative and to strike at the adversary

[690] Success in the operation, among other things, directly depends on the individual and collective determination of the troops to clash with the adversary and break his will to fight

[691] Maintaining the combat capability of troops is a requirement for commanders, who must make every effort to maintain the combat capability of their troops throughout the entire combat mission and must strive to achieve the objective of the operation with a minimum of their own losses

[692] Every opportunity must therefore be used to rest the troops and provide all-round security for the troops

[693] Also, replacing units exhausted by combat and replenishing personnel and material is important for restoring the combat capability of a unit

[694] Flexibility is a requirement for commanders, who must be able to respond quickly and optimally to the development of a specific combat situation during a combat operation, aware of the need to successfully achieve the final objective of the operation

[695] Elimination of the enemy

[696] The objective of the operation can be achieved by physically eliminating the enemy or causing him to lose his combat capability

[697] The importance of physical destruction of the enemy is gradually decreasing in contemporary operations (combat), and in some situations, therefore, the effort will be to achieve the defeat of the enemy by disrupting the coherence of his activities with such a combination of maneuver and firepower that he will no longer have a real opportunity or will lose the will to continue conducting combat activities

[698] Breaking the will to fight

[699] The use of deceptive measures, psychological effects, war tricks, selective use of forces and achieving surprise undermines the enemy&#39;s will to fight

[700] In this way, the commander can avoid extensive physical destruction of enemy forces and can defeat even a stronger opponent

[701] The current combat activity is not conducted only at the front edge, but throughout the entire depth of the enemy&#39;s combat formation

[702] Selective destruction of the enemy&#39;s combat force is based on disrupting the cohesion of the elements of its operational or combat formation (i.e. disrupting the functionality of the elements ensuring its mobility, command posts, logistical systems, communication and information systems, etc.), both in contact and in the depth of its operational (combat) formation

[703] Offensive operations

[704] Offensive operations are decisive combat operations

[705] They represent a set of battles, strikes and maneuvers, coordinated in terms of goal, tasks, place and time of execution according to a unified plan and carried out with the aim of gaining and maintaining the initiative, breaking the enemy&#39;s will to resist, or possibly destroying him, controlling a given area and achieving victory

[706] An offensive operation is characterized by a rapid and covert concentration of forces in a decisive direction and their surprise use, striking at the enemy&#39;s weak points, maintaining continuous pressure on the enemy and flexibly changing the direction and effort of the attack

[707] The forces and means of the ACR must be able to participate in offensive operations and participate in their implementation in a joint group in accordance with the principles of conducting allied joint operations of NATO

[708] Depending on the extent of the preparations that precede the attack and the method of their execution, the following types of attack are distinguished: attack after shortened preparation or without preparation (on the move), attack after full preparation, attack after movement from depth, attack from direct contact, close combat, attack in built-up areas

[709] An attack after shortened preparation or without preparation (on the move) is launched and conducted with the aim of preventing the enemy from organizing a defense, causing him decisive losses, outpacing him in controlling an advantageous area and gaining other advantages

[710] The surprise, speed and force of its execution can compensate for the lack of thorough preparation and other risks

[711] This type of attack places high demands on the initiative and skills of commanders of all levels and their tactical units, and on continuous cooperation between combat forces and combat support forces

[712] An attack after full preparation is characterized by the thoroughly planned and coordinated use of all offensive forces and means

[713] The emphasis is on the deliberate concentration and use of combat power at the expense of the time needed to organize the battle

[714] This type of attack is usually used in a situation where the enemy has managed to prepare his defense well

[715] An attack after a move from depth is usually carried out from the starting point or another area that is beyond the range of most of the enemy&#39;s barrel artillery

[716] The attack itself is preceded by the move of the attack group and its gradual deployment into pre-battle and combat formations

[717] An attack from direct contact with the enemy is usually carried out after a regrouping or after the replacement of troops that are in contact with the enemy

[718] The attack is characterized by a rapid and covert concentration of forces in a decisive direction and their surprising use, striking at the enemy&#39;s weak points, maintaining continuous pressure on the enemy, and flexibly changing the direction and effort of the attack

[719] A close combat can be characterized as an attack conducted against an attacking or advancing enemy, i.e. in a situation where the opposing enemy groupings as well as one&#39;s own troops strive to achieve their goals through offensive action

[720] Close combat can be conducted by formations or various groupings of troops, but in contemporary conditions it can be assumed that it will also be conducted by formations and units

[721] The aim of close combat is usually to crush the attacking enemy grouping (forces) in a short time, to gain or maintain the initiative and to create favorable conditions for further action

[722] An attack in built-up areas is physically and mentally exhausting and requires considerable time to prepare and apply special methods and techniques of combat

[723] Commanders of attacking troops must be aware that the enemy can quickly adapt any city, settlement or blocks of buildings or factory buildings for a circular defense and put up tenacious resistance in them

[724] The expansion of built-up areas is caused by the expansion of traditional human settlements – towns and villages – and by the clustering of these settlements, in which industrial facilities are usually concentrated, into urban (residential and industrial) agglomerations and regions, covering areas of hundreds of km

[725] One of the characteristics of built-up areas is that, due to the high concentration of the civilian population, they are a suitable hiding place for the enemy’s irregular forces, who can use them to mask their own intentions and thus use the built-up area to implement forms of asymmetric warfare

[726] Built-up areas therefore have a very significant impact on the activities of attacking troops

[727] The traditional tactic of bypassing built-up areas (typical of the period of “classical” wars) is no longer successful today

[728] On the contrary, the objectives of the operation can only be achieved if the attacking troops control the activities in these areas

[729] Defensive operations

[730] Defensive operations are operations conducted with the aim of stopping the enemy&#39;s attack, exhausting his offensive capabilities, holding vital areas and creating conditions for subsequent offensive activity (operation) or ending the conflict

[731] The Czech Armed Forces must be able to comprehensively secure and conduct a defensive operation within a joint multinational grouping, perform tasks to ensure the protection and defense of the territory of the Czech Republic and the movement and movement of allied troops on its territory, the integrity and sovereignty of the Czech Republic

[732] The Czech Armed Forces may conduct a defensive operation independently or may be part of a joint multinational operation

[733] Defense is a basic, but not decisive type of combat

[734] Defense can cause losses to the enemy and prevent him from achieving success, hold space, gain time and create conditions for his own active activity, but it is not possible to win a battle and a war using it alone

[735] Therefore, defensive operations and defensive combat are considered an important, but not decisive form of conducting an armed conflict

[736] They require a persistent search for an opportunity to go on the offensive

[737] Defensive operations include not only defensive but also offensive activities that intertwine and complement each other, thus allowing to save 81 forces and resources to conduct active activities in other important directions or to take the initiative in an area where conditions are created for taking the initiative

[738] The general goal of defense is to repel (stop) the attack of an enemy who is in the lead, to weaken and exhaust his forces, to hold strategically, operationally or tactically important areas and objects, to gain time to bring in reserves or to regroup forces and resources, and to create conditions for going on the offensive

[739] Another objective of defense may be to save forces and resources in a certain direction so that superiority of forces can be achieved in another direction, securing the flanks of the group leading the attack or protecting resources, objects or the population, etc

[740] Meeting the requirements for defense and thus the success of defense is achieved primarily by maximizing the use of defense advantages, skillfully concealing and eliminating defense weaknesses, disrupting the enemy&#39;s preparation for an attack and reducing its strike force, maintaining the integrity of the defense, and deceiving the enemy

[741] Depending on the strength, composition and intentions of the enemy, the situation, the task and capabilities of its own troops, the nature of the terrain and the objectives of the defense operation, the forces of the Czech Armed Forces may participate in the leadership or independently conduct a defense operation in the form of maneuver defense, positional defense or delayed combat

[742] Maneuver defense consists of the skillful organization of the defense formation and the combination of offensive and defensive activities and delayed combat

[743] It assumes (allows) a partial loss of territory with the aim of causing the attacker the greatest possible losses, exhausting him, imposing his own method of action on him, and preserving his own forces for a decisive defeat of the enemy in the depth of his own formation (defended territory

[744] Positional defense is aimed at maintaining a designated area through persistent defensive action by the main forces of the defending troops in defensive positions and active action by small mobile reserves

[745] Depending on the task, the capabilities of the troops, and the terrain, its main effort is placed closer to the front edge

[746] It depends on 82 timely detection of the enemy&#39;s main effort and rapid and skillful concentration of forces and resources in the areas most favorable for conducting the defense

[747] Depending on the situation, it is appropriate and possible to combine both types of defense

[748] In such a case, the static elements of the operational (combat) formation are used to contain, direct, weaken and stop the enemy, and the dynamic elements are used to strike, maneuver to the flank and rear of the enemy and to destroy him

[749] The combination of maneuver and positional defense requires commanders to adapt the defense to the given situation and the capabilities of the combat force without rigidly adhering to individual types

[750] Delayed combat is a form of defensive combat in which the defender, who is under pressure from the enemy, gains time at the cost of losing space

[751] The goal is to delay (slow down) and exhaust the enemy&#39;s attacking forces, gain time and at the same time maintain the combat power of one&#39;s own troops at the maximum level

[752] Delayed combat does not bring decisions on the battlefield, but while maintaining the flexibility and freedom of action of commanders and at the cost of the conscious loss of a certain part of space, it creates the conditions for subsequent decisive action

[753] Transition phases between individual types of combat activity

[754] In current operational practice, a number of combinations of activities can be used that can lead to the success of an operation

[755] It is currently typical that in one area of operation, when performing one task, units will simultaneously perform combat and non-combat tasks, and the adversary may appear at an unpredictable moment or may fundamentally change his behavior

[756] This is especially true in asymmetric operations

[757] The commander will often be put in a situation where he will have to decide to switch from attack to defense or from defense to counterattack, if the situation on the battlefield requires or allows it

[758] In both cases, his decision must be made in accordance with the intention of his superior

[759] The latter must be informed of this fact as soon as possible

[760] The commander must be aware of these facts when planning operations

[761] Therefore, he must consider all variants of combat and non-combat activities when planning an operation and be able to organize the transition between them

[762] In combat operations, the following most typical forms of transition phases between individual types of combat activity can be specified:11 movement to the line of contact; engagement; pursuit; operations to establish contact; retreat; retreating combat; detachment; regrouping of forces in combat, as well as others

[763] Peculiarities of asymmetric operations

[764] Armed resistance by paramilitary groups or armed civilians acting against regular armed forces or against the civilian population in the form of terrorist actions represents a conflict that is asymmetric in its goal, content and form of leadership

[765] Resistance by paramilitary groups can be an integral part and continuation of an internal conflict, e.g. a civil war

[766] These activities can also be partial, planned phases of a single operation

[767] Asymmetric operations mean an armed conflict in which an adversary acts against regular armed forces that is incomparable in terms of goals, methods, means of warfare and armament

[768] Asymmetricity is manifested in the fact that regular armed forces conduct combat activities according to the rules expressed in doctrines, while the adversary uses methods that are often in conflict with international humanitarian law and generally recognized ethical and moral principles

[769] Against such an adversary, methods of combat are used that must respond to the peculiarities of his non-traditional actions

[770] The conduct of asymmetric operations against an asymmetric adversary has a number of specific features, in which the following facts must be taken into account in particular: • the political and military resolution of the crisis must correspond to the clearly expressed final state of the operation; • the legitimacy of the operations must be clarified and generally accepted; • military force must be used proportionately and selectively; • the importance of political management of the armed forces increases in these operations; • civil and military intelligence must form a single integrated flow; • increased consideration must be given to the interests and needs of the civilian population; The term asymmetric operations began to be used frequently, especially after September 11, 2001, in connection with the increase in organized terrorism and operations to suppress it

[771] Asymmetry has always been a part of military affairs, and in history we know only a few examples of typically &quot;symmetrical&quot; operations

[772] Most wars began with the fight of armies that were incomparable in many aspects

[773] The fight of an organized resistance movement in the form of guerrilla warfare is a typical example of asymmetric operations

[774] Nowadays, with the development of technology and the deepening of civilizational and cultural differences, asymmetry is much more pronounced than in the past

[775] Modern armies of democratic countries are usually armed and organized to defeat an opponent who defends a territory (the area of operation

[776] An asymmetric opponent usually has other goals and does not only affect regular armed forces, which are incomparable to its combat capabilities

[777] Asymmetric operations are characterized by the involvement of mainly smaller guerrilla units, rebels and terrorists, using the element of surprise

[778] Typical for these operations are covert actions aimed at creating confusion and chaos, and in the case of terrorists, often also at causing great losses to the civilian population or operating in places with high social or economic sensitivity

[779] • the success of the operation is conditioned by the isolation of the armed resistance forces (terrorist groups) from the surroundings; • the operation includes the protection of the neutral or government-sympathetic population, the defense of one&#39;s own operational bases; • the elimination of the intelligence network of the resistance forces (terrorists) is one of the basic conditions for the success of the operation; • the condition for the final solution is only the physical or political elimination of the armed resistance

[780] The key tasks of operations against an asymmetric adversary are: locating (finding) the adversary; engaging resistance fighters (terrorists) and directly fighting them; collecting intelligence; observation, monitoring and investigation; mediation; surveillance; patrolling; protection of convoys; search; protective and defensive operations of one&#39;s own troops and the civilian population; offensive operations (raids

[781] The ACR must prepare for operations in asymmetric operations in its armament, when developing the principles of conducting combat activities and training

[782] Commanders and staffs are expected to be able to respond to the activities of the enemy, who is illegible and unpredictable

[783] CHAPTER 6 NON-COMBAT OPERATIONS 88 NON-COMBAT OPERATIONS

[784] Non-combat operations are military operations conducted by the armed forces, which do not systematically use military means, i.e. forces and weapons, to achieve the objectives of the operation

[785] Non-combat operations also include NATO crisis management operations (Non-Article 5 Crises Response Operation – CRO) outside of Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty

[786] Weapons may be used for self-defense of troops or in some specific phases of the operation as a last resort

[787] Non-combat operations are divided into peacekeeping operations12 and support operations

[788] Peacekeeping operations are operations in which diplomatic, civilian and military means are used impartially, usually in accordance with the principles and principles of the UN Charter, to restore or maintain peace in a crisis area

[789] They may include measures to prevent conflicts, create peace, enforce peace, keep peace, build peace and humanitarian operations

[790] Peacekeeping operations are usually conducted on the basis of a decision of the UN Security Council (SC) or on the basis of a consultation mechanism agreed between NATO and the EU

[791] They are conducted with the consent of the states on whose territory they are carried out, on the basis of a decision of the UN Security Council or other international bodies after the conclusion of a peace agreement between the participating states in a crisis situation

[792] The mission of peacekeeping operations is to create a safe environment in which civilian organisations will be able to restore the infrastructure necessary for lasting peace

[793] The term “stabilisation operations” is currently being gradually introduced for this type of operation

[794] In terms of content, the two terms are synonymous

[795] Support operations are operations in which assistance (usually humanitarian) is provided to the population and civilian authorities and organizations as a response to large-scale non-military threats to the civilian population or the environment

[796] They are a response to situations caused by disasters or other causes, when human lives, property and cultural values are at risk and when the forces and resources of the responsible authorities are insufficient to resolve them

[797] In this context, they also include search and rescue operations

[798] The Czech Armed Forces may participate in non-combat operations within a multinational group abroad or may be used for operations controlled by the Czech government on the territory of the Czech Republic

[799] These are mostly assistance operations in support of the Czech Police or operations within the activities of the integrated rescue system of the Czech Republic

[800] In rare cases, they may involve operations of the Czech Republic abroad, controlled by the Czech government

[801] The principles of these operations are described separately in Chapter 7 of the doctrine

[802] In all types of non-combat operations, it is necessary to keep in mind their humanitarian character

[803] Their common and at the same time primary objective is, first of all, to create conditions for ending tension and normalizing the life of the population in an area threatened by conflict or natural disaster and to assist them

[804] If operations are to be associated with limited use of force, they should not endanger the lives of the population

[805] Achieving the set goals through the long-term use of force may not be the best and most secure solution for the future

[806] Peacekeeping operations are not the same as non-combat operations, they are only a part of them

[807] There are several categories of non-combat operations: military assistance in the event of a disaster, support (assistance) to the police in an anti-drug or anti-terrorist campaign, military advice or support to friendly governments

[808] The deployment of armed forces usually occurs even in times of peace, and it may not be a peacekeeping operation, but local conflicts or even national, interstate or multinational conflicts that have occurred without a formal declaration of war

[813] It is always necessary to keep in mind the final goal and the long-term social, economic and political context

[822] The requirement for the protection of troops and the civilian population is of exceptional importance in non-combat operations

[828] This is achieved through the transparency and credibility of their behavior

[829] This requires a high level of professionalism and high moral and psychological resilience

[830] Without impartiality, there is no prospect of maintaining the trust and cooperation of the warring parties

[831] It is not enough for peacekeeping forces to simply act impartially; they must be considered impartial by the parties involved in the conflict

[839] The potential of troops to use force is one of the decisive aspects in achieving the objective of the operation

[857] Peacekeeping operations

[858] Diplomatic measures, which usually precede military operations, are decisive for the political solution of the objective in peacekeeping operations

[860] This process may be supported by subsequent military operations

[862] The aim of a military operation is to contribute to the political settlement of a dispute and thus prevent the emergence of an armed conflict

[863] Military activities may have different focuses in accordance with political requirements: 93 • warning interested parties to a potential conflict; • monitoring the development of the security situation; • ensuring training and reform of the security system of the country of the potential conflict; • preventive deployment of forces in the area of the potential conflict

[865] They may include the provision of recognized organizations, mediation of reconciliation, diplomatic pressure, isolation, sanctions, embargoes or other activities approved by the governing international political organizations

[866] Peace-making is carried out primarily by diplomatic means

[867] However, military assistance in peace-making is also possible, either indirectly (staff support or planning) or in the form of direct deployment of military assets

[868] Sanctions and embargoes declared by the UN Security Council can help prevent a conflict, but they can also be used in its later stages if the conflict cannot be prevented

[869] Given their organization and combat potential, military forces can enforce and supervise compliance with sanctions and embargoes

[870] This is achieved by observing and patrolling by all means, both from land and air, and using naval and river resources

[871] Peace-keeping operations are operations that follow the conclusion of an agreement or a ceasefire, which has created a favourable environment with a high degree of agreement and understanding and a low level of threat of disruption

[872] The use of force by peacekeepers is usually limited to self-defense

[873] Peace-enforcement operations are operations conducted to achieve a ceasefire or compliance with a peace agreement in situations where the degree of agreement and understanding between the parties concerned is uncertain and the threat of non-compliance is significant

[874] Peacekeepers must be able to use appropriate coercive force and means and must impartially enforce the ceasefire or the establishment of a peace agreement

[875] Peacekeeping operations are generally conducted in accordance with the principles of Chapter VI of the UN Charter to monitor and facilitate the implementation of peace agreements

[876] Loss of consent and failure to comply with obligations by a party can limit the freedom of action of peacekeepers and even jeopardize the continuation of the mission

[877] The requirement to maintain impartiality limits the use of force by peacekeepers

[878] Maintaining and enforcing consent is the main purpose of peacekeeping

[879] Peacekeeping operations are usually conducted in accordance with the principles of Chapter VII of the UN Charter

[880] They are of a coercive nature, with the aim of ensuring compliance with peace agreements, and are carried out in the absence of the consent of all parties to the conflict or in cases where consent may be uncertain

[881] They are intended to maintain or restore peace, or to enforce the terms of the mandate

[882] In carrying out these operations, there must be a very close link between military and political objectives

[883] It is very important to remember that the aim of peace enforcement is not to defeat or destroy the adversary, but rather to induce, compel and persuade the parties to adapt to the course of individual activities

[884] The formation of military forces with adequate coercive combat capabilities is important for any decision to deploy Alliance forces in a peace operation

[885] Post-conflict peace-building operations are peace operations that follow the resolution of the root causes of the conflict in order to provide for the long-term needs of the population and to create a peaceful environment in society

[886] The operation uses complementary diplomatic, civilian and, if necessary, military 95 means

[887] This peacekeeping operation requires a demanding and long-term effort and may be conducted in parallel with other types of peacekeeping operations

[888] Peacebuilding includes activities in favor of political, economic, social and military measures with the aim of promoting a political settlement of the conflict

[889] It is a mechanism for recognizing and supporting structures that are aimed at consolidating peace, strengthening credibility, well-being and economic recovery

[890] It often happens that once the conflict is over, the attention of the international community shifts elsewhere, the peacebuilding phase of the operation is under-resourced and the operation reaches a dead end

[891] Peacebuilding therefore requires the commitment of humanitarian and creative resources to a long-term political process

[893] They may precede, run concurrently with or complement the work of specialized civilian humanitarian organizations

[894] Humanitarian operations may be organized with the participation of military forces separately or within the framework of peacekeeping operations

[895] As a rule, they will be organized within the framework of the UN or the NATO crisis management system

[898] For example, if the operational level commander has concluded an agreement with the party leader on a specific course of action, then he should do everything in his power to ensure that the agreement is extended to those party members who are introduced to his tactical commanders in the area

[1160] OVERVIEW OF KEY TERMS Alliance

[1161] An alliance based on the conclusion of official treaties between two or more states to achieve common long-term goals

[1162] It is an association of sovereign states based on an adopted international treaty act to achieve political, economic or defense goals

[1163] It is characterized by the transfer of part of sovereignty to the hands of common alliance bodies and the coordination of efforts in a certain area (military or other

[1164] Participating states can cancel their commitments if their interests are threatened

[1165] Combat operations Combat operations are military operations in which at least one of the warring parties did not agree with the activities of the other party or the activities of allied (coalition) troops and which, in principle, achieve their goals through combat

[1166] Doctrine Doctrine generally represents a set of distinct opinions on a certain problem

[1167] In the military environment, it represents an expression of a system of principles and guidelines that govern the activities of the armed forces when performing tasks in military operations

[1168] Doctrine of the Czech Armed Forces It represents a set of principles for the preparation and conduct of operations in which the Czech Armed Forces can participate within a multinational grouping or conduct them independently

[1169] It is the common doctrine of all the Czech Armed Forces

[1170] It creates a framework and basis for the content of documents of the Czech Armed Forces doctrinal system

[1171] Coalition A purpose-based agreement between two or more states on a joint approach to resolving international political, economic, military and other problems

[1172] Combatant A member of the organized armed forces, groups or units of one of the parties to the conflict who has the right to directly participate in hostilities

[1173] A member of the armed forces is distinguished by wearing a uniform or by an attached distinguishing sign and by publicly carrying a weapon

[1174] If, due to the nature of the hostilities, it is not possible to distinguish a combatant, a combatant is considered to be anyone who openly carries a weapon during a military engagement or if he is visible to the enemy when moving towards the place from which the combat action begins

[1175] The status of a combatant is also held by residents of unoccupied territories who spontaneously and en masse take up arms when the enemy approaches, who openly carry weapons and observe the principles of international humanitarian law

[1176] The provisions of international humanitarian law fully apply to a combatant; in the event of his capture, he is granted the status of a prisoner of war

[1177] Persons assigned to perform non-combat tasks (for example, medical personnel, war correspondents, military chaplains) and non-combatant civilians and children under the protection of international humanitarian law

[1178] Crisis operations Multifunctional military operations outside Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty, including such political, military and civilian activities that are initiated and carried out in accordance with international law (including international humanitarian law) and that contribute to conflict prevention and crisis management

[1179] Peacekeeping operations Peacekeeping operations are operations in which diplomatic, civilian and military means are used impartially, usually in accordance with the principles and principles of the UN Charter, to restore or maintain peace

[1180] These operations may include conflict prevention, peacemaking, peace enforcement, peacekeeping, peacebuilding and humanitarian operations

[1181] Multinational operations Multinational operations are military operations of forces and assets of two or more states that cooperate in the performance of a task

[1182] For the purposes of doctrine, this term is used to describe activities, operations and organizations that are carried out and maintained by a given (member) state alone without the assistance of allies or without cooperation with them

[1183] National infrastructure Infrastructure that is established and financed by a NATO member state on its own territory solely for its own armed forces (including forces that are assigned or pre-designated for NATO

[1184] National operations National operations are military operations conducted at the national level by only one state

[1185] Any national armed force composed of one or more types of troops under the command of one national commander, assigned to a NATO commander

[1186] National command A command that is created and operates under the authority of a given state

[1187] It may or may not be subordinate to a NATO commander

[1188] National commander The commander of the armed forces of a given state, who is usually not included in the NATO command system

[1189] Non-combat operations Non-combat operations are operations in which the forces and means of the armed forces are used without the pre-planned use of force (weapons and ammunition

[1190] However, in exceptional situations, weapons may also be used during them, in particular in the self-defense of soldiers attacked while performing non-combat tasks, or in the defense of the civilian population attacked with weapons

[1191] Defensive operations Defensive operations are operations conducted with the aim of stopping an enemy attack, exhausting its offensive capabilities, holding vital areas and creating conditions for subsequent offensive activity (operation) or ending the conflict

[1192] The Czech Armed Forces must be able to comprehensively secure and conduct defensive activities within a joint multinational group, perform tasks to ensure the protection of the territory of the Czech Republic and the movement and movement of allied troops on its territory, the integrity and sovereignty of the Czech Republic, which it can carry out independently or which can be part of a joint multinational operation

[1193] Collective defense operations An operation conducted in accordance with Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty to defend the common area

[1194] Operational planning Operational planning is a process that allows the operational commander to develop military-strategic objectives into comprehensive plans for military operations coordinated in time and space

[1195] Operational capabilities Capabilities that express what units should know, know and be able to implement in preparation for deployment and within the framework of an operation and combat

[1196] Rules of Engagement (ROE) ROE define uniform rules and methods of using military forces in an operation

[1197] Joint operations Joint operations are operations conducted by the joint efforts of two or more types of forces (land, air, special, naval) under a unified command

[1198] Offensive operations Offensive operations represent a set of battles, strikes and maneuvers, unified in terms of goal, tasks, place and time of execution according to a unified plan and carried out with the aim of gaining and maintaining the initiative, breaking the enemy&#39;s will to resist, or possibly destroying him, controlling a designated area and achieving victory

[1199] The forces and assets of the Czech Armed Forces must be able to participate in offensive operations (participate in their implementation) in a joint alliance group in accordance with the principles of conducting allied joint operations

[1200] Warzone A geographically defined area or territory with airspace where the armed forces of the warring parties prepare, concentrate, develop and conduct activities to achieve military-political and strategic tasks (goals

[1201] Military operations Military operations represent the basic method of concentrated and coordinated use of armed forces in combat or non-combat operations in a given space and time
