[613] Transforming UK Warfighting 4.1 The Integrated Force Model

[614] The UK has long been at the forefront of efforts to deliver a combat force that is ‘joint’ and ‘multi-domain’

[615] However, in practice, the single Services have largely evolved separately in terms of design, equipment, and training—creating siloes

[616] The result is a force that joins up only on the battlefield: the effectiveness of the Armed Forces on operations is determined by the capabilities available to each Service at the point of deployment, rather than as the result of joined-up planning and delivery

[617] For the Armed Forces to be more lethal than the sum of their parts, they must complete the journey from ‘joint’ to ‘integrated’, inverting the model so that authorities for design and delivery flow top-down from a single point of military authority

[618] This stepchange in approach is dependent on the elevation of the Chief of the Defence Staff (CDS) under Defence Reform as the head of the new Military Strategic Headquarters (MSHQ) with command over the Service Chiefs

[619] The Services must be integrated in planning, readiness, deployment, and procurement

[622] A single force design that delivers a more lethal and agile combat force

[624] This coherent combat force trains and fights across domains under a single vision, drawing on the unique strengths and expertise of the single services and Strategic Command

[625] CDS is responsible for the readiness and endurance of the Integrated Force, supported by the Service Chiefs

[626] In designing this unified force, CDS must balance investment between front-line capabilities and the foundational enablers that sustain the force in protracted, high-intensity warfare

[628] The full range of supporting activity and capabilities Such as training, exercises, infrastructure, logistics, medical services, intelligence, stockpiles, and munitions. are delivered according to a single scheme set by CDS and the MSHQ
