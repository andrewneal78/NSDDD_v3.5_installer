[61] National Security The concept of National Security in Guatemala has evolved in recent decades, beginning the change with the expansion of the traditional idea of National Security, understood restrictively as the protection of the State against external threats to defend sovereignty, independence and territoriality

[62] In the 1990s, Central America promoted significant transformations aimed at strengthening institutions and respecting the rule of law with the signing of the Framework Treaty on Democratic Security in Central America, which establishes as a priority the consolidation of peace, freedom, democracy, and development of the member countries of the Central American Integration System

[63] A new regional security model, unique, comprehensive, and indivisible, is being established to achieve peace and integration. It is known as the Central American Model of Democratic Security. This model prioritizes the security of people and their property, as well as the security of state institutions. This introduces a significant change that entails an anthropocentric approach framed within the concept of human security

[64] The Declaration on Security in the Americas, issued at the Special Conference on Security, Mexico 2003, of the Organization of American States, determines that the threats, concerns, and other challenges to hemispheric security are diverse in nature and multidimensional in scope

[65] It establishes that each State has the sovereign right to identify its own security priorities and define strategies, plans, and actions to address threats, reduce risks, and vulnerabilities in accordance with its legal system and with the support of international law

[66] In this sense, the phenomena the State must address are closely related and must therefore be addressed comprehensively, requiring multifaceted responses from various actors and, in certain circumstances, partnerships between the government, the private sector, and organized society, all acting in accordance with the democratic principles and constitutional norms of the State

[67] In 2008, the State of Guatemala adopted the concept of Democratic Security, which is embodied in the Framework Law of the National Security System, Decree Number 18-2008 of the Congress of the Republic, and concerns "the action of the State that guarantees respect, promotion and protection of security, the full exercise of human rights and the creation of conditions that allow individuals to develop personally, family and socially in peace, freedom and democracy

[68] In this way, the conceptualization of security in Guatemala has a specific meaning

[69] National Security encompasses "the set of principles, policies, objectives, strategies, procedures, agencies, functions, and responsibilities of the State's components in matters of security, which guarantee its independence, sovereignty, and integrity, and the fundamental rights of the population established in the Political Constitution of the Republic of Guatemala, consolidating peace, development, justice, and respect for human rights

[70] The concept of National Security includes both the security of the State, to guarantee its independence, sovereignty, and integrity, and the security of the human person, whose fundamental rights must be guaranteed

[71] From an anthropocentric perspective, it is based on the concept of democratic security, in which the central objective is the human person, their personal, family, and social development in peace, freedom, and democracy

[203] Guatemala continues to contribute to international stability through its participation in United Nations Peacekeeping and Peace Enforcement Missions, in addition to its support for the training of personnel from member countries of the Conference of Central American Armed Forces and partner countries at the Regional Peacekeeping Operations Training Center (CREOMPAZ

[204] Fragmented Approach to National Security By 2024, the root causes of socioeconomic vulnerabilities will not be addressed and corruption levels will increase

[205] Low national and foreign investment reduces economic and social development and exacerbates social inequalities

[206] The homicide rate per 100,000 inhabitants has risen to levels similar to those of Honduras and El Salvador due to institutional vulnerability and isolated efforts in security and development

[207] Extortion continues to rise steadily, as does robbery and theft of property, which is linked to the weak institutional coordination between the security, intelligence, and justice sectors

[208] The absence and implementation of new care models means that extortion remains the main source of financing for criminal organizations

[209] The illegal trafficking of firearms, ammunition, and explosives is increasing considerably

[210] As a result, organized crime is strengthened compared to the capabilities of police and military forces

[211] Money laundering and other asset laundering affect the country's economy, causing inflation, loss of public revenue, and exchange rate problems

[212] Guatemala's international image is affected and it is considered a tax haven due to weak inter-institutional engagement between the security, justice, and financial sectors

[213] Social conflict is worsening, mechanisms for dialogue have been exhausted, and violence is escalating to ever-increasing levels, affecting people and their property

[214] Governance is at a critical juncture, following the imposition of weak political leadership, a fragile rule of law, and the use of force as a last resort to control the state

[215] The depletion and/or degradation of renewable and non-renewable resources is still not considered a priority; these resources are not properly managed by authorities and society

[216] The lack of sustainable development policies in areas susceptible to deforestation contributes to the decline of forests, which in turn contributes to the reduction of the water table

[217] Disasters caused by natural or man-made phenomena are increasingly likely, have greater impact, and occur within a short time window, generating loss of human life and critical infrastructure, affecting the country's economy

[218] The National Coordinator for Disaster Reduction's system and local capacities are not adequate to address the magnitude of the events, affecting the population's resilience

[219] Flooding and climate-related disasters have contributed to the spread of disease, which is linked to an increased risk of epidemics

[220] Guatemala is a country of transit, return, origin, and destination for migrants whose main destination is the United States of America. The absence of regulatory frameworks on immigration controls and the omission of development actions in the areas most prone to migrant populations favors the displacement of people

[221] Irregular migration flows continue to increase over short periods of time

[222] State institutions maintain a reactive response to this risk

[223] Likewise, suspected migrants linked to transnational organized crime and individuals associated with terrorist groups have been identified

[224] Poor planning and investment in infrastructure, human resources, and technology render the National Migration Institute ineffective

[225] The advancement of Information and Communications Technologies (ICTs) in Guatemala favors the development and evolution of crimes such as extortion, robbery and theft of property, child pornography, harassment, intellectual and/or industrial property rights, fraud, among others, as well as identity theft and impersonation

[226] The lack of investment in human resources and equipment to combat cybercrime, along with the lack of regulatory frameworks, is leading to an increase in these crimes

[227] Some state and non-state actors seek to destabilize through social media and influence ungovernability

[228] The telecommunications and financial sectors are affected by individual actors and national and international organizations

[229] Cybersecurity and cyberdefense capabilities are limited by a lack of integration, human resources, and technology

[230] The political and international conflict between the People's Republic of China and the United States of America threatens political stability and international and regional governance

[231] Guatemala's leadership is being affected by the increasing presence of governments that do not share democratic ideals

[232] The tensions are reflected in the abandonment of Central American cooperation and integration mechanisms
