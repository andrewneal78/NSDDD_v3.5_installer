[1] PRESENTATION Under the premise that peace is a social construction, the following Guatemalan National Defense Policy is presented to the Guatemalan people, neighboring countries, and the entire world

[2] This Policy establishes the framework for building the conditions Guatemala needs and demands for the defense of its sovereignty, the integrity of its borders, the protection of its territory, and a firm and lasting peace within a context of regional cooperation

[3] It will also contribute to creating an environment conducive to the comprehensive development of Guatemalans and to shaping national security as a common good based on the rule of law in a strengthened democracy

[4] The National Defense Policy was conceived to be part of and complement the functions of the National Security System, an entity that, through the combined efforts of all public entities of the Nation's power, and with the active participation of its citizens, will generate a system with adequate defense capabilities, in line with the country's political and strategic needs, and that will be affordable in relation to national resources, yet acceptable to address potential risks, which are constantly evolving within the framework of international security

[5] The construction of this system of capabilities, under the Strategic and Legal Framework governing the operation of the National Defense System, will be implemented in three complementary phases: adaptation, modernization, and transformation

[6] This process pursues the fulfillment of a specific set of general and specific objectives to drive the modernization of defense organization and management, and to generate consistency between the force's design and its current, potential, and prospective operational missions

[7] The results achieved during its implementation and development will be continuously monitored and measured to ensure efficient resource allocation in generating the required capabilities

[8] The effectiveness of these capabilities in achieving concrete results will result in a strong and lasting correlation between the progress achieved in security and sustainable socioeconomic development

[9] The National Defense Policy is based on the principles established in the Book of National Defense of the Republic of Guatemala, which was developed with the participation of the public, political, university, social communication, academic sectors, the Congress of the Republic of Guatemala, indigenous peoples, the Human Rights Ombudsman's Office, professional associations, and other sectors of Guatemalan society

[204] They translate Cabal Defense into clear elements of systemic execution, with a clear understanding of how results will be achieved if they are integrated harmoniously and sufficiently into the whole

[205] General Objective for the Structuring of the System

[206] Structure the National Defense System with the entities that, due to the nature of their functions, must integrate it, establishing the functions of the System and developing the relationship protocols between its components

[207] Specific Objectives: a. Recover the operational capabilities of the National Defense Core under a cost-risk assessment

[208] based on a model with a Reaction Interdiction capability, capable of being deployed in four simultaneous scenarios. Its use in the field of Homeland Security must adhere to existing protocols, focusing on aspects of terrain control and security, through a Capability Design model within an Integrated Defense Planning and Management System

[209] b. Develop the Nation's Military Reserves in a Deterrence Integration capability model, by strengthening the current administrative organization and establishing the necessary concentration areas to increase the nation's response capacity through strategic mobilization

[210] Develop Civil Defense so that, when activated, it serves to ensure the continuity of government operations and public order in situations of national emergency and calamity, with the participation of local authorities and other organizations

[212] e. Harmonize the diplomatic efforts of Defense with the action guidelines of the Foreign Policy of the State of Guatemala

[213] General Objective for System Management

[214] Develop the Integrated Defense Planning and Management System to become the primary tool for the proper development and operation of the National Defense System

[215] Specific Objectives: a. Implement the Integrated Defense Planning and Management System at the Core of National Defense, through the processes of Capability Planning, Performance Programming, and Results-Based Budgeting

[216] b. Progressively expand the Integrated Defense Planning and Management System to the rest of the components of the National Defense System

[217] INTEGRATED DEFENSE PLANNING AND MANAGEMENT SYSTEM The System consists of value chains that convert requirements into capabilities, these in turn into programs and finally these into results aligned with the objectives established for National Defense

[218] This is achieved through three interdependent components, whose operation follows a sequential logic in that the outputs of one become inputs of the next

[219] Each input is processed through a method that adopts integrated management frameworks and utilizes a decision-support platform

[220] The three components or processes of the Integrated Defense Planning and Management System are: • Capability Planning

[221] • Performance Programming

[222] • Budgeting by Results

[223] A. CAPABILITY PLANNING

[224] Capability Planning is the process that allows problematic situations to be transformed into feasible solutions

[225] It uses Force Design as a method which, based on the problematic situation, develops requirements to satisfy the manifest needs

[226] The appropriate grouping of these requirements into Capability-Based Programs is called Mission Areas

[227] These mission areas are embodied in force packages, combining units of diverse nature, capable of adapting to different scenarios

[228] These packages can be broken down into one or more capability groups oriented toward problem solutions; these subgroups are called capability equations

[229] Capabilities are defined based on the multiplicity of threat manifestations, not the actors involved. This creates a cost-risk model that overcomes the ambiguity and uncertainty surrounding the current National Security and Defense landscape, responding to political guidelines in terms of solving Defense problems and providing support to the various areas of Security

[230] Capability equations are built by integrating three elements: A force structure that integrates the organization, human and material resources, and weapons systems; Regulatory elements, which constitute the rules of engagement, doctrine, training, and capacity building; and The strategic conception, which includes the mission, political guidelines, and current laws

[231] For the comprehensive management of this process, operational and evaluation protocols are required to ensure that the capabilities effectively solve the problems

[232] The capacity projects that are integrated into this process are the inputs for the next phase

[233] B. PERFORMANCE PROGRAMMING

[234] This process seeks to prioritize the impact to be achieved

[235] The methodology allows for achieving strategic results by aligning the objectives of defense institutions with the objectives of the National Security Policy and the demands of society

[236] It is based on Complex Project Management, since defense projects are long-term and exposed to exogenous variables, in addition to the economic magnitudes and the scope of the coordination that must be established in the political, economic, social, as well as scientific and technological fields

[237] This complexity is reflected in the Master Plan, which encompasses programs and projects that seek to respond to demand

[238] This allows for the selection of those who, in light of current conditions and with a vision for the future, are priorities for state policies and who ensure that the scarce resources available produce what is expected in relation to defense

[239] This decision at the highest level of management is deposited in the so-called Master Plan

[240] C. BUDGETING BY RESULTS

[241] The master plan undergoes a third process to turn intentions into reality by optimally allocating resources to the actors empowered to execute the defense

[242] The preferred method is Strategic Alignment, which consists of allocating resources to obtain measurable products and results that correspond to the highest-level political guidelines

[243] In this way, the financial reflection of the strategic priorities of the capabilities required to generate the desired results is constructed

[244] This seeks to provide an effective response to the demands and needs of the population in accordance with the allocated resources

[245] In parallel, the Metrics Plan is being developed for the evaluation, development, and maintenance of the strategic planning system, containing the indicators, variables, and scales to measure the planned results

[246] National Defense Policy This budgetary exercise, executable and measurable, is materialized in the Institutional Strategic Plan (PEI) and is translated annually into the Annual Operating Plan (POA

[247] The entire system is subject to ongoing monitoring and evaluation to detect changes for new addressing

[248] The description of the operation of each component of the System will be reflected in the Integrated Defense Planning and Management System Procedures Manual (SIPLAGDE

[249] Since there are minor variations in budget and results, the interactions will focus on the third process

[250] In the presence of contingent situations that imply significant variations in the available resources as well as in political decisions, mainly in the area of Security, their realignment and new reprioritization must be sought from the second process (Performance Programming). When the nature of the problem to be solved changes, requiring the development of new capabilities, it is imperative to use the methodology from the first step

[251] National Defense Policy ANNEXES I. METHODOLOGY FOR THE PREPARATION, REVIEW AND FUTURE UPDATE OF THE NATION'S DEFENSE POLICY

[252] This process began with a Differential Diagnosis that included Analytical Modeling, which reviewed the legal framework, environment, and context of the Defense, seeking anything that would reveal existing problems that needed to be resolved

[253] This was followed by Exploratory Modeling, where future scenarios were constructed, which, after being reviewed using the initial parameters, resulted in a preliminary diagnosis of the Problem, thus closing the first cycle of the method. This was repeated as many times as necessary until the Defense Problem was clearly defined

[254] The next step was Formative Modeling, in which the structural premises for addressing the problem identified in the diagnosis were established; after which, a response to it was constructed

[255] To this end, larger objectives were created in line with the premises, thus closing this second cycle that was followed until it was consolidated and the National Defense was established as the answer to the problem

[256] In the following cycle of Successive Conditions, it was verified that the linking of the solution formed with the problem posed responded to the demands of the problem found in the differential diagnosis, then moving on to the consideration of the Measurement Criteria and defining the management of the change that must occur, thus carrying out a programmatic model, which materializes in an Integrated Defense Planning and Management System

[258] National Defense Policy

[259] Mission Areas

[260] These are responses conceived based on the nation's defense and security objectives, and their purpose is to reduce the level of uncertainty surrounding clearly identified problems. To this end, capabilities are generated that, once integrated under conditions of time, space (physical or abstract), and sustainability, will be capable of producing the desired effects

[261] Value Chains

[262] An interrelated set of activities to transform inputs into products or services, through processes that each provide comparative advantages in the operational or management sphere

[263] Deterrence-Integration Capability

[264] For the Reserve, deterrence capability will be developed through a process of agile mobilization, organization, equipment, and training that allows for immediate activation to join the Defense Core in the escalation of conflict

[265] This capacity will also allow, in times of peace, the Reserve to integrate into Civil Defense efforts to address emergencies or public calamities

[266] Reaction-Interdiction Capability

[267] Reaction is understood as the ability to respond quickly and immediately, locating and deploying where required with sufficient autonomy and power to impact the situation

[268] Likewise, interdiction means prohibiting, limiting, or restricting the use or free access to territorial spaces of vectors that are classified as risks or threats, through actions aimed at neutralizing, delaying, or destroying their potential before it can be used against national security

[269] The term reaction-interdiction capability means that units will acquire one or both of these capabilities in accordance with their intended use

[270] Decision Cycles

[271] These are information flows between decision-makers, who possess a wealth of knowledge based on their level within an organization or institution, with the goal of ensuring that the courses of action they choose can reduce risk levels and increase the levels of efficiency and effectiveness that their vision and mission demand

[272] Capability Equations: This is the materialization of the response to a problem within the mission areas, which include the integration of the following elements: mobility, permanence, offensive power, defensive power, sustainment, tactical flexibility and versatility, within a concept of interoperability that allows immediate response times and synchronization of results, through the breadth of control, distribution on the ground, and the available logistical structure

[273] Interoperability

[274] It is to establish mutually complementary relationships between the elements of the National Defense System, ensuring the operational, technical, strategic, and cognitive work of its components, with the goal of operating efficiently within the inter-institutional framework of the State, under the determinations of the National Defense Policy and in accordance with the National Security System

[275] Power Packs

[276] It consists of the concentration of human, material, and technological resources ready to be used for specific functions under clearly defined parameters, supported by specific doctrine and the corresponding legal basis

[277] Force packages are composed of one or more Capacity Equations

[278] It is the tool that encompasses programs and projects that seek to respond to demand and that allows for the selection of those that, in light of current conditions and with a vision for the future, are priorities for State policies and that ensure that the scarce resources available produce what is expected in relation to defense

[279] It constitutes an essential element of strategic budget planning

[280] It is developed by identifying the Ministry of National Defense's political priorities, translated into financial elements of capability requirements, within the budgetary ceiling, in order to produce the results necessary to achieve the defense objectives within the government's agenda

[281] Budgeting by Results

[282] It is a public management strategy that links the allocation of resources to measurable products and results for the benefit of the population

[283] It establishes the determination of those responsible, procedures for generating results information, products and institutional management tools, as well as accountability

[285] This capacity is built on the basis of the lessons learned process, which incorporates new knowledge into the Organization
