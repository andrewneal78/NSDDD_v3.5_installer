[896] ARMY RELATIONS WITH THE PUBLIC 7 The interests of the military defence of the state require that the army be maximally integrated into a democratic society

[897] This is also required by the principle that the military defence of the Czech Republic is based on the universal principle

[898] Only by fulfilling this condition can the army properly fulfil its mission

[899] However, the public in a democratic society usually perceives the army as a somewhat alien element

[900] This is due both to its special position in society in peacetime and to the specifics of its management and the nature of the activities to which it is trained

[901] The experience of the armies of democratic states has shown that unregulated and spontaneous information about defense and the army can have a disruptive effect on ensuring the needs of the state's military defense

[902] This process must be supplemented by purposeful information activities and substantive cooperation with the public, in which the army must significantly participate

[903] Therefore, responsibility for the army's contact with the public is part of the command responsibility

[904] Public relations is therefore a reciprocal and purposeful information and substantive cooperation between the army and its environment, which takes place in the process of multilateral communication on issues of the state's military defense

[905] The purpose is to participate in informing the public about the army and the decisive issues of the state's military defense so that every citizen has enough information to take his own responsible position

[906] Significant demands are placed on communication between the army and the public wherever there is a transformation from a totalitarian to a democratic society and where the army is also changing within the framework of this process

[907] This was also the case in the Czech Republic

[908] For several decades, the army of a totalitarian state created an information and social barrier around itself in order to alienate itself as much as possible from the citizens of its state and to be able to be abused against them at any time

[909] After a fundamental change in the political climate, there is also a change in communication between the army and the public

[910] It is necessary to fill the information vacuum as quickly as possible

[911] Citizens can only trust those armies about which they have sufficient information

[912] Therefore, special attention is paid to communication with the media in contact with the public

[913] This is because they provide the public with most of the current information on military defense issues and thus significantly influence the public's attitudes towards the army

[914] By providing information to the media, the current army also ensures the constitutional right of citizens to information about the army and national security

[915] The Ministry of Defense is therefore gradually creating an information system that is based on NATO standards and allows the media to provide the maximum possible, but limited in the interests of national security and law, information

[916] This system includes press conferences, thematic press conferences of top army representatives with journalists, a dispatch center for individual inquiries and the production of press materials

[917] It also includes press and information centers at military exercises

[918] Army representatives give interviews to the media, appear on television and radio, and personally publish in a number of periodicals

[919] Individual communication with individual citizens is another part of public relations

[920] It involves telephone or written answers to citizens' questions provided by military experts, usually through army lines

[921] In individual units, visits are made to barracks and military schools, organized for certain social groups or on important occasions

[922] Publications are published and distributed for the general public, which provide information about individual branches of the army or provide detailed and comprehensive information about the army, such as the Information Sheets on the Army of the Czech Republic and the Army Yearbook

[923] However, the relationship of a citizen to the army and the military defense of the state is formed even before entering the army, in childhood and youth, as part of the value orientation and education in patriotism instilled by the school, family and environment

[924] Therefore, communication is important, for example, with teachers, entrepreneurs, educators, civic associations, etc., who significantly influence the attitudes of citizens, especially young people

[925] This mainly concerns cooperation on projects in which common interests intersect, for example, the organization of youth camps, various competitions, educational activities, cooperation with military veterans' associations, etc. - Military headquarters and garrison commanders are in contact with the bodies of regional state administration and self-government through the Public Relations Council composed of representatives of the army, public administration and civil associations

[926] The meetings of these councils are an opportunity to inform representatives of the public about the activities and status of the army in the relevant region as well as to resolve any problems between the army and the public

[927] A special information service on the current status and activities of the army and a service ensuring their visits to military units are available to members of Parliament

[928] RELATIONS BETWEEN CITIZENS AND THE ARMY The Army of the Czech Republic is the basic instrument of the military defense of the Czech Republic

[929] Relations between the population and the army are therefore among the most significant and most closely watched in the spectrum of relations between society and defense

[930] The quality of the relationship between citizens and the army is reflected in their attitude towards the state, since the army is one of the instruments ensuring its sovereignty

[931] The development of relations between the population and the army is based on the civic dimension

[932] The starting point of this concept are independent and responsibly acting citizens of a state in which fundamental rights and freedoms are respected

[933] Citizens first enter into a relationship with the army as direct and immediate participants in the activities that take place in the army

[934] They are in the army voluntarily or on the basis of the law and occupy different positions in the military hierarchy

[935] However, their responsibility for the military defense of the state takes on a concrete form only in their approach to military service, especially in the quality of their actions in the performance of military duties in peacetime and in crisis situations

[936] The civic dimension of the relationship between the army and the population also includes consistent respect for the fundamental rights and freedoms granted by the state

[937] This means that soldiers have, apart from exceptions provided for by law, the same rights and freedoms as civilians and that these norms are binding rules for internal relations in the army

[938] Their observance, regardless of the established rank and function, is again a manifestation of individual civic responsibility and has a fundamental influence on the character of relations between soldiers, significantly influencing the atmosphere and internal regime in the army

[939] The first group of relations between the population and the army thus expresses the personal presence and active participation of citizens in ensuring the military defense of the state

[940] They are guided by the credo: a soldier is a citizen in uniform

[941] Citizens perceive the army as active observers who watch how the army fulfills its mission, which was determined by elected legislators

[942] They obtain the necessary information about the army through the media, from submissions by fellow citizens, and directly from their personal experience

[943] They assess the need, functionality, versatility, and a number of other attributes that characterize the army as a whole

[944] Thus, another group of relations between the population and the army arises, which expresses the degree of its acceptance by citizens and its integration into democratic society

[945] By having access to information about the army and being able to express it publicly along with their opinions, citizens exercise civil control over the army

[946] This informal control, even if it only has informative outputs, is thus included in the overall system of civil control over the army

[947] In a democratic society, the army cannot do without the support of citizens

[948] Its human potential and the current state power constituted through them determine its mission and the conditions of its existence

[949] Individual subjects of society can support the position of the army in society

[950] The army must convince the population that it has all the necessary attributes, but it must do so by its own behavior and actions
