[326] Maintaining a constructive public debate is increasingly difficult as algorithm-driven media platforms become more prevalent

[327] We will support editor-controlled media and seek to enhance the ability of people, especially children and young people, to evaluate the credibility of sources

[328] Norway will work, in close cooperation with the EU, to ensure appropriate regulation of technology companies

[329] Legislation must be updated in line with new technological advances

[330] Efforts to counter disinformation and hostile influence activities are also important in terms of ensuring high voter turnout and safe political elections

[331] The relevant authorities must be prepared to deal with such threats and must ensure that there is effective coordination and adequate capacity to do so

[332] Promoting solutions to global challenges Unresolved global challenges pose a direct threat to Norwegian interests, and could exacerbate the impacts of other threats

[333] We will maintain our active engagement at the global level in areas of greatest significance to our interests

[334] Norway will continue to be a principled defender of democracy, the rule of law, human rights and international law

[335] These values are fundamental to building well-functioning societies and reducing the risk of war and conflict

[336] Climate change is affecting all countries and communities and is a threat multiplier

[337] This has implications for national security; it could for example lead to the emergence of new conflicts and increased migration

[338] Norway will promote climate change mitigation and adaptation, biodiversity conservation, food security and the green transition at both the national and the international level

[339] The COVID-19 pandemic uncovered a number of fundamental weaknesses in Norway’s health preparedness

[340] Norway is not equipped to deal with these challenges alone

[341] We are therefore seeking to cooperate more closely with the EU in this area

[342] Norway will continue to invest in global health cooperation

[343] The World Health Organization (WHO) will remain the most important platform for dealing with new and existing health threats, including antimicrobial resistance, pandemics and the impacts of climate change on human health

[344] National Security Strategy

[345] We will strengthen our economic security Ensuring that the Norwegian economy remains strong A strong economy and an adaptable business sector are crucial to Norwegian security

[346] Ensuring continued growth and prosperity is essential in order to be able to finance initiatives relating to security, defence and emergency preparedness

[347] In order to navigate the new situation in the global economy successfully and increase our competitiveness, we will, among other things, further develop our ability to adapt, based on research and innovation

[348] Preserving an open and rules-based system for trade and the free movement of capital Norway is dependent on international trade to gain access to the goods and services that we need, and to maintain an innovative and competitive business sector

[349] Rivalry between the major powers and trade wars are encouraging the formation of blocs and creating new trade barriers that affect Norway

[350] We will seek to preserve and promote the conditions that have served the Norwegian economy so well, including an open, predictable and rules-based system for international trade and the free movement of capital

[351] Safeguarding the EEA Agreement The EEA Agreement is of crucial importance to the Norwegian economy

[352] We will contribute to a well-functioning internal market by implementing the EEA Agreement

[353] We will further develop our cooperation with the EU and align our efforts closely with the EU’s economic security agenda

[354] Securing Norwegian energy supplies to Europe Secure and reliable gas deliveries are our most important contribution to economic security in Europe

[355] The wide-ranging energy cooperation between Norway and Europe serves to strengthen our competitiveness and facilitate the climate and energy transition

[356] We will maintain our position as a stable, long-term supplier of energy to Europe by taking steps to facilitate new profitable discoveries

[357] The critical infrastructure needed to maintain stable energy supplies must be secure and must be able to be rapidly restored if necessary

[358] Priority 26 National Security Strategy Reducing vulnerabilities in our economic cooperation with other countries Our economy must remain open, but we must make it more resilient to external pressures and disruptions

[359] We will identify and map dependencies and safeguard value chains of vital significance to our national security and preparedness

[360] We will carry out broader, more predictable controls of foreign investments

[361] Actors involved in the delivery of critical services or in vulnerable value chains must exercise due diligence with regard to investments from countries with which Norway has no established security cooperation

[362] This also applies to actors in the supply chain

[363] and private actors in the maritime, energy, telecommunications, defence, health and finance sectors and companies that supply products and services of vital importance to Norway’s total defence capability have a special responsibility in this context

[364] Steps are being taken to improve the effectiveness of the export control system in order to prevent sensitive security technology and information from being shared through trade relations with actors that do not share our security interests

[365] There is a need for a better overview of import dependencies in industries that are particularly critical for Norwegian security and value creation

[366] This overview is to be drawn up in cooperation with the business sector

[367] Norway is working with the EU and its Allies to improve security of supply

[368] We will facilitate Norwegian manufacturing of products that are part of key strategic value chains, both for us and our Allies

[369] Norway will work to further develop this cooperation, with a focus on strengthening industrial and business policy ties with the other Nordic countries, the EU, the UK and the US

[370] Identifying and mitigating risks in our relations with China China has a key position in the global economy and is Norway’s most important trading partner in Asia

[371] The country accounts for an increasing proportion of global knowledge production

[372] It is in Norway’s interest to seek China’s engagement in trade and efforts to find solutions to global issues, and we will work to avoid an unintentional decoupling in relations with China

[373] At the same time, China poses an increasing security threat to Norway and our Allies

[374] For reasons of national security, we will increase our vigilance in our dealings with China and Chinese actors

[375] Efforts to identify and reduce risks in our economic cooperation must be strengthened and carried out more systematically

[376] This can best be achieved through close cooperation with our Nordic neighbours, like-minded European countries and Allies

[377] Norwegian authorities will seek to raise awareness of risks, working in close dialogue with the business sector, the municipalities and the knowledge sector

[378] In certain sensitive areas, cooperation is to be avoided

[379] This applies in particular to Chinese involvement in areas designated as fundamental national functions and critical assets, where this could create significant ties

[380] Ensuring access to strategically important knowledge and expertise International cooperation in the area of research and higher education is essential to maintaining Norway’s competitiveness and our ability to find solutions to new challenges

[381] Responsible international knowledge cooperation entails weighing the benefits of open research against security considerations at all levels of the knowledge sector

[382] We will improve our ability to prevent unwanted knowledge transfer and hostile influence activities, as well as the misuse of knowledge and technology that can undermine core social values

[383] Investing in the application and development of new technology Norway will be at the forefront when it comes to understanding and applying new technology

[384] This is vital for the Norwegian economy and for Norwegian security

[385] We will educate our population and provide people with the skills they need to become technologically proficient

[386] We will increase investment in the development and application of new technologies, such as artificial intelligence and quantum technology

[387] Participation in international research and technology cooperation will be crucial in this context

[388] We must improve our ability to make use of new technology for defence purposes, particularly in areas where we have comparative advantages

[389] Norway will seek to be at the technological forefront in areas such as ground-based air defence, missile, space and sensor technology, as well as ammunition and military explosives

[390] We will continue to develop underwater technology that strengthens our security and enables us to maintain our leading position in the maritime sector

[391] The business sector plays a key role in delivering critical functions that are vital to Norwegian and Allied security

[392] In today’s more serious security situation, it is crucial to maintain these functions, and ensure adequate national capacity, controls and expertise in strategically important areas

[393] We will take steps to ensure that there are Norwegian-owned companies in strategically important sectors such as telecommunications, banking and finance, and space technology

[394] Securing access to industrial production capacity and critical raw materials is vital for Norway and Europe as a whole

[395] Norwegian industrial clusters and expert communities have a crucial role in ensuring Norwegian and Allied security

[396] Norway will focus on areas where it has comparative advantages

[397] We will maintain a strong industrial base so that we can help to meet national and Allied needs, in areas such as oil and gas, the processing industry, the maritime sector and the defence industry

[398] Norway will never be fully self-sufficient

[399] We must therefore work to enter into binding partnerships that provide us with what we need

[400] This applies to areas such as vaccines and pharmaceuticals, semiconductors and electronic components and production inputs and imports of goods that help to meet the population’s basic needs

[401] Ensuring sound management of our investments in a more uncertain world. The Government Pension Fund Global plays a vital role in preserving Norway’s economic stability and room for manoeuvre

[402] The new security situation has made management of the fund more challenging, and effective risk management has become all the more important

[403] Norway will work to maintain good, stable framework conditions for international investment activities

[404] Norway will provide clear information about the role of the Government Pension Fund Global in order to reinforce its financial purpose and legitimacy

[405] The fund is not an instrument of foreign and security policy

[406] National Security Strategy Responsibilities and revision This strategy provides a comprehensive, authoritative framework to guide efforts to ensure Norway’s security

[407] The Government has overall responsibility for ensuring that the strategy is followed up, across the central government administration, vis-à-vis other relevant stakeholders in Norwegian society and at the international level

[408] This strategy is to underpin efforts relating to national security in the various government ministries, and is to be used as a basis for establishing overall priorities, identifying concrete measures and developing policy

[409] Implementation of the strategy will require a whole-of-society approach

[410] More time and resources need to be devoted to security across all sectors and at all levels of society

[411] For this reason, the strategy places higher expectations on a range of actors in our society, in particular the business sector and the public authorities

[412] Everyone living in Norway also has a clear responsibility to contribute to this national effort

[413] We must all do more to keep ourselves, and Norway, safe

[414] This strategy is planned to be revised in two years’ time
