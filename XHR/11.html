[43] The Cold War created a bipolar system, led by the two superpowers of that period in history

[46] The collapse of the Cold War order led to a substantial change in the prevailing rules of the game; a significant shift in the structure of relations; the emergence of new phenomena; and the intensification or acceleration of pre-existing ones

[47] In short, the end of the Cold War established a new international agenda, now with a wide range of content and affected by unprecedented variables and nuances

[48] Globalization implies, first and foremost, an intensified and complex interdependence between national economies, with a high degree of interpenetration in all economic activities

[49] This process was already underway during the Cold War, but its evolution was limited by the constraints of the bipolar struggle

[50] Since 1989, with the triumph of one of the worldviews—characterized by democracy and the free market—this trend has become increasingly pronounced, permeating all spheres of interaction between societies, whether social, cultural, scientific, communication, public opinion, etc

[51] Globalization thus transcends the merely economic, creating a multidimensional planetary phenomenon whose complexity requires an effort to synthesize its salient features

[52] Globalization undoubtedly demands greater responses from societies in terms of solidarity and equity, but in this work we consider it not as an ideology that can be chosen, whether for or against, but rather as a fact of the new reality, which represents the central fact of the times and demands of political action—in the pursuit of general well-being—a basic effort at understanding and the consideration of viable alternatives

[53] Having thus posed the two salient expressions of change in the international system - the end of the Cold War and globalization - we can then proceed to break down the other phenomena and processes that are influencing the development of the new international system, which are, depending on the case, a direct or indirect consequence of one or other of the factors described above, or of the interrelationship between the two

[54] This demonstrates, as already noted, the highly complex nature of this intensification of all interstate ties across borders, which, horizontally, also involve state agencies, an aspect that military organizations are no exception to

[57] Furthermore, population growth generates greater demand for food and basic needs, so both situations ultimately act as factors of pressure on natural resources

[59] This aspect of the phenomenon tends to isolate countries or areas of the world that are not absorbed into the globalization process, or that are not integrated for social, ethnic, religious, or cultural reasons, which, in turn, contributes in these cases to the exacerbation of nationalism

[81] Another characteristic is that such antagonisms not only occur between states - interstate - but also increasingly occur within states - intrastate - in many cases in weak states, with fragile or unconsolidated systems of government, of dubious viability, or with impoverished populations below the subsistence level

[90] We now observe a situation of diffuse general power, which is exercised by multiple actors in diverse fields and supported by diverse attributes

MILITARY JUSTICE It is necessary to initially distinguish, with respect to military jurisdiction, two areas: on the one hand, the so-called military disciplinary jurisdiction and, on the other, the so-called military criminal jurisdiction

[1303] The first, which the Code of Military Justice refers to as "executive authority," is a function inherent to command and is therefore overseen by the entire hierarchical structure, beginning with the highest authority, the President of the Nation in his capacity as Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces

[1304] Such exercise entails the sanctioning of disciplinary offenses committed by military personnel, consisting of all those violations of military duties, which the law or regulations punish with the disciplinary sanctions provided for in said Code

[1305] Military criminal jurisdiction, unlike the previous one, refers to crimes and, consequently, to the imposition of penalties that, according to the relevant article of the CMJ, can only be carried out through the intervention of a Court Martial

[1306] This is not a jurisdiction derived from the command assigned to the President of the Nation over the Armed Forces, but rather from the legislative power to dictate the rules of organization and government of the Armed Forces, which arises from the National Constitution

[1307] Military jurisdiction ultimately provides the legal basis for the disciplinary criteria that constitute, as we have seen, one of the most salient characteristics of any military organization

[1308] The Code establishes a threefold conceptual unity: its provisions are common to all three Armed Forces; it contains the rules for times of peace and war; and it brings together in a single body everything related to the organization of military tribunals, their procedures, and penalties

[1309] Reforms to the Military Justice Code Until the amendment made to the CJM by Law No. 23,039, the military jurisdiction exercised by military courts was external to the Judicial Branch of the Nation

[1310] Their rulings became final once they became final, with no possibility of judicial review other than through an extraordinary appeal to the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation

[1311] That jurisdiction basically covered essentially military crimes and misdemeanors, and also all those that, affecting the rights and interests of the State or of people in general, were committed by military personnel in acts of military service or in places subject exclusively to military authority

[1312] Since the 1984 reform of the CJM, which incorporated a new criterion adopted by the Supreme Court, judicial review of the rulings of military courts has become mandatory

[1313] It is implemented through an appeal before the National Court of Criminal Cassation, with mandatory intervention by the public prosecutor in all cases, an essential requirement for the sentence to become final

[1314] Furthermore, these amendments to the CJM limited military criminal jurisdiction in peacetime solely to essentially military crimes and misdemeanors, that is, to infractions that affect the existence of the military institution and that are exclusively provided for and sanctioned by said Code

[1315] All the rest are the responsibility of the civil courts

[1316] Mandatory review and the limitation of military jurisdiction are not applicable in times of war

[1317] Continuing along that conceptual political line, and given the need to further adapt military justice regulations to the changing circumstances of the country and the times, the Ministry of Defense, in conjunction with the General Audit Office of the Armed Forces, has been preparing a revision of the regulations of the Military Justice System since

[1318] The most significant reforms included in this proposal are: The sanctioning regime for violations of the International Law of Armed Conflict is incorporated

[1319] The scope of due obedience is specified, which, in summary, cannot be invoked when the superior's order clearly implies the commission of a crime

[1320] In cases of crimes against protected persons and property in situations of armed conflict, liability extends to superiors for crimes committed by their subordinates, under certain circumstances

[1321] Military jurisdiction is limited to hearing actions committed by military personnel whose classification is formulated in the CMJ, regardless of whether they are included in other criminal norms

[1322] Furthermore, it extends to the prosecution of all crimes that military personnel may commit during a military peacekeeping operation abroad

[1323] Legal experts are incorporated into the Courts-Martial

[1324] It is stipulated that prosecutors will come from military legal bodies

[1325] The voluntary appointment of a defense attorney - who must be a lawyer - is made possible from the moment the defendant becomes a defendant, and is mandatory at the time the statement is received

[1326] The institute of conditional release is incorporated

[1327] The sanction and promulgation of these reforms as national law will significantly modernize the military justice system. This policy will best reflect the changes that social and legal evolution has brought about in the national and international context. It broadly reflects the experiences gathered since 1984 and thus contributes to strengthening republican principles and those established by human rights

[1394] MILITARY EDUCATION AND HUMAN RIGHTS A better understanding of this topic requires some prior conceptual clarifications

[1395] Human Rights and International Humanitarian Law

[1396] While International Human Rights Law (HRL) is comprised of a set of principles and standards designed to respect and guarantee human rights, International Humanitarian Law (IHL) encompasses the standards contained in treaties, as well as customary ones, that regulate the proper conduct of participants in armed conflicts and the protection of persons who may be affected by the conflict

[1397] In other words, in the first case, the applicable circumstance is determined by the individual's status as such, as a holder of rights and as a subject of that field of international law; in the other, by their involvement in a war situation, in which the subjects are the States and the International Committee of the Red Cross, while individuals are reserved the role of protected persons

[1398] Both sets of regulations are compatible through their convergence

[1399] In the area of human rights, a core of non-derogable standards is established that must remain in force in any situation within the State, including conflicts, whether international, internal, unrest, or social tensions

[1400] IHL, as an exceptional law, inherent to emergencies, embraces this "hard core" of human rights in norms that demonstrate their similarity and correspondence with it, demonstrating the concurrent nature of both

[1401] Consequently, whenever the Armed Forces find themselves involved in conflict situations—as is common given their nature—they must adhere strictly to the standards contained in International Humanitarian Law, which, in turn, will require compliance with the non-derogable precepts of Human Rights

[1402] Compliance with the law and acting according to its rules legitimizes the actions of the military forces, enhances their participation, ensures international recognition, and is also a source of renewed confidence on the part of society
