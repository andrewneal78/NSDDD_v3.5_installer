[3] Saeima statement On the National Security Concept I hereby announce that the Saeima, at its meeting on 24 January of this year, has approved the National Security Concept prepared by the Cabinet of Ministers

[4] In Riga on 24 January 2002, the Speaker of the Saeima J. Straume National Security Concept Introduction The Saeima of the Republic of Latvia, by adopting the National Security Law, has determined the basic tasks of the National Security Concept - to determine the strategic basic principles, priorities and measures for preventing threats to the state

[5] The Concept describes the situation of the Republic of Latvia from a security perspective, as well as evaluates the factors that influence the security situation in the country

[6] The Concept is based on the understanding that national security is influenced not only by the foreign and military-political situation, but that internal factors also play a significant role in ensuring security - the democratic foundations of the state, economic development, social security and welfare, the crime situation, environmental quality, etc

[7] The Concept sets out guidelines for the activities of state institutions, supporting the state's common security policy and developing individual sectoral plans and programmes

[8] Based on the strategy and principles set out in the National Security Concept, a National Security Plan is being developed, which includes specific measures and means for neutralising and preventing threats to the state

[9] The analytical basis of this document is an analysis of the threat to the state

[10] National security

[11] National interests of the Republic of Latvia The national security of Latvia is the ability of the state and its society to protect and ensure national interests and fundamental values

[12] They include the existence of the state independence, territorial indivisibility and democratic system of the Republic of Latvia as defined in the Constitution, as well as ensuring the internal security of the state, guaranteeing respect for human rights, safety and security of the population

[13] National interests also include ensuring the prerequisites necessary for the long-term development of the state and society: ensuring economic growth and the well-being of the population, preserving the language and cultural identity, maintaining the defense system, preserving and developing scientific and technical potential, ensuring sustainable development of the environment, ensuring and developing state infrastructure and telecommunications (including information technologies), maintaining internal political stability, based on a general understanding of the democratic development of the Republic of Latvia, and the development of a consolidated civil society, based on the principle of equality of all people

[14] Latvia's opportunities to ensure the realization of national interests also depend on such external conditions as the general climate of international relations and cooperation in the world and the region, the international economic situation, and the quality of the global ecological environment

[15] National Security Policy, Its Principles and Implementation

[16] The national security policy of Latvia is an integral part of the state policy

[17] It must guarantee the existence of the Latvian state and ensure the national interests of Latvia with the forces, means and methods provided for in the Constitution and legal acts

[18] When implementing its security policy, Latvia observes the principles of international law contained in the Charter of the United Nations (hereinafter - UN), the documents of the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (hereinafter - OSCE), as well as in international treaties binding on Latvia

[19] The security policy of Latvia is developed without threatening any other country

[20] The security of Latvia can be achieved by ensuring a stable domestic political, social and economic situation, developing effective defence force structures, establishing crisis management and civil protection systems, creating a legal state system and structures, observing international obligations and norms, as well as by integrating into European and transatlantic political, economic, security and defence structures

[21] The national security policy and the achievement of its goals are the responsibility of all state institutions and the country's residents

[22] Threat to national security Threat to the national security of Latvia are actions directed against the national interests and fundamental values of Latvia, as well as situations created by ecological, technical and other factors that adversely affect the possibilities of realizing national interests

[23] Threats can manifest themselves both separately in the political, military, economic, informational, social, ecological, criminal, etc. areas, and can also be closely related, interacting and accumulating

[24] Risks and threats to the national interests of Latvia arise both from the international environment and from the development processes of the Latvian state and society

[25] Description of the security environment and risks The National Security Concept, when describing the current security situation in the world and in Latvia, examines the main factors that have a direct impact on national security and which at the same time also characterize the current state of the country - the situation in the world and in the region, the development of national democracy and the institutional systems that ensure it, the security of society and its individuals, the development of the market economy, the integration of society, the social situation and the self-defense capabilities of the country

[26] International environment Over the past decade, significant changes have occurred in the global security system, which have also affected Latvia

[27] With the disappearance of the bipolar system of the Cold War, firmly fixed positions and easily modeled situations, the time of declarative plans and conceptual documents has also passed

[28] The situation in the world, in Europe and in the Baltic Sea region is developing very dynamically

[29] The likelihood of a global war has decreased, however, the probability of a fully extensive armed conflict cannot be ruled out

[30] It is not foreseeable that Latvia could be subjected to direct military aggression in the near future

[31] However, as the likelihood of a global war decreases, the likelihood of regional or local crises and conflicts has increased

[32] The reasons for their occurrence may be ethnic and religious conflicts, border conflicts, economic and welfare decline of countries or regions, weakening of internal administrative capacities of countries, as well as the development of undemocratic processes

[33] In the case of local or regional crises and conflicts, the likelihood of uncontrolled migration increases, which becomes especially dangerous if it causes a humanitarian crisis

[34] Currently, Latvia is not a destination for migration flows, but the use of Latvia as a transit country for illegal (especially economically motivated) migration is increasing

[35] The spread of weapons of mass destruction has become one of the significant security risks in the world, which may threaten Latvia's cooperation partners or Latvia itself

[36] Despite the existing system of arms control treaties, the task of which is to limit the spread of weapons of mass destruction or their means of delivery, they are nevertheless coming into the possession of an increasing number of states and even non-state entities, thus creating a risk to international security and stability

[37] The spread of international terrorism, which can also manifest itself as political terrorism, is assessed as a risk to international security and stability

[38] Negative features in this area have been observed in recent years - the danger of terrorist groups is increased by the ongoing processes of globalization, as a result of which any country in the world can become a victim of terrorism

[39] Carefully planned, technically secured and with possible access to chemical, biological and nuclear weapons, terrorist activities pose new threats to the world

[40] International terrorism is characterized by the creation of a global network and the decentralization of the leadership of terrorist organizations, which makes it difficult for states to completely prevent terrorist activities

[41] The international security environment in Europe is significantly influenced by the processes of European integration, which manifest themselves in connection with the deepening of the integration of the European Union (hereinafter - the EU) and the enlargement of the EU, as well as with the enlargement of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (hereinafter - NATO) and its new tasks in the field of security

[42] The majority of European countries have engaged in transatlantic cooperation based on common democratic values

[43] An overwhelming majority of European countries consider the permanent and close involvement of the United States of America (hereinafter - the USA) in European security to be a necessary element for the stability of the continent

[44] European countries are also assuming greater responsibility in the field of security, creating a common security and defence policy within the EU, which will provide greater opportunities for international crisis management

[45] In the field of collective defence, NATO and the USA will still retain a decisive role

[46] The enlargement of NATO and the EU is a stabilising factor in the security systems, as a result of the influence of which the region is expanded, in which stable and democratic political and economic development is guaranteed

[48] Latvia's national security is inseparable from the European continent and transatlantic security

[49] Latvia's membership in the EU and NATO will strengthen its security and allow it to fully integrate into the European and transatlantic security system

[50] These development trends and Latvia's prospects for joining the EU and NATO have a positive impact on Latvia's security situation

[51] A complementary element of global and continental security processes is cooperation within the region

[53] Active support of the Nordic countries - Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland - for the long-term democratic development of the Baltic States has transformed the Baltic Sea region into a unifying and cooperation-promoting environment

[56] The environment for the development of the security process is also influenced by the bilateral relations of the countries

[57] Therefore, the further development of the Eastern neighbours - Russia and Belarus - is of particular importance to Latvia

[58] The democratisation process of these two countries and the predictability of their foreign policies will greatly influence the further development of the region

[59] State and society

[61] A stable institutional system has been established in Latvia that ensures the rule of law, respect for human rights and the protection of minorities

[70] This means both the approximation of legal norms and the compatibility of the EU legal system and the Latvian national legal system (also in the field of law enforcement and legal culture

[72] A corruption prevention program has been developed and is being implemented in Latvia, as corruption can threaten the country's economic, social and legal development and reduce public loyalty to the state

[98] Economy, regional development and environment The level of economic development of a country, as well as economic and financial stability, has a significant impact on national security

[99] Latvia, as a small country with an open market economy, is significantly exposed to international influence and fluctuations, therefore the stability and predictability of the countries of the region and cooperation partner countries is particularly important

[139] National defense Despite the fact that the likelihood of a military threat is assessed as relatively low, increasing the state's self-defense capabilities is the main priority of security policy

[155] Civil defense is a component of the state's total defense system, and it is based on a set of organizational, economic, educational, etc. measures that ensure the protection of the population, the national economy, the environment and the national cultural heritage from the dangers of possible emergency situations and their consequences both in the territory of the country and in the immediate vicinity of its borders
