[3] May 2001 Executive Summary The government is building a modern, professional and well-equipped Defence Force with the necessary military capabilities across all three services to meet New Zealand's objectives

[4] The government has set clear goals and priorities and made a careful and thorough assessment of New Zealand's defence and security needs

[5] The New Zealand Defence Force is being reconfigured so that it is sustainable and affordable over the long-term

[93] Sealift There are two aspects of sealift, strategic and tactical

[94] Strategic sealift involves moving heavy and large equipment from New Zealand to an assembly area, normally a fixed port, where the equipment is married up with personnel deployed by air

[95] Tactical sealift requires a capability to deliver both personnel and equipment directly into the operational area without requiring access to a port facility

[96] Both requirements were demonstrated in the East Timor deployment

[97] Strategic sealift was provided by a chartered commercial ship from New Zealand to the assembly area in Darwin

[98] From Darwin, the equipment was deployed into the Suai operational area by a French military tactical sealift ship which had the ability to offload the armoured carriers and heavy trucks over the beach

[99] Requirement for sealift is sporadic – the deployments to Bosnia and East Timor were the only occasions where strategic sealift was required, while a tactical sealift ship could have been used for the operations in Bougainville and Solomon Islands

[100] For the majority of time the ship would not be needed for sealift duties

[101] Generally, the slow speed and poor manoeuvrability of sealift ships limit their usefulness for other maritime tasks

[102] Their hull design usually means such ships are unsuitable for safe employment in the Southern Ocean or Ross Sea

[103] Given the infrequent need for sealift, the ability to charter commercial ships when required, and the lack of versatility for other tasks, the acquisition of a dedicated sealift ship is not considered a wise investment

[104] A better investment would be a multi-role ship which can undertake a number of roles in our region, including a limited tactical sealift capacity for such operations as disaster relief in the South Pacific
